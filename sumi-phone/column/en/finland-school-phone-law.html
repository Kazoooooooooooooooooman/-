From August 2025, Finnish law bans phones in class, with a few exceptions

November 12, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

Since August 1, 2025, Finnish law has banned pupils in basic education, the Finnish equivalent of elementary and junior high school, from using phones and other mobile devices during lessons, as a rule. They may use them only in limited cases, such as when a teacher allows it for learning or when there is a health reason. This summary is based on the text of the law in Finlex (Finland's official legislation database), the government's bill, and materials from the Finnish National Agency for Education.

The short version

  • They can be used when a teacher allows it for learning, for health reasons, or as an assistive aid, for example because of a disability
  • Each school sets its own rules for breaks and lunch. The National Agency for Education recommends limiting devices during breaks too and banning them at lunch

What does the law say?

The change is in Section 29 of Finland's Basic Education Act (Perusopetuslaki). The amending act (245/2025), issued on May 23, 2025, added the following:

  • Pupils may not use a phone or other mobile device during lessons
  • The exceptions are use for learning with a teacher's permission, use for the pupil's own health care with the principal's or a teacher's permission, and use as an assistive aid
  • School rules (järjestyssäännöt) must say where devices are kept during lessons, and how they may be used and where they are kept at other times of the school day, such as breaks

According to the National Agency for Education (Opetushallitus), "mobile devices" include phones, tablets, laptops, headphones, watch phones for children and smartwatches.

When can pupils still use them?

SituationAllowed?
During lessons (normally)No
During lessons, with a teacher's permission for learningYes
Health reasons (e.g., a pupil with diabetes checking blood sugar)Yes, with the principal's or a teacher's permission
As an assistive aid (e.g., communication for a pupil with a disability)Yes
Breaks, moving between classes, lunchSet by school rules (a school may ban use for the whole day)
Feeling unwell and wanting to contact a parentYes, with a teacher's permission (every school must allow this)
Emergency callsAlways allowed, no permission needed

Schools could already ban phones in lessons through their own rules. The amendment makes the rule the same in every school. According to the National Agency for Education, schools can now also ban device use for the whole school day, and can require pupils to keep their phones in lockers or a "phone parking" spot. In return, essential use must be allowed throughout the day.

A principal or teacher may take a device that is being used to disrupt teaching (Section 36 d of the Basic Education Act). The device is returned after the lesson. If the disruption seems likely to continue after the lesson, it must still be returned by the end of the school day at the latest.

Why Finland changed the law

The National Agency for Education lists six goals for the restrictions:

  • Protect a calm working atmosphere in class
  • Support learning and concentration on learning
  • Reduce disruptive use
  • Promote a physically active lifestyle
  • Build emotional and social skills
  • Encourage good mealtime habits

The government's bill cites the OECD's PISA 2022 results. In math lessons, 41% of Finnish pupils said that using digital devices distracted them in every lesson or most lessons, clearly more than the OECD average of 31%.

However, the bill also notes, from the same PISA results, that pupils who did not use digital devices for learning at school at all did considerably worse in math than those who used them to some extent, up to two hours a day. The law does not end the use of devices in teaching. It aims to cut use that has nothing to do with the lesson and gets in the way. The National Agency for Education also stresses that developing pupils' digital skills is an important task for schools.

Many schools already had limits before the law

In a fall 2024 survey by the Ministry of Education and Culture (preliminary results), 85.2% of the municipalities and other bodies that run basic education said that all their schools limited phones through school rules for grades 1–6. For grades 7–9, the figure was 87.6%. The rest said they limited phones in some schools or were planning to.

In a survey of pupils carried out for the bill (883 respondents), many said it was good not to use phones during lessons so they could concentrate, while others wanted to use phones during breaks to keep in touch with friends and family. The law covers lessons, and each school decides the rules for breaks.

Upper secondary schools are not covered, and the agency recommends going further

  • The law does not apply to general upper secondary schools (lukio) or vocational schools. Their rules can ban disruptive use during teaching, but they cannot ban phones completely during breaks
  • The National Agency for Education recommends that comprehensive schools also limit or ban device use during breaks and when moving between classes, and ban it completely at lunch
  • The National Agency for Education and the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare (THL) are also preparing screen time recommendations for children and young people's free time outside school

School phone rules are spreading in other countries too. We cover the Dutch example in the Netherlands' classroom phone ban, and the global picture in school phone bans now cover 58% of the world's countries.

FAQ

Are phones completely banned in Finnish schools?

No. The law bans their use during lessons in basic education (grades 1–9). Pupils can use them when a teacher allows it for learning, for health reasons, and in similar cases. Each school sets its own rules for breaks, and can ban use for the whole school day. Emergency calls are always allowed.

Will a phone be taken away if a pupil uses it?

A principal or teacher can take a device that is being used to disrupt teaching. It must be returned after the lesson, or by the end of the school day at the latest.

Summary

  • Since August 1, 2025, Finnish law has banned the use of phones and similar devices during lessons in basic education, as a rule
  • Use for learning, for health, as an assistive aid, and emergency calls are allowed
  • The aims are a calm class and better concentration, plus social skills and a more active lifestyle

Outside school, too, you can decide for yourself to put your phone away at mealtimes or when you are with friends.
