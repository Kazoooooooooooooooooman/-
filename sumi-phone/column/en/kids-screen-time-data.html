How much screen time do elementary and middle school students have? The share with 3+ hours on weekdays, and the trend

October 30, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

On weekdays, 50.0% of boys and 49.3% of girls in the second year of middle school (age 13–14) spend 3 hours or more looking at TV, phone or game console screens outside of study. That's nearly one in two. Even among fifth graders, the figures are 42.5% for boys and 37.2% for girls, and both have risen since fiscal 2019. This summary is based on a national survey by the Japan Sports Agency.

The short version

  • 3 hours or more of weekday "screen time outside of study": second-year middle school boys 50.0%, girls 49.3%; fifth-grade boys 42.5%, girls 37.2%
  • Some children are at 5 hours or more too: 17.2% of second-year middle school boys and 16.1% of girls
  • Compared with fiscal 2019, fifth-grade girls rose from 28.6% to 37.2%, and second-year middle school boys from 36.7% to 50.0%

Share at 3 hours or more (fiscal 2025)

In this survey, screen time means the time per weekday spent looking at screens such as TVs, phones and game consoles, not including time for study. It covers fifth graders in elementary school and second-year middle school students across Japan. The share at 3 hours or more was calculated by adding together the "5 hours or more," "4 to under 5 hours" and "3 to under 4 hours" categories in the survey's chart.

One in six middle schoolers spends 5 hours or more on screens on weekdays outside of study. For a child who gets home at 4 p.m. and goes to bed at 11 p.m., that's more than 70% of their time at home.

The Japan Sports Agency says that although fiscal 2025 was slightly lower than the year before, the upward trend that began after the COVID-19 pandemic is continuing. The survey was not carried out in fiscal 2020.

Links with fitness and sleep

In the same survey, the share of children who exercise "less than 60 minutes" a week continues to rise. On the other hand, the share of children who sleep "8 hours or more" is increasing. The sleep figures are covered in sleep time for elementary and middle school students.

What families can do

  • Measure the weekday total once: Look at the weekly average together using Screen Time on iPhone or Digital Wellbeing on Android. Don't forget to add time on the TV and game consoles
  • Decide first what the freed-up time is for: Rather than "Stop that," it lasts longer if you first plan something else, like playing outside, an after-school activity or a family outing
  • Keep screens out of the bedroom: Screens at night cut directly into sleep time

Phone use time by grade is also covered in phone time for middle schoolers and phone time for elementary schoolers.

Summary

  • About half of second-year middle schoolers and about 40% of fifth graders spend 3 hours or more on screens on weekdays outside of study
  • One in six middle schoolers is at 5 hours or more
  • The share has risen since fiscal 2019 for every grade and gender
