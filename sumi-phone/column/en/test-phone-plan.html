A two-week phone lockdown plan before exams: 14 days for people who can't stop

October 23, 2026 (updated October 25, 2026) · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

You decide before an exam, "No phone from today," but within three days you're back to your old habits. The cause isn't weak willpower. It's not having a plan. A lockdown lasts if you limit it to two weeks, set up the system on the first day, and decide in advance when you'll ease off. This article lays out how to pace things from 14 days before the exam to exam day, in a form you can use as is.

The short version

  • Set the start and end of the lockdown by date: "from 14 days before the exam until the last subject is over"
  • There are four things to do on day one: decide what to lock away, tell your friends, hand the restrictions to someone else, and pick a fixed spot for your phone
  • Don't delete LINE (Japan's most-used messaging app). Turn off its notifications and "check it twice a day, for five minutes only." Tell people to use a call or SMS for anything urgent
  • Schedule breaks at a set time after each evening's study. A plan made only of holding back won't last
  • On days you slip, don't try to make up for it. Add one more piece to the system the next day

Why two weeks?

If you decide to "hold out until the exams are over," you give in sooner because you can't see the end. When you set the start and end by date, you can count "how many days left" each day, which makes it easier to keep going.

Two weeks is just the right length to go through all the material on the exam once and then review it. Any shorter and you won't be ready in time; any longer and your focus slips partway through.

This article is about the two-week "plan." Ways to stop your hand from reaching for your phone while you're actually at your desk (where to put it, notifications, working in 25-minute blocks) are covered in how to stop picking up your phone while studying. Use them together.

Four things to set up 14 days out (day one)

Whether the lockdown works is mostly decided on day one. From day two on, you want to be in a position where you just ride the system you built on the first day.

1. Decide what to lock away

If you make your whole phone unusable, you'll run into trouble with school and family messages and end up lifting the ban right away. Write down on paper, in advance, what you'll stop and what you'll keep.

StopKeep
Social media, videos, games, killing time online, aggregator sitesCalls, SMS, family messages, LINE at set times, alarms

The hard calls are study apps and explainer videos. If opening one tends to lead you off to social media or other videos, switch to paper flashcards, textbooks and workbooks for just these two weeks. If you really need an explainer video, watch it on the computer at home and close it when you're done.

2. Tell your friends

If your replies suddenly slow down, people will wonder why. If you let them know, nobody minds when your replies are late. Send a message to your groups and close friends on day one.

Example message

"Starting today, I'm locking away my phone until my exams on [date] are over. I'll check LINE once in the evening. For anything urgent, please call or text. I'll be back to normal once exams are done."

It's important to include the end date. It tells people "I'm not disappearing for good," so they won't worry. Inviting a friend taking the same exams to start at the same time with you works well too.

3. Hand the restrictions to someone other than yourself

Log out of social media and video apps, or delete them. Then use your phone's built-in screen time limits and have a family member set the passcode for turning them off. A passcode you set yourself is one you'll remove yourself when things get hard. Step-by-step settings for both iPhone and Android are in for people who keep switching off their own Screen Time limits.

If you're a university student living alone, or a working adult studying for a certification exam, make the passcode a long number, write it on paper, and either put it in an envelope and give it to someone, or leave it in a locker at work or university.

4. Pick a fixed spot for your phone

Make its spot for the two weeks somewhere you can't see from where you study and that's a hassle to go to. A living room shelf, the entryway, a family member's room and so on. Charge it there too. If you decide to put it there first thing when you get home, you won't have to think about it each day.

At night, don't bring it into the bedroom. Use an alarm clock instead of your phone to wake up. What's most likely to fall apart before an exam is the "just for a minute" before bed.

Don't delete LINE; set times to check it

Many students get class notices, changes to the exam material and assignment reminders through LINE. If you stop LINE too, worrying about missing something important can actually make you open it again and again. Keep LINE, and just change how you use it.

  • Turn off notifications: Turn LINE notifications off for these two weeks. If nothing pings, nothing pulls you away in the middle of studying
  • Check twice a day, for five minutes only: For example, "when I get home in the late afternoon" and "after evening study is done." Set a five-minute timer and reply only to what needs a reply
  • Check only LINE: Don't open other apps while you're at it. After five minutes, put the phone back in its spot
  • Urgent things by phone or SMS: As in the message you sent on day one, take urgent contact outside LINE
  • Ask someone to be your messenger: Ask one close friend, "If anything comes up about exams or assignments, let me know." It eases the worry about missing things

Instead of keeping exam material and due dates in your head from the LINE feed, copy them onto your paper plan on day one. That's one less reason to open LINE.

From 14 days out to exam day

Split the two weeks into five stages. The closer you get to the exam, the stricter you are with your phone.

13 to 8 days out: keep the routine intact

In the first week, put running the system every day ahead of how much you study. Phone in its spot, LINE twice, evening breaks up to 30 minutes. Once this becomes normal, the second half gets easier.

7 days out: review once along the way

On this day, right at the halfway point, spend just 15 minutes looking back. Did your studying go to plan? How many days did your phone win, and around what time and where did it happen? Once you know the times and places things fell apart, you'll know what to do in the second half.

6 to 3 days out: get stricter

In the final week before the exam, drop weekday breaks and keep them for the weekend only. Instead, when you pause between study blocks, take breaks that don't involve a screen. Take a short walk outside, stretch, have a snack. If you look at your phone during a break, you may not make it back.

The day before and exam day: add nothing

The day before, don't go looking for new workbooks or "likely questions" floating around on social media. Look only at your own notes and the problems you got wrong. The most important thing is sleep, so leave your phone outside the bedroom at night and go to bed early.

On the morning of the exam, don't open social media either. Other people's "I haven't studied at all" or "apparently this will come up" won't raise your score; they'll just unsettle you. If exams run over several days, keep this up until every subject is done.

Schedule rewards and breaks in advance

A plan made only of holding back will blow up at some point. To make it through the two weeks, build time to ease off into the plan from the start.

  • Put breaks after study: Make it "once the day's plan is done, I can use 30 minutes however I like." If you look before studying, 30 minutes won't be enough
  • Set the time with a timer: When 30 minutes are up, put your phone back in its spot. No extensions
  • Make a slightly longer slot on the weekend: Such as an hour on Saturday night. Save videos you want to watch and chats with friends for this. If you can think "I'll watch it at the weekend," you have fewer reasons to open your phone on weekdays
  • Rewards on the day it's over: On day one, write on your plan what you want to do the day exams finish. Going out to eat something you love, seeing a film you've wanted to see and so on. For middle and high school students, agreeing on a reward with your family works well too

Whether you use your phone during your nightly break is up to you. If you can't stop once you pick it up, making your break something other than your phone will make the two weeks easier to get through.

What to do on a day you break the rules

Over two weeks, there'll probably be a day when things fall apart. What matters is the next day.

  1. Write one line that same day: "Around what time, where, what set it off, and roughly how many minutes." Write it as a record, not as blame
  2. Don't try to make up for it: The biggest loss is thinking "today's a write-off, I'll start tomorrow" and using up the whole evening. As soon as you notice, put your phone back in its spot and get back to your plan for whatever time is left
  3. Add one piece to the system the next day: If it fell apart at night, hand your phone to your family at night. If notifications did it, delete that app. If boredom did it, make your study blocks shorter
  4. If it falls apart twice in three days, go one step stricter: If keeping it in its spot isn't enough, hand it to your family. If that still isn't enough, use a lockable box that won't open until the time is up

Blaming yourself for slipping won't raise tomorrow's score. What you fix isn't yourself but where the phone is and the system around it. If your phone keeps winning no matter what you try, you can also change how you have a phone at all (phone strategies for students preparing for entrance exams: cancel it, hand it over or switch to a flip phone).

What to change for middle and high school, university and certification exams

The shape of the plan is the same. What changes is who holds the restrictions and which contacts you keep open.

After the exams

Once the last subject is over, lift the ban on the day you decided. No extensions. Finishing by keeping your promise means you can start the same plan with confidence for the next exam.

Once the ban is lifted, look back on just one thing. Over the two weeks, what did you not miss? If having LINE notifications off caused no problems, you can leave them off after the exams too. Anything you didn't miss is something you can let go of for good.

Summary

  • Bound your phone lockdown by date: from 14 days out until the last subject is over
  • On day one, take care of four things: what to lock away, telling your friends, handing over the restrictions, and a fixed spot
  • Don't delete LINE. Turn off notifications and check it twice a day for five minutes. Urgent things by phone or SMS
  • Get stricter in the second half, and add nothing new on the day before and exam day
  • Schedule breaks and rewards in advance. On days you slip, don't make up for it; add one piece to the system
