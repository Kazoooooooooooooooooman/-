Fewer than half as many people camp as at the peak. But those who do keep going back

November 7, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

During the pandemic, camping got a lot of attention on TV and online. But did more people actually go camping? According to estimates by the Japan Auto Camping Association, the number of people who went auto camping at least once a year peaked at 15.8 million in 1996. It was 8.6 million in 2019, 6.1 million in pandemic-hit 2020, and 6.5 million in 2022. That is less than half the peak, but those who do camp go about five times a year. This summary is based on the association's "Auto Camping White Paper".

The short version

  • Auto-camping participation peaked at 15.8 million in 1996, then fell by more than half over the following decade or so
  • It grew every year from 2013 to 2019, reaching 8.6 million, then fell to 6.1 million in 2020 because of the pandemic
  • Fewer people camp than during the 1990s boom, but trips and nights per camper remain high

Auto-camping participation over time

"Auto-camping participation" is an estimate of the number of people who went auto camping (camping where you drive to the site) at least once in a year. A boom in the mid-1990s pushed it up to 15.8 million in 1996, but it then kept falling, to 7.05 million in 2008.

From 2013 it began to grow again, beating the previous year every year: 8.3 million in 2016, 8.5 million in 2018 and 8.6 million in 2019.

After the pandemic

In pandemic-hit 2020, participation fell to 6.1 million, about 30% lower than the year before. It recovered to 7.5 million in 2021, but in 2022 it was 6.5 million, 86.7% of the previous year. Even while camping was a hot topic on TV and online, the number of participants came nowhere near the 1990s boom.

We could not find participation figures for 2023 or later in the press releases and executive summaries the association has published.

How often each camper goes has changed a lot. In 2015 the average was 3.5 trips a year; since 2022 it has been about five. The association says camping has shifted from "a special once-a-year family outing" to an everyday kind of leisure that people do with family or alone whenever they feel like it.

In 2024, trips and nights dipped slightly, partly because of extreme summer heat, but in 2025 they stayed high at 5.1 trips and 6.9 nights. In 2025 the share of "beginners" with one year of camping experience rose to 16.1%, the first increase in five years.

Campsites and costs

  • In 2024 the average cost of going camping (including fuel, food and campsite fees) was ¥23,258, up ¥2,865 on the previous year
  • In 2025 campsite occupancy was 16.3%, the same level as in 2020

While the share of people going out at all is falling (see the weekday outing rate hit a record low of 74.1%), camping seems to have become a pastime that its fans return to again and again.

FAQ

How many people go camping in Japan?

The Japan Auto Camping Association estimates that 6.5 million people went auto camping at least once in 2022. The peak was 15.8 million in 1996.

Is the camping boom over?

In terms of people, participation fell from 7.5 million in 2021 to 6.5 million in 2022. But campers still went an average of 5.1 times in 2025, so those who stick with it go often.

Summary

  • Auto-camping participation peaked at 15.8 million in 1996
  • It was 8.6 million in 2019, 6.1 million in 2020 and 6.5 million in 2022
  • What has grown is not the number of people, but how often each person goes
