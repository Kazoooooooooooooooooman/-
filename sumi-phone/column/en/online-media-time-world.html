People in Japan spend 15 hours 10 minutes a week on social media and online video, the shortest of any country surveyed

November 3, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

People often say that the Japanese spend too much time on their phones. But compared with the rest of the world, people in Japan spend 15 hours 10 minutes a week on online media such as social media and video. That was the shortest of all the countries and regions surveyed. The global average is 33 hours 27 minutes a week, more than double Japan's figure. The short version

  • Japan's 15 hours 10 minutes a week is the shortest of all the countries and regions surveyed
  • The global average is 33 hours 27 minutes a week. The US is at 43 hours 23 minutes and South Korea at 24 hours 22 minutes
  • The figure simply adds up time spent on social media, video, online news, music and so on

The daily figure is a rough guide, worked out by dividing the weekly time by seven. After Japan, the shortest are Hong Kong (20 hours 27 minutes) and China (21 hours 03 minutes), so East Asian countries and regions cluster at the short end.

What time is being counted

The figure comes from asking internet users aged 16 and over about time spent on the following, and adding it up.

  • Social media
  • Online video
  • Online news and articles
  • Podcasts and music streaming
  • Streaming services (TV watched online)

Email, work and looking things up are not included. The report also notes that someone scrolling social media while listening to music is counted for both, so the figures can come out longer than the real time.

Is Japan's figure really that short?

The picture changes when you compare it with Japanese surveys. In a survey by Japan's Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC), people in Japan aged 13 to 79 used the internet for 183.9 minutes (about 3 hours 4 minutes) a day on weekdays. That figure includes work and looking things up.

  • International survey (online media only): about 2 hours 10 minutes a day
  • MIC survey (all internet use): about 3 hours 4 minutes a day (weekdays)

Either way, it is not true that the Japanese are uniquely heavy users. But a short figure is no reason to relax, either. Looking only at younger people in Japan, the MIC survey found that people in their 20s used the internet for 266.9 minutes (about 4 hours 27 minutes) on weekdays, close to the global average.

Things to keep in mind about the numbers

  • These are times that people reported themselves in a survey, not measured times
  • The research company GWI changed its methods substantially in 2024, so the figures cannot be compared with earlier years
  • The age and lifestyles of internet users differ from country to country, so this is not a like-for-like comparison

FAQ

Where does Japan rank in the world for time spent on social media and video?

In DataReportal's 2026 report, Japan was last, with the shortest time of all the countries and regions surveyed. At 15 hours 10 minutes a week, it is less than half the global average (33 hours 27 minutes).

Why is Japan's figure so short?

The report does not give reasons. Several factors could play a part, such as differences in how people answer, differences in the age of internet users, and the fact that people in Japan still spend a lot of time watching TV. But this survey alone cannot say for sure.

Which country spends the longest?

Kenya, at 63 hours 34 minutes a week. It is followed by the Philippines (54 hours 00 minutes), Mexico (53 hours 41 minutes) and Brazil (53 hours 30 minutes).

Summary

  • People in Japan spend 15 hours 10 minutes a week on social media, video and similar media, the shortest of all those surveyed
  • The global average is 33 hours 27 minutes a week, more than double Japan's
  • But among people in their 20s, internet time in Japan tops 4 hours a day
