How long do young people play video games? Weekday hours, and what happens as play time grows

October 30, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

Among people aged 10 to 29 who play video games, 24.6% of males and 10.4% of females play for 3 hours or more on weekdays. The longer people played, the more said they "can't stop when I should" or "my day and night got reversed", and among those playing 6 hours or more a day, half had experienced a reversed day-night cycle. This summary is based on a national survey by the Kurihama Medical and Addiction Center.

The short version

  • 85.0% of people aged 10 to 29 had played games in the past year. The device they used most was a smartphone (80.7%)
  • 3 hours or more on weekdays: males 24.6% (6 hours or more: 3.7%), females 10.4% (6 hours or more: 1.6%)
  • Among people who play 6 hours or more a day, 50.4% had a reversed day-night cycle and 29.8% said it affected their grades or work

This is time per weekday among people aged 10 to 29 who played games in the past year. One in four males plays for 3 hours or more. On days off, both males and females played longer than on weekdays.

What increases as gaming time grows

It is not only a reversed day-night cycle. The problems below also became more common as gaming time grew.

Under 60 min21.9%5.0%4.3%

Past 2 hours, more than one in five say their grades or work efficiency dropped. Note, however, that this shows long play and problems occurring together; it does not prove that games alone are the cause.

Where, and with whom, they play

  • Device: smartphone 80.7%, home game console 48.3%
  • Place: at home 97.6%, while on the move 32.5%
  • Mainly play online: 48.1%
  • With whom: alone 89.1%, real-life friends 38.5%, family 18.7%, strangers online 11.7%

Are the rules kept?

In the same center's survey of people aged 10 to 79, those aged 19 and under were asked about parent-child rules when they first got their own game device.

  • Made rules and kept them: 25.0%
  • Made rules but sometimes could not keep them: 34.6%
  • Made rules but could not keep them at all: 9.6%
  • Did not make rules: 18.4%

Even with rules in place, only one in four kept them fully. One approach is for parents and children to agree not only on time limits but also on a signal for stopping (a timer, a place to put the device away, and so on). How to make family rules is covered in how to make family phone rules.

About the survey

  • From January to March 2019, questionnaires were sent to 9,000 people aged 10 to 29 chosen at random from 300 locations nationwide, and 5,096 responded (a 56.6% response rate)
  • Because more people aged 10 to 18 were sampled, the figures are shown as age-adjusted percentages
  • This is a 2019 survey; we will update this article when results from a newer national survey come out

Summary

  • One in four young male gamers plays for 3 hours or more on weekdays
  • At 6 hours or more, half had experienced a reversed day-night cycle
  • Past 2 hours, more than 20% feel it affects their grades or work
