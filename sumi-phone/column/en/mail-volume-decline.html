Japan's mail volume is now less than half its peak. The trend from fiscal 2001 to fiscal 2025

November 9, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

Japan's mail has fallen to less than half of what it was a quarter of a century ago. Domestic mail peaked at 26.2 billion items in fiscal 2001 (Japan's fiscal year runs April–March), and in fiscal 2025 it was about 11.7 billion. In fiscal 2025, Japan Post accepted 11,751.03 million items of mail, 6.5% fewer than the year before. This summary is based on Japan Post's announcements and a document posted by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC).

Domestic mail, fiscal 2001 to 2025

Domestic mail fell from 26.2 billion items in fiscal 2001 to 13.6 billion in fiscal 2023. The document describes this as "a 48.3% decrease over 22 years (an average decrease of 3.0% a year)." The decline has continued since, and domestic mail in fiscal 2025 was 11,729.26 million items. That is about 55% less than at the peak.

Fiscal years run from April to the following March. Figures through fiscal 2023 are rounded to the nearest 100 million; figures for fiscal 2024 and 2025 are from Japan Post's announcements, shown to the nearest 100 million.

Both letters and postcards are down

TypeFiscal 2001Fiscal 2025
First-class (letters, etc.)13.2 billion6.19 billion
Second-class (postcards)11.3 billion4.34 billion

Letters have fallen to less than half, and postcards to about 40%. Among postcards, New Year's mail (nengajo, the greeting cards Japanese people traditionally send for the New Year) came to 507.1 million items in fiscal 2025, down 27.1% from the year before. We covered the change in how many New Year's postcards are printed in New Year's postcard printing falls to 560 million.

Why has mail fallen?

The document posted by MIC lists reasons such as these:

  • The spread of the internet and social media
  • Bills and similar documents moving online
  • Companies cutting their spending on communication and advertising
  • Less letter writing between individuals

Postage rates were revised in October 2024. The same document also includes an outlook, as of December 2023, putting mail volume at 11.3 billion items in fiscal 2028.

Parcels have not fallen like mail

Japan Post counts mail (items) and parcels separately. In fiscal 2025 it handled 4,302.28 million parcels, down 0.8% from the year before. Of these, Yu-Pack (Japan Post's standard parcel service) accounted for 565.47 million, up 1.3%. In fiscal 2024, parcels had risen by 11.7%. So parcels go up in some years and down in others, while letters and postcards keep falling.

Things to keep in mind

  • The figures are the number of items Japan Post accepted. They are not the number of items people received or the number of people who sent mail
  • Mail includes direct mail and bills sent by companies. It is not only personal letters
  • In fiscal 2024 and 2025, election postcards (defined under the Public Offices Election Act) increased from the year before

FAQ

How much has mail in Japan fallen?

Domestic mail was 26.2 billion items at its peak in fiscal 2001. In fiscal 2025 it was 11,729.26 million, a fall of about 55%. Total mail in fiscal 2025, including international mail, was 11,751.03 million items.

How much have New Year's cards fallen?

According to Japan Post's figures for accepted mail, New Year's mail in fiscal 2025 was 507.1 million items, down 27.1% from the year before. In fiscal 2024 it had already fallen by 28.4%.

Summary

  • Domestic mail fell from 26.2 billion items in fiscal 2001 to about 11.7 billion in fiscal 2025
  • Both letters and postcards have fallen to less than half
  • Parcels stand at about 4.3 billion and have not fallen the way mail has

Even after this decline, Japan still delivers more than 10 billion items of mail a year. Now and then, closing the screen and walking a handwritten postcard to the nearest postbox is not a bad way to spend some time.
