How to stay in touch without LINE: the tricky situations and the alternatives

October 21, 2026 (updated October 25, 2026) · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

You can live without LINE (Japan's most-used messaging app), even in Japan in 2026. For keeping in touch with family and friends, the built-in messaging app, which works with just a phone number, can stand in for almost everything. The tricky parts tend to be school, club and group communication. This article covers the alternatives to LINE and how to get through each of the tricky situations.

The short version

  • Since September 2026, all three major carriers, docomo, au and SoftBank, let iPhone and Android users message each other free through the built-in messaging app (RCS)
  • Even if the other person isn't using RCS, your message will always get through by SMS (from 3.3 yen per message)
  • The tricky parts are school, club and group communication, and services you can only use through LINE. Tell people in advance to "contact me by phone number," or receive messages through a family member's LINE

How widely is LINE used?

In the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications' fiscal 2025 survey, 92.9% of people used LINE, far ahead of other social media. According to LY Corporation (the company behind LINE), its monthly users in Japan passed 100 million (as of December 2025).

Precisely because nearly everyone uses it, people worry, "If I quit LINE, won't I lose touch?" But even when the other person uses LINE, there are ways to reach them without using it yourself.

Ways to stay in touch instead of LINE

1. The built-in messaging app (RCS)

The best alternative is the messaging app that comes on your phone. RCS on iPhone was launched by au in April 2025 and by SoftBank in March 2026. In September 2026, docomo iPhones also gained support from iOS 27, so RCS now works between iPhone and Android on all three major Japanese carriers.

  • You can send with just a phone number (no account sign-up needed)
  • You can send photos and videos and chat in groups
  • In most cases there's nothing to apply for, and sending and receiving are free (it uses mobile data)
  • If the other person isn't using RCS, the message is automatically sent as an SMS (in which case SMS charges apply)

On iPhone this is the Messages app; on Android it's Google Messages.

2. SMS (text messages)

As long as you have a phone number, it reaches any mobile phone. On docomo, a message of up to 70 full-width (Japanese) characters costs 3.3 yen (tax included), and receiving is free. Its strength is that it reliably reaches people using flip phones or simple phones too.

3. +Message

A messaging app that works with phone numbers, launched by docomo, au and SoftBank in May 2018. Its users passed 40 million (February 2024). However, on Android each carrier's default app has switched to Google Messages (au from 2024; docomo and SoftBank on new models from March 2026 onward). If you're starting fresh now, the built-in messaging app (RCS) is the better choice.

4. Phone calls

For urgent matters or working out details, a phone call is the quickest way to get it done. A one-minute call is often faster than going back and forth by text many times.

5. Email

For long messages or sending files, email such as Gmail works well. Exchanging documents for work or school was always better suited to email anyway.

Tricky situations and how to get through them

Friend groups

The most common is making plans to hang out, or group chats with classmates.

  • Say at the start, "I've quit LINE. Please message me by phone number"
  • Ask one person in the group to forward you just the important decisions
  • RCS supports group chats too, so set up an RCS group with a few close friends

Communication between school and parents

More and more schools use dedicated apps rather than LINE for school notices. For example, "tetoru," an app for elementary and middle schools, had been adopted by more than 6,300 schools as of March 2026, and its basic features are free for public schools. There are others too, such as "Classi" for high schools and "Sugūru," which local governments adopt.

In a survey by LY Corporation of elementary and middle school teachers and parents, among those using two or more ways of communicating, 89% of parents and 87% of teachers said they wanted them "combined into one." School communication is shifting toward being received through an app chosen by the school or local government.

Clubs, lessons and the PTA

For school clubs and local groups, messages are still often passed around in LINE groups.

  • Tell the club adviser or coach, "I don't use LINE, so please contact me by phone number or email"
  • Suggest a group app such as "BAND," which has a message board and attendance checks
  • For a child's club, receive messages on a parent's LINE and pass them on to the child

Some boards of education restrict teachers and students from contacting each other one-on-one through LINE (Shizuoka and Okinawa prefectures, for example). For school clubs, if you ask to be contacted by some way other than LINE, you'll rarely be refused.

Services you can only use through LINE

Some information, such as shop coupons, reservations and local governments' official LINE accounts, can only be received through LINE. Many local governments post information through official LINE accounts, but the same information is usually also put out on their websites or by email. You can also split things so that you use LINE on a family member's phone or a computer only when you need to do something that can only be done through LINE.

Tips for keeping it up without LINE

  1. Tell people first: Let the people you contact most know up front: "I've quit LINE. Use phone-number messages or call me." That's far easier than having them find out later
  2. Decide how you'll reply: There are no LINE-style "read" receipts, so deciding to "send a quick reply once I've seen it" puts the other person at ease too
  3. Keep one way to receive: Decide on just one fallback for when you really need LINE, such as a family member's LINE or LINE on a computer

We believe separating the tools you use to stay in touch from the tools that eat up your time is the shortest route to changing how you live with your phone.

Summary

  • 92.9% of people use LINE. But you can stay in touch without it
  • Since September 2026, all three carriers let iPhone and Android users message each other free through the built-in messaging app (RCS)
  • More schools are using dedicated apps other than LINE for school communication
  • Tell friends and clubs in advance, and keep one fallback for when you really need LINE
