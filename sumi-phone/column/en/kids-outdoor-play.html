How much do children in Japan play outside? What surveys of nature experiences show

November 9, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

In the fiscal 2019 survey, 50.8% of elementary and junior high school students said they had swum in the sea or a river "many times." In fiscal 1998 the figure was 60.2%, so it fell 9.4 points in about 20 years. The share who had caught insects "many times" also went from 50.2% to 43.6%. This summary is based on the National Institution for Youth Education's "Survey on Youth Experiences."

The short version

  • Fewer children than in fiscal 1998 have had nature experiences such as swimming in the sea or a river, or catching insects, "many times"
  • Children who spent more time outdoors were less likely to be nearsighted
  • In fiscal 2022, more than half of elementary school students did not take part in any nature activity run by local groups and similar organizations

Children who have swum in the sea or a river

There is a big jump in fiscal 2012, but the wording of the question changed that year. Up to fiscal 2009 the question was "How often have you done the following?", and from fiscal 2012 it was "How often have you done the following so far?" Comparing only the years from fiscal 2012 onward, which used the same wording, the share fell gradually from 57.3% to 50.8%.

The share of children who had "almost never" caught insects rose from 19.0% in fiscal 1998 to 26.4% in fiscal 2019. Watching or hearing wild birds, on the other hand, did not decline. The institution concludes that "through the 2010s, the share answering 'many times' shows a slight downward trend for some nature experiences."

Time outdoors and nearsightedness

The same survey also looked at how the time 4th to 6th graders spend outdoors each week relates to nearsightedness. Among children who spent "almost no" time outdoors, 36.7% were nearsighted; among those who spent "10 hours or more," it was 19.8%. Children who spent 10 hours or more outdoors were also less likely to be obese. Here, "nearsighted" means children who wear glasses or contact lenses, or who need them but do not wear them. We covered how children's eyesight has changed in the decline in children's eyesight.

What the fiscal 2022 survey found

Results of the fiscal 2022 survey were presented at a panel meeting of MEXT (the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology).

  • The share of children who had nature experiences with family or friends was 55.2% (fiscal 2006), 57.5% (fiscal 2019) and 56.4% (fiscal 2022), so it has not changed much
  • The share of elementary school students who did not take part in any nature activity run by children's associations, local groups, public facilities and the like rose from 32.3% in fiscal 2006 to 55.2% in fiscal 2022
  • The reasons parents gave for not taking part were "my child showed no interest" (27.7%), "it takes too much of the parents' time" (24.9%) and "I didn't know such groups or events existed" (23.7%)

Things to keep in mind

  • To allow comparison with fiscal 1998, the trend figures count only the answers of 4th graders, 6th graders and 8th graders (2nd year of junior high), excluding missing answers
  • The fiscal 2019 survey was carried out from January to April 2020 at public elementary, junior high and high schools across Japan, and 14,477 children answered
  • The link between time outdoors and nearsightedness or obesity comes from measuring both at the same time. It does not show that time outdoors caused less nearsightedness
  • The fiscal 2022 figures are as cited in the panel's materials (a presentation by a university researcher)

FAQ

Do children play outside less than they used to?

Fewer children than in fiscal 1998 have done things like swimming in the sea or a river or catching insects "many times." However, the share of children who had nature experiences with family or friends stayed at around 55–57% from fiscal 2006 to fiscal 2022 and has not changed much.

Does spending time outdoors make nearsightedness less likely?

In the National Institution for Youth Education's fiscal 2019 survey, elementary school students who spent 10 hours or more a week outdoors tended to be less often nearsighted. The institution says it "may help keep it in check."

Summary

  • The share of children who have swum in the sea or a river "many times" fell from 60.2% in fiscal 1998 to 50.8% in fiscal 2019
  • Children who spent more time outdoors tended to be less often nearsighted
  • In fiscal 2022, 55.2% of elementary school students took part in no nature activity run by local groups and similar organizations
