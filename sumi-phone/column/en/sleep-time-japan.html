How long do Japanese people sleep on average? Trends by gender, and the share sleeping under 6 hours

October 30, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

Japanese people aged 10 and over sleep an average of 7 hours 54 minutes a day (2021, averaged over the whole week). That's 14 minutes longer than in 2016. But this average includes weekends, and a Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare survey found that 37.5% of men and 40.6% of women sleep less than 6 hours. This summary is based on materials from the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare.

The short version

  • Average sleep is 7 hours 54 minutes a day (men 7 hours 58 minutes, women 7 hours 49 minutes)
  • Up 14 minutes from 2016. Until then it had been on a downward trend
  • 37.5% of men and 40.6% of women sleep less than 6 hours (2019)

Sleep time had been shrinking little by little, but it rose in 2021. That was during the COVID-19 pandemic, a year when working from home became widespread. The same survey also found that people who worked from home slept 18 minutes longer than those who didn't (on weekdays, among people in work).

Why "7 hours 54 minutes on average" may not match how it feels

  • It's an average that includes weekends: It's a "whole week" figure that evens out weekdays and weekends. Someone who sleeps little on weekdays and a lot on days off still looks like a long sleeper on average
  • It's an average of everyone aged 10 and over: It includes people who don't work and children. Looking only at employed people on workdays, those not working from home slept 7 hours 14 minutes
  • It varies a lot from person to person: Even with an average in the 7-hour range, nearly 40% sleep less than 6 hours, as shown below

Share sleeping under 6 hours

According to the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Sleep Guide for Health Promotion 2023," the 2019 National Health and Nutrition Survey found that 37.5% of men and 40.6% of women slept less than 6 hours a day on average. Among men in their 30s to 50s and women in their 40s and 50s, more than 40% sleep under 6 hours.

The same guide notes that a 2021 OECD (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development) report also found Japan's average sleep time to be the shortest of the 33 countries it looked at.

How much sleep is enough

  • Adults: aim for 6 hours or more, and make sure you get the sleep you need
  • Middle and high school students: 8–10 hours
  • Elementary school children: 9–12 hours

The guide says that it's important to look not only at hours but also at whether you feel rested in the morning ("sense of restfulness from sleep"). How much children actually sleep is covered in sleep time for elementary and middle school students.

How to get more sleep

  • No screens in the hour before bed: Using your phone right before bed pushes your bedtime later. Compare your own use with average smartphone use time in Japan
  • Keep your phone out of the bedroom: Getting a separate alarm clock makes this easier to stick with
  • Get up at the same time: Sleeping in on days off makes it harder to fall asleep the next night

Practical steps are covered in how to stop using your phone before bed.

Summary

  • Japanese people sleep 7 hours 54 minutes on average (2021, aged 10 and over, whole week)
  • Even so, 37.5% of men and 40.6% of women sleep less than 6 hours
  • For adults, the guideline is 6 hours or more
