How university students can focus on writing papers: dealing with the temptations of working on a computer

October 24, 2026 (updated October 25, 2026) · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

When you study on paper, you can just put your phone in another room. But a paper (a written report for a university class) has to be written on a computer. And a computer has a browser, social media, video and chat on it. The tool you write with is the same as the doorway to temptation. That is why papers are so hard to focus on. This article sticks to computer work and sets out five steps to protect your focus.

The short version

  • Before you start writing, decide your headings and what you need to look up on paper. Don't start thinking only after you open the computer
  • Silence notifications with your computer's Focus settings before you write
  • Keep browser tabs for research time separate from writing time. Close them while you write
  • Put your phone in another room. If you need it for two-factor authentication, place it face down out of reach
  • On days when you can't get started, write a "bad draft" for just 15 minutes

Step 1: Decide on paper before you open the computer

If you only start thinking "what should I write?" after opening your computer, your hand drifts to the browser the moment your thoughts stall. Before writing, put these three things down on paper:

  • The question of the paper (what it is about and what you will argue), in one sentence
  • Three to five headings
  • What you need to look up

When no structure comes to mind, it can help to leave your desk and think while walking. Experiments have confirmed that walking increases the number of ideas people come up with (the benefits of walking).

Step 2: Silence notifications with your computer's Focus settings

Mac: go to the Apple menu → System Settings → Focus in the sidebar to create a Focus. You can limit which apps and people can send notifications while you write, and have it turn on automatically at set times.

Windows: in Windows 11, go to Settings → System → Notifications to use "Do not disturb", which silences notifications, or "Focus", which sets aside a fixed block of time to concentrate. Screen names may change depending on the version.

Close chat and email apps during your writing time. How to sort out your notifications is covered in which notifications to turn off and which to keep.

Step 3: Separate research time from writing time

The most common mistake is researching while you write, and then drifting off to some other page while you research.

  1. Research time (say, 30 minutes): open the sources you need and paste the parts you will use into a note or document. Write down the sources at the same time
  2. Writing time: close the browser. If you want to look something up, type "[CHECK]" in the text and keep going
  3. Check everything at the end: look up only the "[CHECK]" items, all together

Step 4: Don't keep your phone on the desk just for two-factor authentication

Logging in to your university's systems sometimes requires authentication on your phone. While you are at it, you glance at a notification, and 30 minutes are gone. Log in before you start writing, then put your phone back in another room. For phone habits in general, see how to stop picking up your phone while studying.

Step 5: On days when you can't get started, write badly for 15 minutes

When you freeze in front of a blank screen, it is easy to start doing something else. On days like that, set a timer for 15 minutes, decide that "a bad draft is fine", and write. Fixing it can wait. Writing is much easier to fix once it exists. For a way to break up your time, the Pomodoro Technique works well.

Summary

  • Papers are hard to focus on because the computer is both your writing tool and the doorway to temptation
  • Decide your structure on paper before opening the computer, silence notifications with Focus, and keep research time separate from writing time
  • What to do today: write three headings on paper, then turn on Focus and write for 15 minutes
