Six months after the "2 hours of phone use a day" ordinance, only 5.1% say they use their phones less

November 3, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

In October 2025, Toyoake City in Aichi Prefecture brought in an ordinance that asks residents to keep phone use outside of work and study to "within 2 hours a day" as a guideline. In a survey six months later, 87.6% of residents knew about the ordinance. Yet only 5.1% said they now spend less time on their phones. There is a big gap between knowing and cutting back. This summary is based on documents published by the city.

What the ordinance says

  • Its official name is the "Toyoake City Ordinance on Promoting the Appropriate Use of Smartphones and Other Devices"
  • It took effect on October 1, 2025
  • It encourages people to keep their use of smartphones and similar devices in free time, excluding work, school and so on, within 2 hours a day as a guideline
  • There are no penalties. The city's first goal is "to get people to become aware of their own use"

What changed after the ordinance (ages 16 to 80)

43.2% said "some aspect of my behavior or awareness has changed." But only about 1 in 20 actually cut their time. The city itself notes that the ordinance has no penalties and that it has barely begun to put measures in place.

On weekdays, 22.9% of teens and 16.7% of people in their 20s spend 5 hours or more a day on their phones in free time alone. On days off, it is 46.1% of teens and 46.6% of people in their 20s, close to half.

Results for children (elementary and junior high students)

  • 46% of junior high students sleep 7 hours or less
  • About 30% of upper elementary students and about 40% of junior high students go to bed later because of phones and similar devices
  • Only 10.1% of families had house rules that applied to parents as well as children

Phone time and loneliness

In the survey of people aged 16 and over, those who spent 5 hours or more a day on their phones in free time were more likely to feel lonely "sometimes" or "often or always." The mayor commented that the city needs places where people can easily go out and meet others.

Reading these numbers

  • The survey of people aged 16 to 80 was mailed to 5,000 randomly selected residents, and 1,907 responded (a 38.1% response rate)
  • The survey of elementary and junior high students was carried out on the learning devices at the city's public elementary and junior high schools, and 4,313 students responded
  • These are times people reported themselves, not measured times

FAQ

Does Toyoake's ordinance have penalties?

No. "Within 2 hours a day" is a guideline, and there is no penalty for going over it. The city's first aim is to raise awareness.

Did the ordinance reduce phone time?

In the survey of people aged 16 to 80, 5.1% said they use their phones less. 27.1% said they had become more aware of how they use their phones.

Do the 2 hours include time for work or study?

No. The ordinance's guideline is about free time, excluding work and study. We cover how to think about setting rules as a family in family phone rules.

Summary

  • Nearly 90% know about the ordinance, but only 5.1% have cut their time
  • Among people in their teens and 20s, more than 80% go over 2 hours of free-time phone use on days off
  • Awareness alone does little to cut the time we spend on our phones
