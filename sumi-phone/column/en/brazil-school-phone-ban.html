Brazil restricted phones in schools by law in 2025. One year on, 92% of schools have put it in place

November 12, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

On January 13, 2025, Brazil passed a law restricting phone use in schools (Lei nº 15.100/2025). From primary school through high school, in public and private schools alike, students may not use their phones during class or during breaks. A year later, a survey led by the Ministry of Education found that, according to school managers, 92% of schools were already applying the rule. This summary is based on the text of the law, government announcements and press reports.

What the law says

The stated aim of the law is to protect the mental, physical and psychological health of children and teenagers. The main points are:

ItemDetails
Who it coversEvery stage of basic education (primary through high school), public and private
What is bannedStudents using their own phones and similar devices during class, at recess and between lessons
Classroom exceptionUse strictly for learning, as directed by the teacher
Always allowed forAccessibility (so students with disabilities can learn), inclusion, students' health needs, and protecting fundamental rights
EmergenciesSituations of danger and similar emergencies are not covered by the ban
Schools' dutiesTeach about mental health and the risks of overusing phones, train staff regularly, and provide spaces where students can talk and get support

The law was published in the Official Gazette on January 14, 2025 and took effect that day. A presidential decree with more detailed rules (Decreto nº 12.385) followed on February 18, 2025. Each school decides how phones are stored and what happens when students break the rule. The law bans "using" phones, not bringing them to school.

What the one-year survey found

The Ministry of Education ran a national survey on the law's first year together with Inep (the national education research institute), the Alana Institute (a nonprofit) and UNESCO's office in Brazil. It drew a random sample of 8,189 schools and asked school managers and others in March and April 2026.

The share of schools that ban phones across the whole school (including the playground and breaks) more than doubled, from 20% before the law to 48%. Before the law, 13% of schools let students use phones anywhere and at any time; reports say such schools no longer exist.

Remaining problems

  • 39% of managers said getting students to follow the new rules is hard
  • Another 39% said providing facilities to store phones is hard, with a gap between public schools (45%) and private schools (18%)
  • 31% said keeping up supervision during lessons and breaks is hard

The survey asked school managers and others how they see things; it did not measure grades or other outcomes. Keep that in mind when reading the figures.

How it compares with other countries

What stands out about Brazil is that it set the rule by national law, and that the rule covers breaks too. The Netherlands removed phones from classrooms through a national agreement rather than a law (the Dutch classroom phone ban). Time outside school is up to families, but when there are no phones at break, students may naturally spend more of that time talking with friends or moving around.

FAQ

Are students in Brazil banned from bringing phones to school?

The law bans "using" phones during class and breaks. Each school decides how phones are stored. Phones can be used for learning tasks set by the teacher, and for needs related to disability or health, among other exceptions.

How many countries ban phones in schools?

According to UNESCO, about 58% of countries worldwide as of March 2026. See school phone bans now cover 58% of the world's countries.

Summary

  • A January 2025 Brazilian law bans phone use in class and at break, from primary through high school
  • In a 2026 survey, school managers reported that 92% of schools apply the rule, and 97% said students take more part in lessons
  • Getting students to follow the rule and providing phone storage remain the main problems
