Notifications to turn off, notifications to keep: how to clean up your phone's notifications

October 24, 2026 (updated October 25, 2026) · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

Notifications are one of the main reasons we pick up our phones. But if you turn them all off, you start worrying about missing something important, and end up opening your phone even more. The key is not to turn everything off, but to sort them. This article explains how to split notifications into three groups, with step-by-step instructions for iPhone and Android. It takes about 15 minutes.

The short version

  • Sort notifications into three groups: "arrive right away," "arrive in a batch" and "don't arrive"
  • Only messages from people (calls, family, work) should arrive right away
  • In an experiment, people who got notifications batched three times a day focused better, felt better and felt more in control of their phones
  • But people who turned off notifications entirely became more anxious
  • On iPhone you can sort them with Scheduled Summary; on Android, with App notifications

Batch notifications instead of turning them all off

Researchers at Duke University in the US and elsewhere (Fitz, Ariely and colleagues) split 237 people into three groups for a two-week experiment.

  • A group that got notifications as usual
  • A group that got notifications batched three times a day (9 a.m., 3 p.m. and 9 p.m.)
  • A group that got no notifications at all

As a result, the group with notifications batched three times a day focused better, felt better and felt more in control of their phones than the group that carried on as usual. On the other hand, the group with no notifications at all became more anxious and more worried that they might be missing something. Batching notifications every hour had almost no effect.

In other words, the best setup is not "zero," but "messages from people right away, everything else in a few batches a day."

Sort notifications into three groups

GroupWhat goes in itExamples
Arrive right awayMessages a person sent directly to youCalls, messages from family and friends, work messages, calendar events
Arrive in a batchThings you want to see, but that aren't urgentNews, shopping, banking, delivery updates
Don't arriveThings sent to get you to open an appSocial media likes and recommendations, games, video apps, sales

If you're not sure, ask: "Did a person send this, or did an app?" Notifications that apps send automatically almost all belong in "don't arrive" or "arrive in a batch."

Cleaning up notifications on iPhone

1. Turn off notifications from apps you don't need to hear from

  1. Open Settings → Notifications
  2. Under Notification Style, tap an app
  3. Turn off Allow Notifications

2. Bundle non-urgent notifications at set times (Scheduled Summary)

  1. Tap Settings → Notifications → Scheduled Summary
  2. Turn on Scheduled Summary and choose when to receive your summaries (for example, three times: morning, midday and evening)
  3. Turn on the apps you want included in the summary

You can also choose between Immediate Delivery and Scheduled Summary under Notification Delivery on each app's screen.

Cleaning up notifications on Android

  1. Open Settings → Notifications → App notifications
  2. Under Most recent, check which apps have sent notifications lately. You can see other apps under All apps
  3. Tap any app you don't need to hear from and turn its notifications off

You can also stop a notification as soon as it arrives. Swipe down from the top of the screen, touch and hold the notification, then use the settings icon to turn off all notifications from that app. Menu names can differ slightly depending on the phone model.

Tips for keeping it up after you clean up

  • When you install a new app, choose "Don't Allow" when it first asks about notifications. If you need them later, you can turn them on
  • Review App notifications once a month. Turn off any notifications that have crept back in
  • If you still open your phone with notifications off, turn the screen black and white (how to turn your phone black and white)

Summary

  • Rather than turning off every notification, sort them into "right away," "in a batch" and "don't arrive"
  • An experiment found that batching them three times a day made it easier to focus and improved mood
  • What to do today: open your notification settings and turn off notifications from social media, games and video apps

The only notifications that really matter are messages from people. Soto Phone is a phone in development that delivers calls and messages, while notifications from social media and games never arrive in the first place.
