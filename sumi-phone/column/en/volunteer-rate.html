17.8% of people in Japan volunteered in a year: the lowest in 20 years, and highest among people aged 65–69

November 12, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

In the year up to 2021, 17.8% of people in Japan aged 10 and over did some volunteer work. That is roughly 1 in 6. In 2016, five years earlier, the figure was 26.0%, so it fell by 8.2 points. It is the lowest figure in the surveys since 2001. The survey period included two COVID-19 states of emergency, which may have played a part. This summary is based on the Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities by the Statistics Bureau of Japan.

The short version

  • The rate stayed at 26–29% from 2001 to 2016, then fell to 17.8% in 2021
  • It fell in every age group. The biggest drop was among 10- to 14-year-olds (26.5% → 12.1%)
  • The 2021 figure covers a period when COVID-19 cut back on activities where people gather

Share of people who volunteered (2001–2021)

After a small dip in 2006, the rate held steady at around 26%. It then fell sharply in 2021, to 17.8%. In numbers of people, that is a drop from about 29.44 million in 2016 to about 20.06 million in 2021.

From 2001 onward, women always volunteered at a higher rate than men. In 2021, though, the drop among women (−9.4 points) was bigger than among men (−6.8 points), and men's rate came out higher.

By age: highest among people aged 65–69

The rate was highest at 23.4% for ages 65–69 and lowest at 10.1% for ages 25–29. It was above 20% for people in their 40s and for those in their 60s to early 70s. From the late teens to the early 30s, it stayed at around 10%.

Things to keep in mind

  • The Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities has been carried out every five years since 1976. The 2021 survey covered about 190,000 people aged 10 and over in about 91,000 households (about 180,000 were included in these results)
  • "Volunteer work" means giving your own time, effort or skills for your community or for others, without aiming to be paid. It includes blood donation, neighborhood cleanups, disaster prevention and crime prevention, helping with children's neighborhood groups, and disaster relief
  • The survey asked whether people did it even once between October 20, 2020 and October 19, 2021. That period included two COVID-19 states of emergency
  • The share of people who did disaster-related volunteer work was high at 3.8% in 2011, the year of the Great East Japan Earthquake, and 0.8% in 2021

Measured as time per day, "volunteer and social participation activities" take up just 2 minutes on average (how much free time people in Japan have each day). The share of people who go out has also fallen (the share of people who go out).

FAQ

How many people in Japan do volunteer work?

In the 2021 Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, 17.8% of people aged 10 and over, about 20.06 million people, did volunteer work in the past year. That is roughly 1 in 6.

Which age group volunteers the most?

In 2021, the rate was highest at 23.4% for ages 65–69, followed by 23.0% for ages 70–74. It was lowest at 10.1% for ages 25–29.

Summary

  • In 2021, 17.8% of people did volunteer work, 8.2 points lower than five years earlier
  • The rate fell in every age group. Among 10- to 14-year-olds it fell by more than half, from 26.5% to 12.1%
  • These results come from the COVID-19 period, so it will be worth watching how far the rate recovers in the next survey, five years on
