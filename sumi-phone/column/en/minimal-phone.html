What is a minimal phone? Models you can choose in Japan and how to pick one

October 15, 2026 (updated November 1, 2026) · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

A minimal phone is a mobile phone that leaves out time-draining features such as social media, games and a browser from the start. Overseas it is also called a "dumb phone", and it is drawing attention mainly among younger people who are tired of smartphones. This article covers the types of minimal phone you can choose in Japan, and what you should know about "giteki" (Japan's radio equipment certification) before buying an overseas model.

The short version

  • In Japan you can choose from three types: (1) a carrier's 4G feature phone, (2) a flip-style smartphone, and (3) an overseas minimal phone
  • (1) and (2) are safe to use in Japan. With (3), using it in Japan without the "giteki mark" may break the Radio Act
  • When choosing, first decide "are calls and SMS enough?" and "do I need maps, transit and LINE?"

What is a minimal phone (dumb phone)?

There is no fixed definition, but it usually means a phone like this:

  • It has no social media, games, video apps or browser (or they cannot be installed)
  • It is limited to the features you need, such as calls, SMS, a camera and music
  • It is built so you don't want to keep looking at it, for example with a small screen or a black-and-white (e-paper) display

"Dumb phone" means a phone with deliberately few features, in contrast to the clever, "smart" phone. Recently, models that put weight on design and philosophy are more and more often called "minimal phones".

Why they are drawing attention overseas

In the US, there are reports of Gen Z switching from smartphones to dumb phones to cut their screen time. Groups that enjoy spending time without smartphones have also appeared, such as the "Luddite Club" for teenagers in New York. HMD, which makes Nokia-branded phones, said that in 2022 tens of thousands of feature phones, including flip phones, were selling every month in the US.

A collaboration edition of the US company Light's minimal phone with the rapper Kendrick Lamar (limited to 250 units) sold out on the day it was released. A major Swiss online retailer was also reported to have seen sales of traditional mobile phones rise 66% year on year in April to June 2024 (reports also said the buyers were mainly older people).

Behind this is a common frustration: "I want to use my smartphone less, but settings and apps don't make it stick" (for people who keep turning off their own Screen Time). The idea is that with a phone that never had those features, you don't have to rely on willpower.

Three types you can choose in Japan

(1) A carrier's 4G feature phone (garaho)

A button phone you can buy from Docomo, au or SoftBank. These are built for Japanese networks and have giteki certification, so they are the safest to use. There are also cheap plans at around 1,500 yen a month.

On the other hand, many models cannot run maps or transit apps, or make them awkward, and depending on the model LINE (Japan's most-used messaging app) may not work either. A detailed comparison of models and prices is in can you still use a feature phone in 2026?

(2) A flip-style smartphone

These look like flip phones on the outside but are Android smartphones inside. For example, Mode1's "RETRO II" is a flip-style smartphone running Android 13. It went on sale in 2023 for 29,800 yen and was also sold by the budget carrier mineo.

Because you can install apps, its strength is that LINE and maps work. But social media and video apps can be installed too, so if your goal is to use a smartphone less, you have to decide for yourself not to install them.

(3) An overseas minimal phone

Models such as the Light Phone III (US), Mudita Kompakt (Poland) and Punkt MC02 (Switzerland) are popular for their design and their "doesn't eat your time" philosophy. Their design choices are clear: some use a black-and-white e-paper screen, some have no app store.

However, there is a big caveat for using them in Japan. We explain it next.

Before buying an overseas model: the giteki caveat

In principle, any device that transmits radio signals in Japan needs a "giteki mark" showing it meets Japanese standards. The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) warns that using radio equipment without the giteki mark breaks the Radio Act and can lead to imprisonment of up to one year (called "penal servitude" until May 2025) or a fine of up to 1 million yen.

  • Most minimal phones sold overseas do not have Japanese giteki certification (as of October 2026, the Light Phone III has giteki certification but is not officially sold in Japan. We could not confirm either giteki certification or official sales in Japan for the Mudita Kompakt or Punkt. A model-by-model comparison is in comparing minimal phones you can use in Japan)
  • MIC's "special system for experiments using equipment without technical conformity certification" (which lets you use a device for up to 180 days after filing a notice) is meant for experiments and testing, and covers things like Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Mobile network signals (such as LTE) are not covered, so it is no basis for using one as your everyday phone
  • If you buy an overseas model, first check MIC's giteki search to see whether that model is registered

Besides the legal problem, a model not built for Japanese networks can also be inconvenient: it may struggle to get a signal, and Earthquake Early Warnings may not reach it.

How to choose the right minimal phone for you

Start by answering these three questions.

  1. Are calls and SMS enough? If so, (1) a 4G feature phone is the cheapest and most reliable
  2. Do you need maps and transit? If you go out a lot, this is the deciding point. Most of type (1) make them awkward
  3. Do you need LINE? If so, with (1) you need to choose the model carefully. (2) can run it, but other apps can be installed too

FAQ

Can you use a dumb phone in Japan?

A 4G feature phone (button phone) sold by Docomo, au or SoftBank works as is. For a dumb phone sold overseas, first check whether it has Japanese giteki certification. Transmitting with a model that lacks giteki may break the Radio Act.

Are there dumb phones that work on Docomo?

The 4G feature phones sold in Docomo shops and its online store work on Docomo's network. If you use an overseas model with a Docomo SIM, you need to check not only giteki but also whether it supports Docomo's 4G (LTE) frequencies.

Where do I check for the giteki mark?

It is shown in the device's settings (under items such as "Certification" or "Regulatory information") or on a label on the device or its box. You can also look up a model by name using MIC's search for equipment with technical conformity certification.

Can you use a Light Phone in Japan?

The Light Phone III has Japanese giteki certification (as of October 2026). However, it is not officially sold in Japan, so you would have to order it from overseas. For details, see comparing minimal phones you can use in Japan.
