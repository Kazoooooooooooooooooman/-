Japan's live entertainment market reaches 856.4 billion yen, a record for the third year in a row

November 5, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

People are coming back to concerts and the theater. According to Pia Research Institute, Japan's live entertainment market was 856.4 billion yen in 2025. That is up 12.6% from the year before, and a record high for the third year in a row. There were about 100,000 performances, back to the level seen before the COVID-19 pandemic. More people are paying for the joy of seeing something in person rather than through a screen.

The short version

  • The combined market for music concerts and stage performances was 856.4 billion yen, a record for the third year running
  • There were about 100,000 performances, back to pre-pandemic levels
  • Shows at large venues such as arenas and domes lifted the total

Market size over time

The market grew by about 96 billion yen in a year. Spending per attendee also rose 4.5% from the year before, to 9,286 yen.

What lifted the market

  • Shows at large venues such as arenas and domes
  • Crowds gathering for popular artists and productions
  • The number of performances recovering to about 100,000

Challenges ahead

Pia Research Institute points to a shortage of stage technicians and aging theaters and halls as challenges. It says the key to further growth is raising the value of the performances themselves.

Points to keep in mind about these figures

  • Pia Research Institute's figures are estimates based on ticket sales. They combine music concerts with stage performances such as plays and musicals
  • They are calculated differently from figures published by other bodies, such as the All Japan Concert & Live Entertainment Promoters Conference
