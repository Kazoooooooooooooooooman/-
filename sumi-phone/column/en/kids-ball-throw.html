In 60 years, the ten-year-old ball throw fell from 30.4 m to 21.9 m. Side steps, on the other hand, improved

November 3, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

In fiscal 1964, the year of the Tokyo Olympics, 10-year-old boys threw a softball an average of 30.4 m. Their children's generation (fiscal 1993) threw 27.1 m, and their grandchildren's generation (fiscal 2023) throws 21.9 m. That is more than 8 m shorter in 60 years. But not every kind of fitness has declined. Today's children are the best of the three generations at side steps. This summary is based on the Japan Sports Agency's three-generation comparison.

Ages are as of fiscal 2023. People who were 10 in fiscal 1964 are now 69, and people who were 10 in fiscal 1993 are now 40. Side steps count how many times a child can step side to side across lines within a set time.

What fell and what improved

  • Fell: the ball throw. Boys throw about 5 m shorter than in fiscal 1993, and girls about 2 m shorter
  • Improved: side steps. Both boys and girls do 7 to 9 more than in fiscal 1964
  • Came back down: grip strength and the 50 m dash. These were best in fiscal 1993 and are now about the same as in fiscal 1964

Even though children's bodies have grown (today's 10-year-olds are the tallest of the three generations), their ability to throw a ball far has weakened. Their ability to move quickly has improved, so rather than saying "all fitness has declined", you could say the movements children are good at have changed.

Things to keep in mind about the numbers

  • The Japan Sports Agency used 60 years of data to compare 10-year-olds at three points in time
  • This survey does not say why ball throws got shorter. Several factors are possible, such as changes in the places and time children have to play outside, and fewer chances to play with balls
  • Exercise habits and screen time of elementary and junior high school students are also covered every year in the National Survey on Physical Fitness, Motor Abilities and Exercise Habits

FAQ

Are children less fit than they used to be?

It depends on the test. The ten-year-old ball throw has fallen sharply over 60 years, but side steps have improved. Grip strength and the 50 m dash are lower than in the parents' generation and about the same as in the grandparents' generation.

How can a child throw farther?

Start by giving them more chances to throw a ball, such as playing catch in the park. They can also learn throwing technique from school teachers or local sports classes.

Is phone time related to fitness?

This three-generation comparison did not look at the link with smartphones. We cover the link between screen time and exercise in data on children's screen time.

Summary

  • For side steps, today's 10-year-olds score highest, for both boys and girls
  • What fell is "throwing power". The ability to move quickly has improved
