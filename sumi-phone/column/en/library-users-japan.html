More libraries, fewer loans: how public library lending in Japan has changed

November 7, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

A library is a place where you can borrow books for free and spend time in quiet. According to Japan Library Association statistics, Japan has 3,318 public libraries, and 57,065,000 registered users. In fiscal 2024 (Japan's fiscal year runs April–March), libraries lent 607,465,000 books and other items to individuals. But loans are down about 15% from 716.18 million in fiscal 2010. This summary is based on "Libraries in Japan: Statistics and Directory 2025".

Loans to individuals over time

Loans to individuals grew strongly through the 1990s and 2000s. Among the years shown in these statistics, the highest is 716,181,000 items in fiscal 2010. After that, loans fell little by little, dropping to 545,343,000 in pandemic-hit fiscal 2020. They recovered to 632,676,000 in fiscal 2022, but were 607,465,000 in fiscal 2024.

Loans include audiovisual materials such as CDs and DVDs as well as books.

The number of libraries rose by about 1,000, from 2,363 in the 1996 edition to 3,318 in the 2025 edition. Since the late 2010s it has stayed roughly flat at around 3,300. Registered users were highest, among the years shown, at 58,041,000 in the 2020 edition, and have settled at around 57 million.

There are more libraries and more registered users, yet fewer loans. This suggests that each person is borrowing from the library less often.

Fewer people work in libraries, too

Full-time library staff fell from 15,289 in the 1996 edition to 9,294 in the 2025 edition. Even as the number of libraries grew, full-time staff fell by about 40%.

The link with reading time

More than 60% of people do not read a single book in a month (see if you can no longer read books). The fall in library loans seems to go hand in hand with less time spent reading. A library is one of the public places where you can put your phone down and pick up a book or a newspaper.

Notes on the numbers

  • These figures come from "Libraries in Japan: Statistics and Directory", compiled every year by the Japan Library Association, and include private libraries
  • Loans are for the fiscal year before each edition (the 2025 edition shows fiscal 2024)
  • Registered users are the sum of each library's count, so people registered at several libraries are counted more than once
  • An errata list for some libraries' figures was issued after the 2025 edition was published. The effect on the national totals is likely small
