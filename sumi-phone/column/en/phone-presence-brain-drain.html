Does just having your phone on the desk reduce your brainpower? What happened after the "brain drain" study

November 8, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

When you study, do you leave your phone face down on the desk? The "brain drain" study, published in the US in 2017, reported that just having your phone in front of you reduces the mental capacity you have available for thinking, and it made headlines around the world. But some follow-up studies have not found the same result. Here is the original study and the follow-up results, side by side.

The 2017 "brain drain" study

A team including researchers from the University of Texas at Austin asked college students to put their own phones in one of three places and then take tests. Where each student put the phone was decided at random.

  • On the desk (face down in Experiment 1)
  • In a pocket or bag
  • In another room

Every group turned off sound and vibration. In Experiment 1 (520 people), students took a test of holding information in mind while doing math (working memory) and a test of spotting patterns in shapes (reasoning). Students who left their phones in another room did better than students whose phones were on the desk. Pocket or bag fell in between.

In Experiment 2 (275 people), the researchers also split students by whether their phones were on or off. Again, the other-room group did better on the memory test than the desk group, and whether the phone was on or off made no difference. The more dependent students were on their phones, the bigger the gap.

Interestingly, many students said the phone's location had not affected their performance. In Experiment 1, 75.9% said so.

On the other hand, even in the original study, phone location made no difference on a test of sustained attention (pressing a button in response to cues).

The replications that did not find it

In psychology, it matters a lot whether a result from one study holds up when another team repeats it the same way. This is called replication. Brain drain has been put to that test.

  • The Swiss study (2020): compared having a phone on the desk with not having it there. Overall, there was no difference on a short-term memory test or on a test of remembering to do something later (prospective memory). There were 302 participants (according to a British Psychological Society write-up). Only among people with low dependence on their phones did prospective memory scores come out better without the phone
  • The Canadian study (2022): repeated the original Experiment 2 with the same tests and conditions, with the plan registered in advance. Analyzing 383 people, it found no difference by phone location on either the memory test or the attention test

The Canadian team concluded that the mere presence of one's smartphone may not be enough to affect performance.

Pooling 22 studies

In 2023, a team at the University of Augsburg in Germany pooled 22 studies (43 results) on how using a phone, or just having one nearby, affects thinking (a meta-analysis). Overall, they found a negative effect, but the effect size was −0.14, which is small.

  • By area, only "memory" showed a clear effect. "Attention" and "general cognitive performance" did not
  • By region, only studies from Asia showed a clear effect. Studies from North America showed almost none (−0.03)
  • Results varied widely between studies, and the analysis included both studies of phone use and studies of phones simply being present

So "a phone on the desk dulls your mind" is not backed by strong enough evidence to state flatly. But we cannot say there is no effect at all, either, and research continues.

FAQ

Does having a phone nearby lower your concentration?

In the 2017 study, students who left their phones in another room did better on tests. But follow-up studies in 2020 and 2022 did not find the same result, and a 2023 meta-analysis found only a small effect. For now, there is no clear conclusion.

Is it enough to put the phone face down or turn it off?

In the 2017 study, whether the phone was on or off made no difference. The researchers suggested that turning a phone face down or switching it off may not help. But since the original result itself has not been replicated, it should be taken with caution.

Summary

  • In the 2017 study, leaving the phone in another room led to better scores on memory and reasoning tests
  • Follow-up studies in 2020 and 2022 found no difference by phone location
  • A meta-analysis of 22 studies found that any negative effect is small, and results vary by study
