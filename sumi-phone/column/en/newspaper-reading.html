How many people read newspapers? Reading rates, reading time and differences by age

November 1, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

In the fiscal 2025 survey, 21.3% of people read a newspaper on a weekday. That is one in five. In fiscal 2020 the figure was 31.2%, so it fell by about 10 points in five years. Reading time has also shrunk, but as a "trustworthy medium", newspapers are rated highest of all. This summary is based on a survey by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC).

The short version

  • 21.3% of people read a newspaper on weekdays, and 18.8% on days off
  • Daily reading time is 8.8 minutes on weekdays. For people in their teens to 30s, it is under 1 minute
  • Newspapers score 58.8% for trust as a medium, higher than TV or the internet

Share of people reading newspapers over time

This is the share of people who read a newspaper at all on the survey day. In fiscal 2025 it rose slightly from the year before.

Reading time by age

These averages include people who did not read at all. The weekday average across all ages fell from 13.1 minutes in fiscal 2020 to 8.8 minutes.

Still the most trusted

  • For "trust as a medium", newspapers score 58.8%, the highest of all media
  • By age, TV ranks highest among people in their teens, 20s and 60s, and newspapers rank highest in the other age groups
  • 37.6% use printed newspapers as a way to read the news (Where do people read the news?)

Reading the news without a smartphone

  • Read just the front page of the morning paper: even skimming the headlines tells you the day's big stories
  • Read at a library or café: many places keep newspapers, and it gives you a reason to go out
  • Paper sends no notifications: you won't drift off to another app halfway through

Summary

  • 21.3% of people read a newspaper on weekdays, down about 10 points in five years
  • Reading time is 8.8 minutes on weekdays. Younger people hardly read at all
  • At 58.8%, newspapers rank highest for trust among media
