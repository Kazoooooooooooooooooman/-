How many children become crime victims through social media? Numbers, school levels and how it starts

October 30, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

In 2025, 1,566 children (under 18) became victims of crimes committed by people they had never met in person and got to know through social media. That is 80 more than the year before. Almost half of the victims were junior high school students, and the number of elementary school victims keeps rising, reaching 167. This summary is based on National Police Agency statistics.

The short version

  • 1,566 children became victims through social media (1,486 the year before)
  • 758 junior high students (48.4%), 579 high school students (37.0%) and 167 elementary school students (10.7%)
  • Of the children whose filtering status was known, about 90% (688 of 771) were not using it

The trend over time

The peak was 2,082 in 2019. The number then fell, but it rose again in 2025. "Social media" here also includes online games.

By school level

Elementary school victims rose from 43 in 2016 to 167, about four times as many. As children start getting phones younger, the harm is spreading to younger ages too.

What kinds of crimes

  • Violations of local youth protection ordinances: 300
  • Child pornography: 426; child prostitution: 128
  • Non-consensual sexual intercourse: 329; non-consensual indecent acts: 185; kidnapping and abduction: 79

The definitions of non-consensual sexual intercourse and non-consensual indecent acts changed with a legal revision in July 2023, so these cannot be compared directly with earlier years.

The posts that started it

In 74.2% of cases (1,162 children), the first post came from the child who became the victim. The most common kinds of posts among those 1,162 were:

  • Profile only: 18.1%
  • Everyday life: 14.9%
  • Offers of "compensated dating" (enjo kosai): 12.9%
  • Hobbies and things they like: 11.3%
  • Looking for friends: 11.1%

It is not only risky-looking posts. Strangers also make contact through profiles and posts about everyday life.

Devices and filtering

  • Of the 1,566 victims, 1,529 (97.6%) accessed social media from a smartphone
  • Of the 771 whose filtering status was known, 688 (89.2%) were not using filtering

What families can do

  • Turn on filtering: In Japan, mobile carriers are required to offer filtering when a phone contract is for someone under 18. The most important thing is that parents do not switch it off
  • Check features that connect to strangers: Also review in-game chat and the settings for who can send direct messages
  • Agree to tell an adult right away if someone asks to meet: The cases counted here involve people the child had never met and got to know online. Some harm can be prevented by stepping in before a meeting happens

For how to think about what age to give a child a phone, see what age should kids get a smartphone?

Summary

  • 1,566 children became victims through social media, up from the year before
  • About half were junior high students. Elementary school victims rose about fourfold in ten years
  • About 90% of the victims were not using filtering
