For people who can no longer read books: with 60% reading none in a month, how to get your reading back

October 25, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

You used to read books on the train, but now you find yourself looking at your phone. Even when you open a book, you stop after a few pages. You are not the only one who feels this way. In a survey by the Agency for Cultural Affairs, more than 60% of people did not read a single book in a month. Using the data, we look at what is taking away our reading time and how to get it back.

In this article

  1. 62.6% do not read a single book in a month
  2. The top reason for reading less: smartphones
  3. Five ways to get reading back
  4. Summary

The short version

  • 62.6% of people do not read a single book in a month (Agency for Cultural Affairs, survey conducted March 2024). In earlier surveys the figure was in the high 40s
  • The top reason for reading less is "smartphones and other devices take up my time" (43.6%)
  • Among 16- to 19-year-olds who read less, 70.9% gave this reason
  • Creating "time when your phone is out of reach" works better than willpower

62.6% do not read a single book in a month

Every year, the Agency for Cultural Affairs carries out the Public Opinion Survey on the Japanese Language among people aged 16 and over across Japan. In the March 2024 survey (3,559 respondents), people were asked, "Roughly how many books do you read a month?" (including e-books, but excluding magazines and manga). These are the results.

In past surveys, "none" was in the high 40s. The Agency for Cultural Affairs cautions that the figures cannot be compared directly because the survey method changed, but the gap is still large.

The top reason for reading less: smartphones

In the same survey, 69.1% of people said they read less than they used to. These are the reasons they gave (up to two answers).

By age group, the younger people were, the more likely they were to blame devices: in the 60% range for people in their 20s and 30s, and 70.9% among 16- to 19-year-olds. It is not that people have come to dislike books. The time they would have spent reading has moved to the screen.

Five ways to get reading back

  1. Put a book in "time when your phone is out of reach": the commuter train, the bath, the 30 minutes before bed. Put your phone deep in your bag and hold a book instead. What you end up doing depends less on whether you feel like reading than on what is in your hand (how to use your commute)
  2. Start with short books: stopping after a few pages is not because your concentration has declined, but because you are used to the speed of your phone. Ease back in with books in short pieces, such as short story collections or essays
  3. Choose paper books: e-books are fine too, but reading on your phone puts notifications and other apps right next to the page. Paper suits your first book
  4. Go to a bookshop or library: a book you chose yourself from the shelf, rather than from a list of recommendations, makes you want to read it to the end. Going there is also a reason to get out of the house
  5. Write one line when you finish: note the day you finished and one line that stayed with you. A record that keeps growing nudges you toward the next book

Summary

  • 62.6% of people do not read a single book in a month
  • The top reason for reading less is "smartphones and other devices take up my time" (43.6%). The younger the person, the more common it is
  • Instead of relying on willpower, put a book in the time when your phone is out of reach
  • Something to do today: put a book in your bag for tomorrow's commute
