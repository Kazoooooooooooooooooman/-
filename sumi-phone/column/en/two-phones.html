How to carry two phones and leave your smartphone at home

October 21, 2026 (updated October 25, 2026) · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

Even if giving up your smartphone is hard, you can stop carrying it around. Take a simple phone with just calls, maps and a camera when you go out, and leave the smartphone at home. That is what we mean by "two phones." The three things people get stuck on are the phone number, the cost and LINE (Japan's most-used messaging app). This article explains how to decide each one, and how to be ready to stay in touch and pay when you go out without a smartphone.

The short version

  • A second phone can cost as little as 290 yen a month. The easiest option is a separate number for the second phone
  • Calls to your smartphone's number can be forwarded to the phone you carry outside (no monthly fee)
  • Using the same number on two devices only works with services for smartwatches. It cannot be done with two smartphones
  • On Android, LINE can be used on a second phone as a sub-device. An iPhone cannot be a sub-device

How many people carry two phones?

In a 2024 survey by the NTT DOCOMO Mobile Society Research Institute, 14% of people had two or more mobile phones. The figure has hovered around 10% since about 2012, with little change (see the chart below). "Smartphone plus smartphone" and "smartphone plus tablet" make up about 90% of the combinations.

The two-phone approach in this article is a little different. The goal is not to use two devices for different purposes, but to not carry a smartphone while you are out.

Choosing a number for the second phone

A. Get a separate number for the second phone (the easiest)

Sign up for a new, cheap plan for the second phone. Tell your family and friends it is your number for when you are out.

Prices are from each company's official page as of October 2026. The second phone only needs calls and a little map use, so 1 to 3 GB is enough in most cases.

B. Forward calls from your smartphone's number to the phone you carry

Calls to your smartphone's number can be forwarded to the phone you take with you.

  • DOCOMO "Call Forwarding Service": no monthly fee (sign-up required; not available on ahamo)
  • au "Call Forwarding Service": no monthly fee (no sign-up needed)
  • SoftBank "Call Forwarding": no monthly fee (no sign-up needed)

When a call is forwarded, you pay the call charge from your smartphone to the forwarding number. You can turn forwarding on before you go out and off when you get home.

C. Use the same number on two devices (smartwatches only)

DOCOMO's "One Number Service" (550 yen a month) and au's "Number Share" (385 yen a month) let you use the same phone number as your smartphone on an Apple Watch or similar. However, the number can only be shared with smartwatches and other dedicated devices. There is no consumer service for using the same number on two smartphones.

What to do about LINE

LINE is basically designed for one account on one main device. Since March 2025, you can use an Android smartphone as a "sub-device," so the same account works on a second phone too.

  • You can add up to one Android device, one computer and one iPad as sub-devices
  • An iPhone cannot be a sub-device
  • Some actions, such as transferring the account or backing up chat history, are not available on a sub-device

Deciding not to use LINE on the phone you carry outside is another option. While you are out, calls and the standard messaging app that sends texts to phone numbers are enough to stay in touch (how to stay in touch without LINE). Many people are happy to check LINE all at once on their smartphone when they get home.

Getting ready to go out without a smartphone

Staying in touch

In a November 2024 survey by the Mobile Society Research Institute, only 49% of people remembered the cell phone number of even one family member they live with. More than half of people cannot call their family without a smartphone.

  • Save your family's numbers in the phone you carry outside. Write them on paper and keep it in your wallet too
  • There are about 96,000 public phones across Japan (as of the end of March 2025). Many remain near stations and government offices

Paying

If you use smartphone payments, this may be your biggest worry. In JCB's fiscal 2025 survey, 47% of people used contactless card payments. At convenience stores and supermarkets, you can usually pay with a contactless card or a transit IC card (rechargeable cards like Suica and PASMO).

Getting around

You can ride trains and buses with a transit IC card. Lines that accept a tap of a credit card are also spreading across the country. When you go somewhere new, it helps to pick a phone with maps and transit directions.

How to start carrying two phones

  1. Choose your second phone: a 4G feature phone if you only need calls and SMS, or a minimal phone if you also need maps and transit directions (what is a minimal phone?, can you still use a flip phone in 2026?)
  2. Choose a plan: for a separate number (A), sign up for a cheap plan. If you often get calls on your current number, also use forwarding (B)
  3. Tell your family and the people you're in touch with most: say, "When I'm out, call this number"
  4. Try it close to home: at first, only for walks and shopping in your neighborhood. Once you are used to it, gradually spend longer out

Summary

  • Carrying two phones is a way to not carry a smartphone while you are out
  • A separate number for the second phone is easiest. Cheap plans start from 290 yen a month
  • Calls to your smartphone's number can be forwarded to the phone you carry outside, for free
  • On Android, LINE can be used as a sub-device. Deciding not to use it is another option
  • With a written copy of your family's numbers, plus a contactless card and a transit IC card, you will rarely have trouble without a smartphone
