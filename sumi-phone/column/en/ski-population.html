In 20 years, the share of people in Japan who ski or snowboard has fallen to less than a third

November 14, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

How many people in Japan still ski or snowboard? In the Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC), the share of people aged 10 and over who skied or snowboarded in the past year fell from 10.6% in 2001 to 3.2% in 2021. That is less than a third in 20 years. This summary draws on government statistics and figures from the Leisure White Paper.

Share of people who skied or snowboarded (2001–2021)

The Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities is a large survey that MIC runs every five years. It asks whether people skied or snowboarded in their free time during the past year. The share was almost flat between 2011 and 2016, then fell to 3.2% in 2021. Applied to the estimated population aged 10 and over in 2021 (112.46 million), that is about 3.6 million people.

Note, however, that the 2021 survey covers the year from October 2020 to October 2021. It includes a winter when people held back on going out and traveling because of COVID-19, so the drop may look larger than it otherwise would.

The 1990s peak

The 2001 survey includes a long-term trend for people aged 15 and over. There were 13.54 million participants (13.5%) in 1991 and 13.68 million (13.1%) in 1996, meaning roughly one in every seven or eight people aged 15 and over hit the slopes. The 1986 and 1991 figures cover skiing only and do not include snowboarding.

The Leisure White Paper published by the Japan Productivity Center also puts the peak in the 1990s. According to press reports, the white paper gives a peak skiing population of 18.6 million in 1993 (skiing only) and 7.9 million in 2012. Because the survey methods differ, these figures do not match the MIC numbers in size.

A December 2023 document from the Consumer Affairs Agency, citing the Leisure White Paper 2023, puts the 2022 skiing population at 3.1 million and the snowboarding population at 1.6 million. Some people do both, so adding the two together does not give the number of people who hit the slopes. By age (averaged over 2018–2022), skiers are mostly middle-aged and older, while snowboarding is centered on younger generations.

The 2016 Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities also shows a clear gap by age. The share who skied or snowboarded was 13.4% among 10- to 14-year-olds, 13.7% among 20- to 24-year-olds and 11.1% among 25- to 29-year-olds, but only 2.4% among 55- to 59-year-olds.

Ski resorts are seeing more visitors from abroad

A report compiled in March 2026 by a Japan Tourism Agency committee says domestic participation "has fallen sharply," while the number of foreign visitors who come to Japan for snow sports, and the money they spend, are on the rise. It points to the quality of Japan's snow and easy access to its ski resorts as reasons.

Skiing and snowboarding are outdoor sports that get you moving in the snowy mountains. For how other sports have changed, see the sports people in Japan actually do. Camping, which also peaked in the 1990s, is covered in auto-camping participation over time.

FAQ

How many people ski in Japan?

According to the Leisure White Paper 2023 (as cited by the Consumer Affairs Agency), the skiing population in 2022 was 3.1 million and the snowboarding population was 1.6 million. In MIC's Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities, 3.2% of people aged 10 and over skied or snowboarded in the year covered by the 2021 survey.

When did skiing peak in Japan?

In the 1990s. In the Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities, the highest figures for people aged 15 and over are 13.54 million in 1991 and 13.68 million in 1996. The Leisure White Paper is reported to put the peak at 18.6 million in 1993 (skiing only).

Summary

  • The share of people aged 10 and over who skied or snowboarded fell from 10.6% in 2001 to 3.2% in 2021
  • In the 1990s, more than 13 million people aged 15 and over hit the slopes
  • Fewer people in Japan ski now, but more visitors come from abroad to ski resorts
