    What is an "awe walk"? What an eight-week experiment found

    In an experiment at the University of California, San Francisco (Sturm et al., published in Emotion), 60 healthy people aged 60 to 90 took a 15-minute walk once a week for eight weeks. The group that set out to feel awe smiled more broadly in their photos and felt less daily distress. Here is how the study worked, what it found and its limits.

    Forest bathing: how much does the research really show?

    A look at forest bathing (shinrin-yoku) through papers that combine many studies (systematic reviews and meta-analyses). An analysis of 20 trials and 732 people found systolic blood pressure 3.15 mmHg lower on average in forests, and salivary cortisol was also lower. But most studies are small, one analysis limited to randomized controlled trials found no clear blood pressure difference, and another rated the certainty of the evidence as very low.
