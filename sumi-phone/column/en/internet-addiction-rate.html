How many people show signs of internet addiction? Rates by age, and the 8 questions used

October 29, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

You can't stop thinking about the internet. You try to quit, but you can't. Among people aged 13 to 79, 6.1% showed this kind of "tendency toward internet addiction." The rate was 21.4% among teens and 17.0% among people in their 20s, so the younger the age group, the higher it is. This summary is based on a survey by Japan's Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) for fiscal 2025 (Japan's fiscal year runs April to March).

The short version

  • 6.1% of all respondents answered "yes" to five or more of the eight questions (men 5.7%, women 6.4%)
  • Teens 21.4%, 20s 17.0%. From the 30s up, about 1–7%
  • Compared with the year before, the rate for teens fell from 23.6% to 21.4%, while the rate for the 20s rose from 11.0% to 17.0%

Rates by age

That's one in five teens and one in six people in their 20s. From the 30s up, even the highest group, people in their 40s, is at 6.7%.

Change from the year before

Fiscal 2024Fiscal 2025
Teens23.6%21.4%
20s11.0%17.0%

The rate for people in their 20s rose 6 points in one year. The survey asks different people each year, so it's important not to judge from a single year's movement and to look at several years in a row.

The eight questions used

This survey uses the "Young 8-item criteria." The questions are based on Kimberly Young's book Caught in the Net (1998) and were adapted with the Hashimoto Laboratory (at the time) of the Interfaculty Initiative in Information Studies at the University of Tokyo. Respondents answer "yes" or "no" to the following eight:

  1. I think about the internet even when I'm not using it
  2. I can't feel satisfied unless I spend more and more time online
  3. When I try to control how long I spend online, I can't manage it
  4. When I try to cut back on the internet, I feel restless or irritable
  5. I end up using the internet for longer than I originally planned
  6. Because of the internet, my relationships with family and friends have suffered, or I'm close to neglecting work, study and so on
  7. I have lied to family or friends about how long I spend online or how absorbed I am in it
  8. I use the internet to escape from reality or to lift a low mood

Anyone who answers "yes" to five or more is counted as showing a "tendency toward internet addiction." This is a cut-off for the survey, not a medical diagnosis.

Things to keep in mind when reading the numbers

  • MIC notes that when comparing with other surveys, you need to keep in mind that it uses this criterion and how it was carried out (a paper questionnaire left with and collected from people aged 13 to 79 at home)
  • Other surveys looking at "smartphone addiction" sometimes produce much higher rates. That's because their questions and cut-offs are different

If you want to check where you stand, how to check for yourself whether you have smartphone addiction also covers tests used by medical institutions and where to get help.

Summary

  • 6.1% of respondents overall show a tendency toward internet addiction
  • Rates are high among young people: 21.4% of teens and 17.0% of people in their 20s
  • The rate for people in their 20s rose from 11.0% the year before to 17.0%
