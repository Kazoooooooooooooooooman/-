How long do people in Japan use their smartphones? The latest data by age group

October 15, 2026 (updated November 1, 2026) · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

How much time do people in Japan spend on their smartphones each day? In the latest surveys, adults spend about two and a half hours a day on smartphones alone, and high school students spend over six hours on weekdays on the internet as a whole. Every survey shows the time getting longer year by year. In this article, we lay out surveys by public bodies and research institutes, making clear what each number actually counts.

The short version

  • Adults (Tokyo, ages 15–69) spend about 165 minutes a day on smartphones and mobile phones, the longest on record (Hakuhodo, 2025)
  • The share of people who use the internet on their smartphones for four hours or more a day in their private time almost doubled in three years, from 12% to 23% (Mobile Society Research Institute, 2026)
  • High school students use the internet for about 6 hours 44 minutes on weekdays (Children and Families Agency, fiscal 2025 preliminary results)
  • 62.5% of people feel they are "dependent" on their smartphones (MMD Labo, 2024)

Adults: about two and a half hours a day

About 165 minutes a day on smartphones and mobile phones

In the "Media Fixed-Point Survey 2025" by the Hakuhodo DY Media Partners Media Environment Institute, people aged 15–69 in the Tokyo area spent 165.1 minutes a day (weekly average) on mobile phones and smartphones. That is more than 40 minutes longer than TV (122.1 minutes), and the longest figure since the survey began.

The total time spent on all media in a day is 440.0 minutes. Smartphones account for nearly 40% of that.

People using it four hours or more almost doubled in three years

In a January 2026 survey of people aged 15–79 across Japan by the NTT Docomo Mobile Society Research Institute, 23% used the internet on their smartphones for four hours or more a day in their private time. In 2023 the figure was 12%, so it almost doubled in three years. The estimated average time also rose, from 111.2 minutes to 153.9 minutes. The increase was not only among young people: it rose by about 50 minutes among people in their 60s and 70s too.

By age group: people in their 20s use it the most

The MIC Institute for Information and Communications Policy's survey gives internet use time by age group, across the internet as a whole, including computers and other devices. In the fiscal 2025 survey, the average for all ages was 183.9 minutes on weekdays and 192.9 minutes on days off. In every age group, use from smartphones and mobile phones was the longest.

People in their 20s spend more than four hours even on weekdays. On weekdays, only people in their 60s and 70s watched more TV than they used the internet (fiscal 2025).

School students: high schoolers spend about 6 hours 44 minutes on weekdays

In the Children and Families Agency's "Survey on the Internet Usage Environment of Young People" (November–December 2025, ages 10–17), average weekday internet use across all respondents was about 5 hours 27 minutes. That is about 25 minutes more than the previous year.

In hours and minutes, that is about 3 hours 54 minutes for elementary school students (age 10 and over), about 5 hours 24 minutes for junior high students and about 6 hours 44 minutes for high school students.

These figures are totals across seven types of device, including smartphones, computers, game consoles and TVs, and include use for studying. Even so, high school students' 6 hours 44 minutes on weekdays is almost as long as the time they spend at school. The share using a smartphone of their own was 95.4% for junior high students and 99.1% for high school students.

Six in ten feel "dependent"

In a 2024 survey by MMD Labo of 10,000 smartphone owners aged 18–69, 23.4% said they were "very dependent" and 39.1% "somewhat dependent," so 62.5% in total felt they were dependent on their smartphones. 81.1% said they would be "in trouble" without one. By age group, the figures were especially high among teens and people in their 20s.

It is not just that people use their phones for a long time: many people feel for themselves that it is "too long." And yet they have not managed to cut back. That is the picture in Japan today.

A caution when reading the numbers

Smartphone use time is counted in completely different ways in different surveys. You cannot line them up and compare them, or add them together.

How to check your own phone time

Before comparing yourself with the averages, take a look at your own numbers. In most cases, they are longer than you expected.

  • iPhone: Settings → Screen Time → See All App & Website Activity. It shows your daily and weekly time and the apps you used most.
  • Android: Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls. It shows today's usage time and time per app (menu names vary a little by model).

165 minutes a day comes to about 1,000 hours a year, or about 42 days. If you feel you want to cut back, see also how to stop reaching for your phone while studying.

FAQ

How long do people in Japan use their smartphones on average?

Adults (Tokyo, ages 15–69) spend about 165 minutes (about 2 hours 45 minutes) a day on smartphones and mobile phones (Hakuhodo DY Media Partners, "Media Fixed-Point Survey 2025").

How many hours a day do people in Japan spend on the internet on average?

Across all ages, it is 183.9 minutes (about 3 hours 4 minutes) on weekdays and 192.9 minutes (about 3 hours 13 minutes) on days off. This includes devices other than smartphones (MIC, fiscal 2025).

Which age group uses it the most?

People in their 20s. Their internet use is 266.9 minutes (about 4 hours 27 minutes) on weekdays and 324.5 minutes (about 5 hours 25 minutes) on days off (MIC, fiscal 2025).

How much do high school students use it?

Their weekday internet use is about 6 hours 44 minutes. This includes use for studying and use from game consoles and TVs (Children and Families Agency, fiscal 2025 preliminary results).

Where can I see my own usage time?

On iPhone, go to Settings → Screen Time. On Android, go to Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls.

Summary

  • Adults spend about 165 minutes a day on their phones (Tokyo, ages 15–69, 2025), and it grows every year
  • People using the internet on their smartphones for four hours or more in their private time rose from 12% to 23% in three years
  • People in their 20s use the internet for about 4 hours 27 minutes on weekdays, and high school students for about 6 hours 44 minutes
  • More than 60% feel "dependent"
  • Each survey counts differently, so always read the numbers together with their conditions
