How children's eyesight is declining: ten years of students with uncorrected vision below 1.0

October 29, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

In fiscal 2025, 36.07% of elementary school students, 59.35% of junior high students and 71.51% of high school students in Japan had uncorrected eyesight (visual acuity without glasses or contacts) below 1.0, roughly 20/20 on the decimal scale used in Japan. Among high school students, that is 7 in 10. Based on the "School Health Statistics" from the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT), here is how things have changed since fiscal 2015.

The short version

  • Uncorrected vision below 1.0: 36.07% in elementary school, 59.35% in junior high, 71.51% in high school (fiscal 2025)
  • Compared with fiscal 2015, that is up about 5 points in elementary school, about 5 points in junior high and about 8 points in high school
  • Compared with fiscal 2024, elementary and junior high fell slightly, and high school rose slightly

Fiscal 2025: the higher the school level, the more students below 1.0

The School Health Statistics are MEXT's tally of the results of school health checkups (held from April 1 to June 30 in fiscal 2025; Japan's fiscal and school year runs April to March). For health status, the survey covers a sample of 25.4% (about 3.14 million) of all young children and students nationwide.

In kindergarten it is about 1 in 4, but it passes half in junior high and tops 70% in high school.

The trend over ten years

FY2015FY2019FY2024FY2025
Elementary school30.97%34.57%36.84%36.07%
Junior high school54.05%57.47%60.61%59.35%
High school63.79%67.64%71.06%71.51%

MEXT notes that the figures for fiscal 2020 to 2023 include data collected at different times of year, so they cannot be compared with other years. For that reason, they are left out of this table.

Changes since fiscal 2024

  • Kindergarten: 26.53% → 23.90% (down)
  • Elementary school: 36.84% → 36.07% (down)
  • Junior high school: 60.61% → 59.35% (down)
  • High school: 71.06% → 71.51% (up)

A single year's movement is not enough to say things have "gotten better" or "gotten worse." Over the long term, every school level is higher than it was ten years ago.

What to keep in mind when reading the numbers

  • These figures measure how well children can see (visual acuity), not whether they are nearsighted
  • The statistics do not look at any link with time spent on phones
  • Because the survey uses a sample, the numbers can differ slightly from other statistics

Summary

  • In fiscal 2025, the share with uncorrected vision below 1.0 was 36.07% in elementary school, 59.35% in junior high and 71.51% in high school
  • Every school level is about 5 to 8 points higher than ten years ago
  • Fiscal 2020 to 2023 cannot be compared directly with other years
