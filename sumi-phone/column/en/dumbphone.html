What is a dumbphone? Its meaning, why it is catching on abroad, and models you can use in Japan

October 23, 2026 (updated October 25, 2026) · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

A dumbphone is a mobile phone built around calls and SMS, without apps or the internet. The name deliberately calls it a "not-smart phone," in contrast to the smartphone. In the US, young people who want to spend less time on their phones have been reported switching to dumbphones; in Switzerland, it is mainly older people. This article covers what the word means and where it comes from, why dumbphones have spread abroad, how they differ from garakei (Japan's traditional flip phones) and minimal phones, and what to watch out for when using one in Japan.

What is a dumbphone? Meaning and origin

The English dictionary Dictionary.com describes a dumbphone as a mobile phone that cannot run apps or connect to the internet, used for calls and text messages. The first recorded use dates from around 2005–2010.

Before smartphones spread, a mobile phone was just a "mobile phone." Once smartphones ("smart") became the norm, people started using the opposite word ("dumb") to tell the older phones apart.

In English, similar phones are also called "feature phones." The CNBC article introduced below uses this term for old-style flip and slider phones with added features such as GPS and tethering.

Why dumbphones are catching on abroad

US: Gen Z cutting down screen time

In March 2023, the US business news outlet CNBC reported that some Gen Z people who wanted to reduce their screen time were switching to dumbphones. According to HMD Global, which makes Nokia phones, sales of flip feature phones grew in the US in 2022, with tens of thousands sold each month. At the same time, HMD's global feature phone sales were falling.

In other words, dumbphones are not selling everywhere. What is happening is that, in countries where smartphones are everywhere, some people are choosing to step back from them.

In New York in 2022, teenagers who had swapped their smartphones for flip phones started a "Luddite Club," which was featured as a movement to keep social media at a distance (Business Insider Japan).

Europe: sales up 66% in Switzerland

In August 2024, the Swiss news site swissinfo.ch reported that sales of conventional mobile phones at the Swiss retailer Digitec Galaxus rose 66% in April–June 2024. It also reported, however, that the interest came mainly from older people, many of whom searched for "phones for seniors." The company thinks this is because physical buttons are easier to use than touch screens. It is not only a trend among young people: people who simply want an easy phone are choosing dumbphones too.

What lies behind it

CNBC reports that behind the spread in the US is concern about how smartphones affect mental health. In the swissinfo.ch article, a Swiss sociologist points to "interest in simpler, less intrusive habits," and describes the trend less as giving up smartphones entirely and more as using two phones for different situations. As long as social media is on your smartphone, it is right there the moment you open it, even if you restrict it in the settings (for people who keep turning off their own Screen Time limits). A dumbphone simply does not have it, so you do not have to rely on willpower.

How it differs from garakei and minimal phones

In English terms, Japan's 4G feature phones are a kind of dumbphone. "Minimal phone" usually refers to newer models within the dumbphone category that are designed specifically to have fewer features, typically with no app store. For more on minimal phones, see what is a minimal phone?

Three things to watch out for when using a dumbphone in Japan

1. For models from abroad, check giteki certification

In principle, any device that emits radio waves in Japan needs a "giteki mark," which shows it meets Japanese technical standards. The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) explains that using a radio device without the giteki mark may violate the Radio Act, in which case it can be punished by imprisonment of up to one year or a fine of up to 1 million yen. Even smartphones made for overseas markets violate the Radio Act if used in Japan without the giteki mark.

There is a special system that lets you use uncertified devices for up to 180 days if you file a notification for experiments or testing, but it covers things like Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Carrier LTE, 4G and 5G cannot be used under this procedure, so it is no reason to use a dumbphone from abroad day to day. Before you buy, check whether the model is registered on MIC's search page.

2. 3G models do not work in Japan

au's 3G ended on March 31, 2022, and SoftBank's 3G on April 15, 2024 (July 31 in Ishikawa Prefecture). The last remaining network, Docomo's 3G (FOMA), ended on March 31, 2026, so all 3G in Japan has now shut down. Used garakei that run on 3G, and cheap 3G-only phones from abroad, cannot even make calls in Japan. Only choose models that support 4G (VoLTE).

3. Decide first whether you need LINE

On models where you cannot add apps, LINE (Japan's most-used messaging app) is not available. Even on models that can run LINE, LINE's official help says support for old versions of the app (below 14.6.3 on iOS, below 14.4.6 on Android) is scheduled to end on November 4, 2026. Devices running Android 7.1.2 or earlier cannot keep using it unless the OS is updated to 8.0 or later. If you need LINE, check before you buy that it will still work after November. If you will live without LINE, decide on another way to stay in touch (how to keep in touch without LINE).

Dumbphones you can use in Japan

These are the two options you can use in Japan with confidence.

  • Carrier 4G feature phones (garaho): button phones sold by Docomo, au and SoftBank. They have giteki certification and are built for Japanese networks. The safest choice (can you still use a garakei in 2026?)
  • Light Phone III: a minimal phone from The Light Phone. It received Japanese giteki certification (construction design certification) dated April 25, 2025 (S-MAX). As of May 2025, the official website was taking preorders

There are also "flip-style smartphones," which look like feature phones but run Android inside. They can use LINE and maps, but you can also install social media on them, so strictly speaking they are not dumbphones.
