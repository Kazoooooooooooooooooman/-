What social media fatigue really is, and how to step back without deleting your accounts

October 24, 2026 (updated October 25, 2026) · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

You opened the app because you wanted to, yet you feel a little tired after closing it. Seeing friends' posts leaves you vaguely down. You feel you have to reply, and you can't relax. That is social media fatigue. But deleting your accounts feels unsettling too. This article sorts the causes of social media fatigue into three, and covers ways to keep your distance without deleting your accounts.

In this article

  1. Social media fatigue comes down to three things
  2. Cutting back makes you feel lighter (from research)

The short version

  • Social media fatigue has three causes: "comparing yourself", "feeling you have to reply" and "it never ends"
  • In an experiment, college students who cut social media to about 30 minutes a day felt less lonely and less depressed after three weeks
  • You don't have to delete your accounts. Change the time, the notifications, where the app sits, and who you follow
  • The single most effective step is removing the app from your home screen

Social media fatigue comes down to three things

  • Comparing yourself: social media lines up the best parts of other people's lives. You compare them with your own ordinary days and feel down
  • Feeling you have to reply: read receipts, likes, comments. Not responding feels rude, so you never fully relax
  • It never ends: scroll down and there is always something next. With nothing to make you stop, you keep going until you're worn out

Which of these sounds most like your own fatigue determines which fix will work. For how much other people use their phones, see average daily smartphone use in Japan.

Cutting back makes you feel lighter (from research)

Hunt and colleagues at the University of Pennsylvania in the US split 143 college students into two groups for a three-week experiment.

  • One group cut Facebook, Instagram and Snapchat to 10 minutes a day each (about 30 minutes in total)
  • The other group used them as usual

The result: compared with the group that used social media as usual, the group that cut back showed clear drops in loneliness and depression. In both groups, anxiety and the feeling of "I might be missing out" also went down. The researchers think that simply tracking and looking at their own use may have had an effect.

In other words, you don't have to quit entirely. Just cutting back can make you feel lighter.

Four ways to step back without deleting your accounts

  1. Set a time limit (for "it never ends"): decide on a daily limit and let your phone enforce it. On iPhone, use Screen Time; on Android, use the app timers in Digital Wellbeing (setting up Screen Time)
  2. Turn off notifications (for "I have to reply"): turn off notifications for likes and recommendations, and keep only messages sent directly to you by people (notifications you can turn off, and ones to keep)
  3. Take the app off your home screen (for "it never ends"): don't delete the app, just remove its icon from the home screen. That one extra step to open it cuts down how often you open it without thinking
  4. Rethink who you follow (for "comparing yourself"): unfollow or mute accounts that leave you feeling down. Many social media apps let you see less of someone without them knowing

If you still feel tired

  • Separate "looking days" from "no-looking days": some people find it easier to step away completely a few days a week than to use a little every day
  • Stop posting and just look: the fatigue of worrying about reactions drops sharply once you stop posting
  • Carry a phone without social media: if you want to get away from social media at least while you are out, see also how to choose a phone if you don't need social media or games

Summary

  • Social media fatigue has three causes: comparing, feeling you have to reply, and no end
  • In one experiment, cutting back to about 30 minutes a day reduced loneliness and depression
  • Keep your accounts, but change the time, notifications, where the app sits, and who you follow
  • Something to do today: remove the icon of the social media app you use most from your home screen
