Do you need blue light glasses? The answer from six eye-care organizations and a review of the research

October 25, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

Your eyes get tired from your phone and computer, so should you buy blue light glasses? Should your child wear them? Many people wonder about this. The short answer: research has found almost no evidence that wearing them during the day does anything. Let's look at what eye-care professional organizations and a review of the research have to say.

In this article

  1. Six eye-care organizations: "no evidence to recommend them"
  2. For adults, too, the benefit is unproven
  3. Light at night is a slightly different story

  5. Summary

The short version

  • Six organizations including the Japanese Ophthalmological Society: there is no evidence to recommend blue light glasses for children (2021)
  • A Cochrane review of 17 studies (619 people): the glasses probably do not reduce eye strain from computer work (2023)
  • Screens give off less blue light than natural light on a cloudy day or through a window
  • Avoiding bright light late at night may have some benefit

Six eye-care organizations: "no evidence to recommend them"

In April 2021, six organizations issued a joint statement: the Japanese Ophthalmological Society, the Japan Ophthalmologists Association, the Japan Myopia Society, the Japanese Association of Strabismus and Amblyopia, the Japanese Association for Pediatric Ophthalmology and the Japan Association of Certified Orthoptists. It raised scientific concerns about the push to have children wear blue light glasses. They gave four reasons.

  1. Screen light is weak: The blue light from digital screens is less than natural light on a cloudy day or through a window, and nowhere near a level that damages the retina
  2. Children need sunlight: Sunlight is good for children's mental and physical development. Too little sunlight raises the risk of nearsightedness getting worse. Blocking it with glasses could actually do harm
  3. Eye strain is not reduced: A randomized controlled trial published in a US scientific journal found no effect on eye strain
  4. No reason to wear them during the day: Before bed is one thing, but there is no evidence for deliberately wearing them in the daytime

The statement concluded that "there is no evidence to recommend blue light glasses for children, and they could even harm their development."

For adults, too, the benefit is unproven

Cochrane, an international organization that gathers and evaluates research, published a systematic review in 2023 of studies on blue light glasses in adults. It covered 17 studies from six countries, with 619 people in total.

  • Eye strain: Compared with ordinary lenses, they probably do not reduce short-term eye strain from computer work
  • Sleep: Results varied from study to study, so it is unclear
  • Protecting the retina: No studies looked at this, so nothing can be said

Some side effects such as headaches were reported, but they were mild and temporary, and are thought to come from wearing glasses at all.

Light at night is a slightly different story

The six organizations' statement does acknowledge that exposure to bright light late at night may disrupt sleep, and says that reducing blue light from the evening on may have some benefit.

But you do not necessarily need glasses for that. Dimming the screen before bed, turning on night mode, or simply not taking your phone into the bedroom all work toward the same goal (how to stop using your phone before bed).

What works instead of glasses

  1. Blink more: When you look at a screen, you blink only 5 to 7 times a minute instead of 15 (American Academy of Ophthalmology). Dryness is one cause of tired eyes
  2. Look into the distance now and then: This rests eyes that have been focused up close
  3. Match screen brightness to your surroundings: When only the screen is bright, your eyes have to work harder
  4. For children, more time playing outside: Sunlight helps protect against nearsightedness (data on phones and eyes)

Summary

  • Six eye-care organizations say there is no evidence to recommend blue light glasses for children
  • A review of 17 studies also found no effect on eye strain
  • Avoiding bright light at night may help, but you can do it without glasses
  • Something to do today: an hour before bed, dim your phone screen or leave it in another room
