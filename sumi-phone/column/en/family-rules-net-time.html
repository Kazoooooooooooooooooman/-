How many families have phone and internet rules, and what those rules say

October 29, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

Overall, 67.2% of children say their family has set rules about how they use phones and the internet. Among elementary school students the figure is over 80%, but among high school students it falls below half. This summary of how many families have rules, and what the rules cover, is based on a survey by Japan's Children and Families Agency carried out in November and December 2025 (report published in March 2026).

The short version

  • 67.2% of children said their family has rules (26.1% said no, 6.7% didn't know)
  • 83.8% of elementary, 74.5% of junior high and 44.3% of high school students
  • The most common rule is about how long they can use it (73.5%)

About two-thirds of families have rules

The Children and Families Agency's "Survey on the Internet Usage Environment of Young People" asks children who use the internet (3,030 children) whether their family has set rules about how they use it. 67.2% said yes, a figure that has stayed roughly flat over the past five years.

The figure drops by more than 30 points between junior high and high school.

What the rules say: time comes first

Children who said their family has rules (2,036 children) were asked what kind of rules they are (multiple answers allowed).

  1. How long they can use it (73.5%)
  2. Talk to a parent when something goes wrong (52.4%)
  3. Spending limits or how to pay for in-app purchases (47.2%)
  4. What kinds of sites and apps they use (44.7%)
  5. Don't post personal information (34.1%)
  6. Where they can use it (28.2%)

Children with rules tend to spend less time online

On weekdays, children with rules used the internet for an average of 299.8 minutes, compared with 386.1 minutes for children without rules, a difference of 86.3 minutes.

This does not show that making rules shortens screen time, though. The group without rules includes many high school students, who already tend to spend longer online.

A note on the numbers

  • These are the children's own answers. Even when a parent thinks there are rules, the child may answer "no" or "don't know"
  • Time online is what the children reported, and includes use for studying

For how to make rules, see how to set family phone rules. For how to think about taking a phone away, see what to know before taking away a child's phone.

Summary

  • 67.2% of children say their family has rules (fiscal 2025)
  • 83.8% of elementary, 74.5% of junior high and 44.3% of high school students
  • The most common rule is about how long they can use it (73.5%)
  • Children with rules spend 86.3 fewer minutes online on weekdays, but differences in age also play a part
