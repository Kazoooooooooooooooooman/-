Will you get your lost phone back? Lost-property data from the Tokyo police

October 31, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

In 2025, 144,381 mobile phones and similar devices were found in Tokyo and handed in to the police. That is about 400 a day. Of the phones whose cases had been closed, 83% made it back into their owners' hands. This summary is based on lost-property data from the Tokyo Metropolitan Police Department.

The short version

  • Phones found and handed in: 144,381
  • Phones reported dropped or lost: 225,236
  • Of the 144,064 phones processed, 119,516 (83.0%) went back to their owners

What gets found in Tokyo

There were 4,538,244 reports of found property in 2025, up 3.0% from the year before. About 4.5 billion yen in cash was handed in.

Even the phones that did not go back to their owners were not given to the people who found them or taken over by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government. The remaining 24,548 were disposed of or otherwise dealt with.

More phones are reported lost than are found

There were 225,236 reports of lost phones, about 80,000 more than the number found and handed in (144,381). The gap includes phones left on trains or in shops and never found, phones that were stolen, and so on.

If you lose your phone

  1. Locate it from another device: with Find My on iPhone or Find My Device on Android, its location may show up on a map
  2. Contact your carrier and suspend your line: this stops anyone misusing your calls or text messages
  3. File a lost-property report with the police: report it at the nearest police station or koban (neighborhood police box). They can check it against items that have been handed in
  4. Ask at stations and shops too: railway companies and venues sometimes keep found items themselves for a while

The more you put payments, contacts and photos all on one phone, the bigger the trouble when you lose it. We look at the idea of carrying two phones in carrying a smartphone and a simple phone.

Summary

  • 144,381 phones were found in Tokyo in a year
  • 83% of the phones processed went back to their owners
  • More than 220,000 phones were reported lost. If you drop yours, suspend your line and report it right away
