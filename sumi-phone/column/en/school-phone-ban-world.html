School phone bans have spread to 58% of the world's countries, more than doubling in two years

November 5, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

More than half the countries in the world now ban phone use in schools as a national rule. According to UNESCO, it was 1 in 4 countries (24%) in June 2023, but by March 2026 it was 114 education systems, about 58%. That is more than double in under two years. This summary is based on UNESCO's Global Education Monitoring Report.

How the share of countries with bans has grown

In its 2023 report, UNESCO called on countries to rethink how phones are used in schools. Over the next two years, the share of countries with a ban rose from 24% to 58%.

An "education system" can be a country, or a region that sets its own education rules, each counted as one. During 2024, 19 education systems introduced new bans.

Approaches differ by country

  • A full national ban: phones cannot be used during lessons or during breaks
  • Requiring schools to set rules: the national government stops short of a ban, but requires each school to set rules for phone use (Colombia, Estonia and others)
  • Some countries started a ban and then dropped it: Saudi Arabia lifted its ban after calls to accommodate children with disabilities who need phones for medical reasons

What about Japan?

Japan has no single national ban; each school or local authority sets its own rules. Japan already has one of the lowest shares in the world of students who get distracted by phones in class. For details, see students distracted by phones in class (PISA 2022).

What to keep in mind when reading the numbers

  • UNESCO counts whether a ban is written into law or national policy. It does not count whether the ban is actually followed
  • For countries like the US, where rules differ from state to state, not every state is counted

FAQ

How many countries ban phones in schools?

According to UNESCO, 114 education systems, about 58% of the world, as of March 2026. That is more than double the 24% of June 2023.

Why are more countries banning phones?

Because of concerns about the effects on concentration in class, bullying and children's mental health. In its 2023 report, UNESCO called on countries to reconsider the pros and cons of using phones in school.

Summary

  • In just over two years, school phone bans spread from 24% to 58% of the world's countries
  • Some countries have full bans, while others only require schools to set their own rules
  • Japan has no single national ban
