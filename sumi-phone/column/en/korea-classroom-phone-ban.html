From March 2026, South Korean law bans phones in class

November 7, 2026 · By Kazuma Sasaki (Founder, Soto Phone)

In South Korea, from the new school year in March 2026, the law bans students from using smartphones and other smart devices during class. The National Assembly passed an amendment to the Elementary and Secondary Education Act on August 27, 2025, and it took effect on March 1, 2026. This summary is based on reports of the Ministry of Education's explanation and articles in major Korean newspapers.

What the law says

A new Article 20-5, "Restrictions on the use of smart devices, etc.", was added to the Elementary and Secondary Education Act. At its heart is one sentence: "Students must not use mobile phones or other smart devices during class."

What it says
RuleUsing phones and other smart devices during class is banned
ExceptionsUse as an assistive device by students with disabilities or special educational needs, use for educational purposes, and use to respond to emergencies (with a teacher's approval)
Outside classPrincipals and teachers may restrict the use or possession of devices on school grounds when needed to protect the right to learn and teaching
DetailsEach school sets the criteria, methods and types of devices covered in its school rules

Why make it a law?

Since 2023, South Korea's Ministry of Education had limited phone use in class through an administrative notice on student guidance. A ministry official explained that the amendment raises the restrictions in that notice to the level of law.

Behind it is teenagers' dependence on smartphones. In a 2024 survey by the Ministry of Science and ICT, 42.6% of people aged 10–19 were in the "at risk of overdependence" group. That was up 2.5 points on the previous year (22.9% across all ages).

Teachers have spoken up too. In a May 2025 survey of 5,591 teachers by the Korean Federation of Teachers' Associations, 66.5% said students' phones had disrupted their classes.

South Korea's National Human Rights Commission once held that schools collecting students' phones violated students' rights. But in October 2024 it changed its view, ruling that collecting phones is not a human rights violation.

For and against

  • Both the ruling party and the main opposition party supported it. The Korean Federation of Teachers' Associations welcomed it, saying it protects students' right to learn and their health while strengthening teachers' authority
  • Youth Act, a coalition of civic groups, opposed it, saying it "disproportionately infringes on students' constitutional rights" such as communication and privacy
  • A researcher at the National Assembly Research Service pointed out that most schools already have phone rules, so the change may be small and it "may end up as merely symbolic legislation"
  • Others warn of confusion if each school draws the line differently on what counts as an "educational purpose" or an "emergency"

Schools since the law began (Seoul)

According to press reports, of Seoul's 1,311 elementary, middle and high schools, 872 (66.5%) let students carry phones and ban use only in class, while 378 (28.8%) collect phones before class. Under the same law, schools are doing things very differently.

France is extending its "digital pause", in which students hand in their phones on arrival, to all middle schools (see France's "digital pause"). Countries and schools are split between a rule against using phones and a system that keeps them out of reach.

FAQ

Are phones banned in South Korean schools?

Since March 1, 2026, the law has banned the use of phones and other smart devices during class. Exceptions are assistive use by students with disabilities, educational purposes and emergencies. How phones can be used at break time, and whether they are collected, is set by each school's rules.

How many countries restrict phones in schools?

According to UNESCO, about 58% of countries worldwide as of March 2026. See school phone bans now cover 58% of the world's countries.
