From the new school year that began on September 11, 2024, Greece banned students in lower and upper secondary schools from having their phones in visible view or using them. The ban covers not just lessons but breaks and the schoolyard too. Breaking it means the principal imposes a one-day suspension from classes. This summary is based on the Greek Government Gazette, Ministry of Education documents and press reports.

The short version

  • Students may bring phones to school, but must keep them put away in a bag or pocket. Having one in view and using one are both banned
  • In secondary schools, getting caught means a one-day suspension. Filming or recording teachers or classmates brings heavier penalties
  • Suspension numbers have been announced, but we have not found an official evaluation of the effect on learning or bullying

What the rules say (lower and upper secondary)

The rules are set out in Article 28 of a ministerial decision dated September 10, 2024 (Government Gazette ΦΕΚ Β' 5130). They apply to Greek lower secondary schools (gymnasio) and upper secondary schools (lykeio).

  • Inside school buildings and in outdoor areas such as the schoolyard, students may not have a phone in visible view or use it
  • The ban covers not only phones but also other electronic devices or games with a camera or microphone. Calls, filming and any other use are all off-limits
  • The only exception is a medical app, and only with a doctor's certificate
  • Teachers must also switch their phones off during lessons. At break they may use them only inside the staff room

Announcing the rules, Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis said, "You can bring phones to school, but they must remain in your bags throughout the school day." That year's slogan was "Cell phones in the school bag." Kyriakos Pierrakakis, the education minister at the time, said the rule "applies even during breaks" and called it "a matter of public health."

What happens if you break the rules?

What the student didWhat happens
Had a phone in visible view or used itThe principal imposes a one-day suspension and informs the parents, who either come to collect the student or agree in writing to the student leaving school
Recorded or filmed teachers or classmatesThe student must switch off and hand over the phone, and the parents are called in. The principal can impose up to three days' suspension; for especially serious cases, the teachers' council can impose up to five days
The filming involves especially sensitive personal dataThe teachers' council must order a change of school (transfer to another school)

Under the decision, once a teacher finds a student with a phone in view, the teacher informs the principal, who imposes the one-day suspension. The decision does not set out a series of warnings first.

What about primary schools and kindergartens?

For primary schools and kindergartens, the rules appear in the ministry's annual operating circulars. Under the circular as amended in September 2024, primary school students may not use phones or similar devices for calls, filming or any other purpose, inside the building or on outdoor school grounds. According to press reports, if a phone is found outside a bag, the school keeps it, returns it after classes and informs the parents. No suspension rule like the one for secondary schools is mentioned. The kindergarten operating circular for 2026–27 (September 8, 2026) contains the same ban on use and the same exception for medical apps.

How many suspensions have there been?

Suspension figures have been reported as Ministry of Education data or as statements by ministers. Each figure is a running total from the start of the school year in September 2024.

  • September 11–27, 2024: 800 one-day suspensions, all for students who had their phones in plain view in class or at break (Ministry of Education data, reported by esos)
  • October 21, 2024: Education Minister Pierrakakis said on the radio that 6,043 students had been suspended since the start of the school year
  • September 2025: Education Minister Sofia Zacharaki announced 14,806 suspensions between September 2024 and May 2025 (66 a day)

Zacharaki said, "even if it was just a one-hour suspension, it certainly sent a message."

Reading the numbers with care

  • Suspension counts are "the number of students caught breaking the rule." On their own, these numbers cannot tell you whether the rule is working or not
  • The figures come from ministers' statements and press reports. We could not confirm their publication as official Ministry of Education statistics. Reports also sometimes mix up the number of suspensions and the number of students
  • As of November 16, 2026, we could not find an official evaluation of the effect on learning, bullying or students' mental health

FAQ

Can students bring phones to school in Greece?

Yes. But inside the school and in the schoolyard, they must keep them put away in a bag or pocket. In secondary schools, having a phone in view or using it leads to a one-day suspension. Medical apps backed by a doctor's certificate are the exception.

Does Greece's phone ban apply to teachers?

Yes. Under the September 2024 ministerial decision, teachers must switch their phones off during lessons and may use them at break only inside the staff room. Principals and deputy principals may use theirs sparingly, as needed for teaching or running the school.
