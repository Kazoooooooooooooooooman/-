People use their phones longer on days off than on weekdays. Time spent on the internet on smartphones and similar devices was about 20 minutes longer on days off across all ages, and more than an hour longer for teens and people in their 20s. This breakdown by age group is based on the report (published June 2026) of a survey the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) carried out in December 2025.

The short version

  • Internet use on mobile devices across all ages: 140.7 minutes on days off, 120.5 minutes on weekdays (a gap of about 20 minutes)
  • Teens spend 265.5 minutes (about 4 hours 26 minutes) on days off, 64.0 minutes longer than on weekdays
  • For people in their 60s and 70s, time on days off is slightly shorter

Time on days off by age group

In the survey by the MIC Institute for Information and Communications Policy, 1,800 people aged 13–79 kept a diary of what they did on two weekdays and one day off, in 15-minute blocks. The average time spent on the internet on smartphones and mobile phones on a day off was as follows.

The younger the age group, the more the time grows on days off. One reading is that the free time on days without work or school goes straight to the phone.

What grows on days off: videos and social media

Looking at time by type of internet use in the same survey (including devices other than smartphones), videos and social media grew sharply on days off for teens and people in their 20s.

Video sharing services means services such as YouTube and Niconico (a Japanese video site). If you want to rethink how you spend your days off, see also how not to lose your day off to your phone.

A caution when reading the numbers

  • These are averages that include people who did not use them, counted as 0 minutes
  • Days off were recorded for one day only, so there are fewer respondents than for weekdays (two days)

Summary

  • Internet use on mobile devices is about 20 minutes longer on days off, across all ages (fiscal 2025)
  • Teens spend 265.5 minutes on days off, 64.0 minutes longer than on weekdays
  • What grows on days off is videos and social media
