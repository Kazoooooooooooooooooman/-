Does looking at a phone all the time make your eyesight worse? Is it safe to give one to a child? Many people wonder about this. The short answer is that the screen itself does not damage your eyes. But your eyes certainly get tired, and children's eyesight keeps getting worse. Here is what the data say, and what eye specialists recommend.

The short version

  • Uncorrected vision below 1.0: 36.8% in elementary school, 60.6% in middle school, 71.1% in high school (fiscal 2024)
  • The American Academy of Ophthalmology says screens do not permanently damage your eyes. But they do tire them
  • While you look at a screen, you blink only 5 to 7 times a minute instead of 15
  • For myopia (nearsightedness) in children, "outdoor light" matters. Without enough sunlight, myopia progresses more easily

Children's eyesight gets worse as they move up through school

Every year, Japan's Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT) publishes the School Health Statistics, which compile the results of school health checkups. These are the shares of children whose uncorrected vision was below 1.0 (Japan measures eyesight on a decimal scale; 1.0 is roughly 20/20) in fiscal 2024.

The high school figure of 71.06% is the highest among comparable years (fiscal 2020 to 2023 are excluded because the survey was carried out at a different time of year). As countermeasures, MEXT lists raising awareness of eye health and also encouraging outdoor activities.

Screens do not damage your eyes. But they do tire them

The American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO) says that looking at computer or phone screens for a long time does not permanently harm your eyes. The old saying that "sitting close to the TV ruins your eyes" is not true either, according to the AAO.

On the other hand, it is true that long periods of screen time tire your eyes. One reason is blinking. People normally blink about 15 times a minute, but while looking at a screen this drops to 5 to 7 times. The surface of the eye dries out, causing blurriness, stinging and pain. This is the same kind of fatigue you get after a long stretch of close-up work such as reading or sewing.

For myopia in children, outdoor light

In a 2021 statement, six organizations including the Japanese Ophthalmological Society said that sunlight has a good effect on children's mental and physical development, and that not getting enough sunlight raises the risk of myopia progressing.

Cutting screen time also tends to mean more time outdoors.

Five habits to rest your eyes

  1. Blink on purpose: this is the AAO's first piece of advice. It even suggests putting a sticky note that says "blink" on the edge of your screen
  2. Look up now and then, at something far away: this rests eyes that have been focused up close. Look out the window at a distant building or tree
  3. Match your screen brightness to your surroundings: if the screen is brighter than the room, your eyes have to work harder. Turn it down especially at night
  4. Keep your computer about an arm's length away: the AAO recommends sitting about 63 cm (25 inches, about an arm's length) from the screen, positioned so you look slightly down at it
  5. Make time to go outside: for children in particular, time spent outdoors itself protects the eyes

Summary

  • More than 70% of high school students have uncorrected vision below 1.0
  • Screens do not permanently damage your eyes, but you blink less and your eyes get tired
  • For myopia in children, outdoor light matters
  • Something to do today: the next time you put your phone down, look at something far away out the window for 20 seconds
