China is building a "minor mode" into phones. In November 2024 the Cyberspace Administration of China (the government agency that regulates the internet) issued guidelines that set default limits: up to 1 hour of use a day for under-16s and up to 2 hours for 16- to 18-year-olds. By default, devices are also not available from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. This summary, based on the agency's announcements, explains how the guidelines differ from the 2023 draft and what has happened since.

The short version

  • A draft for public comment came out in August 2023, and the guidelines were finalized on November 15, 2024
  • The time limits are "default settings" that parents can change. The government says they are not a one-size-fits-all mandatory requirement
  • In September 2026, a new draft was released that would build minor mode into regulations (it is still only a draft)

The draft had a shortest limit of 40 minutes for children under 8. The final guidelines have only two time bands: "under 16" and "16 to 18." The wording for night hours also changed, from "prohibited" in the draft to "no service by default (parents can grant exemptions)" in the final guidelines.

The main points of the final guidelines

  • A "three-party linkage" in which devices such as phones, apps and app stores work together, with shared technical standards for features such as "one-tap activation"
  • When a minor uses the device continuously for more than 30 minutes, it reminds them to take a break
  • For the first time, standards for "age-tiered recommendations" that show age-appropriate content first
  • Parents can adjust the default settings, except for apps and services that the law bans minors from using

In its answers to reporters' questions, the agency said these restrictions are "not a one-size-fits-all requirement or a mandatory rule." It explained that the recommended times are given as default settings and that parents can adjust the time and hours as needed.

What has happened since

On April 29, 2025, the "mobile internet minor mode" was officially launched. According to the agency, Huawei, OPPO and ZTE support the mode through system updates, while Xiaomi, Honor and vivo include it on new phones. App stores have also set up sections for minors.

On September 18, 2026, a draft for public comment of the "State Council Regulations on Ensuring Minors' Healthy and Safe Use of the Internet" was published. The deadline for comments is October 17, 2026. The draft says that some services, such as online games and social networking, should be provided to users under 16 through minor mode, and that device makers and others should file the details of their minor mode with the authorities. It does not set a specific number of hours per day. This is still a draft, and no date has been set for it to take effect.

FAQ

How many hours a day can children in China use phones?

Under the November 2024 guidelines, the default settings in minor mode are up to 1 hour a day for under-16s and up to 2 hours for 16- to 18-year-olds. Parents can change the time. By default, no service is provided from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.

Is minor mode mandatory?

Regarding the 2024 guidelines, the Cyberspace Administration of China has said they are "not a one-size-fits-all requirement or a mandatory rule." However, the new draft released in September 2026 would require some services to be provided to under-16s through minor mode. That draft has not been finalized.

Summary

  • In November 2024, China finalized guidelines for a minor mode with default limits of 1 hour for under-16s and 2 hours for ages 16–18
  • The draft's 40-minute limit for under-8s was dropped from the final guidelines
  • In September 2026, a draft was released that would build minor mode into State Council regulations (still a draft)
