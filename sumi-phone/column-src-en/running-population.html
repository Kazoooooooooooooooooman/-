In 2024, an estimated 7.58 million people in Japan aged 20 and over went jogging or running at least once. That is about 3 million fewer than the record 10.55 million in 2020. The participation rate was 7.4%, which the Sasakawa Sports Foundation says is back to about the 2008 level. This summary is based on the foundation's "SSF Sports Life Data" surveys.

The short version

  • The estimated number of joggers and runners fell from 10.55 million in 2020 to 7.58 million in 2024
  • 5.64 million men and 1.75 million women. Women fell below 2 million for the first time in 20 years
  • Among women in their 20s, the rate dropped from 15.8% in 2020 to 4.9% in 2024

How the number of runners has changed

The participation rate was 6.9% in 1998. It then rose, with ups and downs, to 9.7% in 2012, 9.3% in 2018 and a record 10.2% in 2020. Since then it has fallen twice in a row: 8.5% in 2022 and 7.4% in 2024.

By sex and age

In 2024 the participation rate was 11.4% for men and 3.3% for women. The estimates were 5.64 million men and 1.75 million women, so men outnumber women by more than three to one. When the survey began in 1998, the gap was less than two to one.

  • Among men, the rate is highest in their 40s at 18.2%, followed by their 20s (13.5%), 30s (12.9%) and 50s (12.6%)
  • For men in their 40s and 50s, the rate rose from 2022
  • Among women, the rate is highest in their 40s at 5.1%. For women in their 20s it fell to about a third of the 2020 level
  • By place, Tokyo's 23 wards have the highest rate, at 10.1%

Why has it fallen?

A Sasakawa Sports Foundation researcher gives two possible reasons. One is that people have more ways to spend free time, such as social media, smartphone games and videos. The other is that rising prices and labor costs have pushed up race entry fees, and some marathon races have been cancelled or discontinued. Both are presented as guesses; the survey does not confirm the causes.

Even if you don't run, walking has many known benefits (the benefits of walking).

Reading the numbers

  • The 2024 survey asked 3,000 people aged 18 and over across Japan, from June 7 to July 7, 2024. The figures in this article are for people aged 20 and over
  • The estimated population is calculated by multiplying the adult population in the Basic Resident Register by the participation rate
  • "At least once a year" includes people who only run now and then

FAQ

How many people run in Japan?

The Sasakawa Sports Foundation estimates that 7.58 million people aged 20 and over went jogging or running at least once in 2024.

Is the number of runners rising or falling?

Falling. The record was 10.55 million in 2020, followed by 8.77 million in 2022 and 7.58 million in 2024.

Summary

  • An estimated 7.58 million people went jogging or running in 2024
  • That is about 3 million fewer than 10.55 million in 2020, with a particularly sharp drop among women in their 20s
  • More men in their 40s and 50s are running, so the face of running is changing
