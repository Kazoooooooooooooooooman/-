In Italy, students may not use phones in class, even for schoolwork. A July 2024 notice from the Ministry of Education covered nursery school through middle school, and a June 2025 notice extended the ban to high schools. When the ministry surveyed middle and high schools in the 2025–26 school year, almost all of them had written the ban into their school rules. This summary is based on notices and a survey from Italy's Ministry of Education and Merit (MIM).

The short version

  • Italy does not allow phones in class, even for learning. Computers and tablets are still allowed
  • The ministry points to reports from the OECD, UNESCO, the WHO and others, citing concerns about focus, grades and mental health
  • In the 2025–26 survey, only 0.6% of schools said they had not written the ban into their rules

How the rules developed

WhenWhat
December 2022A circular confirms that phone use during lessons is banned. With the teacher's permission, phones could still be used for learning
July 11, 2024Notice no. 5274 bans phone use in class, even for educational purposes, from nursery school through middle school, ahead of the 2024–25 school year
June 16, 2025Circular no. 3392 bans high school students from using phones during lessons and, more generally, throughout school hours, even for learning

Both notices ask schools to rewrite their school rules and their "educational co-responsibility pact" (an agreement between school and family), and to set penalties for breaking the ban. The 2025 circular leaves it to each school to decide how to enforce the ban in practice, such as how phones are stored.

The reasons given

  • The 2024 notice cites a 2023 UNESCO report, noting that in 14 countries, simply having a phone nearby distracted students and harmed their learning
  • It also cites the OECD's PISA 2022 report: students who use phones more often at school get more distracted, with focus dropping especially in math lessons
  • The 2025 circular cites a 2024 OECD report, a 2024 report from WHO Europe, and research from Italy's National Institute of Health (ISS). The ISS says problematic smartphone use affects more than 25% of teenagers, with harm to sleep, concentration and relationships

Computers, tablets and interactive whiteboards may still be used for learning under the teacher's guidance. The ban is on "phones," not on digital learning itself.

Exceptions

  • When it is written into the individual education plan (PEI) of a student with a disability, or the personalized learning plan (PDP) of a student with a specific learning disorder
  • When there is a well-founded personal reason
  • In high schools, in technical courses on computing and telecommunications, when a phone is truly needed for the lesson

What schools are doing (2025–26 survey)

In the 2025–26 school year, the ministry surveyed how middle and high schools handle digital devices, and published the results on March 31, 2026.

72.9% of schools had written the ban into both their school rules and the pact, 17.0% into the school rules only, and 1.9% into the pact only; 7.5% were still updating them, and 0.6% had not included it. Still, what is written in the rules is not the same as whether students actually follow them. The survey compiles schools' own answers, and the report does not state how many schools took part.

Compared with France's "digital pause," where phones are handed in each morning, most Italian schools simply keep phones in bags (France's digital pause). A phone within reach keeps pulling at your attention, at school and at home alike.

FAQ

Are students in Italy banned from bringing phones to school?

The notices ban "using" phones at school. Many schools have students keep their phones in their bags. Each school decides how phones are stored.

How many countries ban phones in schools?

According to UNESCO, about 58% of countries worldwide as of March 2026. See school phone bans now cover 58% of the world's countries.
