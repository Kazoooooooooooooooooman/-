How many people in Japan play tennis today? According to a Japan Tennis Association report (March 2025), Japan's tennis population, meaning people who played tennis (not soft tennis) at least once in the past year, is 3.12 million. Over the past 20 years it has fallen by about 2 million people, or roughly 40%. This summary draws on surveys by the Japan Tennis Association and the Sasakawa Sports Foundation, and on MIC's Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities.

How the tennis population has changed

The Japan Tennis Association defines the tennis population as "the estimated number of Japanese people who played tennis (not soft tennis) at least once in the past year." Its figures are based on the Sasakawa Sports Foundation's "Survey on Sports Life." The estimate for adults aged 20 and over has moved like this:

As a participation rate, it was 3.9% in 2000 and again 3.9% in 2008. It fell to 2.9% in 2012, 2.3% in 2020 and was 2.6% in 2022. The 2020 figure was also affected by the COVID-19 state of emergency.

Among adults aged 20 and over, an estimated 1.13 million play tennis at least once a week. About 40% of adults who played at least once a year are "regular" players who play weekly or more.

A big drop among teenagers

The fall is largest among teenagers. The teen tennis population peaked at 1.25 million in 2005. It has kept falling since 2015, and in 2023 it was 440,000, about a third of the 2005 level.

According to the Japan Tennis Association, the biggest drop was among occasional players, who played at least once a year but less than twice a month. This group fell from 540,000 in 2015 to 120,000 in 2023. Meanwhile, 55% of teens who play tennis do so at least once a week.

Government data shows a decline too

In MIC's Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities, the share of people aged 10 and over who played tennis in the past year has also fallen. This survey's "tennis" item does not say it is limited to tennis with a hard ball.

Older figures for people aged 15 and over show 11.8% (11.10 million) in 1986 and 11.9% (11.91 million) in 1991. By 2001 the rate was 5.6% (6.01 million), less than half of the level ten years earlier.

In 2021 the rate was 3.8% for men and 3.0% for women. By life stage, it was higher among students in their teens: 15.8% for junior high school students and 12.0% for high school students. To compare tennis with other sports, see our ranking of the sports people in Japan play.

Recent trends

In the Sasakawa Sports Foundation's 2024 survey (preliminary results), 2.4% of people aged 18 and over, an estimated 2.51 million, played "tennis (not soft tennis)" at least once in the year. However, the 2024 figure covers people aged 18 and over, while the Japan Tennis Association's 2.68 million covers people aged 20 and over, so the two cannot be compared directly.

FAQ

How many people play tennis in Japan?

The Japan Tennis Association's report (March 2025) puts it at 3.12 million: 2.68 million adults aged 20 and over (2022) and 440,000 teenagers (2023). It counts people who played tennis (not soft tennis) at least once a year.

Is the number of tennis players rising or falling?

Falling. According to the Japan Tennis Association, it has dropped by about 2 million, or roughly 40%, over the past 20 years. Among teenagers it went from 1.25 million in 2005 to 440,000 in 2023.

Summary

  • Japan's tennis population is 3.12 million, down about 40% in 20 years
  • The drop is sharpest among teenagers, from 1.25 million in 2005 to 440,000 in 2023
  • Still, about 40% of adult players play at least once a week, so those who stay with it keep playing
