More than half of elementary, junior high and high school students now read no books at all on a typical day. In a survey by the University of Tokyo and the Benesse Educational Research and Development Institute, 52.7% of children had a reading time of zero minutes in 2024. That is about 1.5 times the 34.3% of 2015, ten years earlier. Children who spent longer on smartphones also tended to read for less time.

The short version

  • 52.7% of elementary, junior high and high school students read for zero minutes a day
  • The higher the grade, the larger the share reading zero minutes
  • Children who spend longer on smartphones spend less time reading

How the share reading zero minutes has changed

The survey asks the same parents and children every year and has been running since 2015.

Smartphone time and reading

In 2024, daily smartphone use was 33.4 minutes for 4th to 6th graders, 95.7 minutes for junior high school students and 138.3 minutes for high school students. The survey found that children who spent longer on smartphones tended to spend less time reading.

That said, this does not prove that "smartphones made children stop reading". It is also possible that children who did not like books to begin with are the ones spending a long time on smartphones.

Things to keep in mind with these numbers

  • What counts as "reading" differs from survey to survey, so these figures cannot be compared directly with other surveys
  • Smartphone times were reported by the children themselves, from 4th grade up
