You open your phone on the morning train, and the next thing you know it is your stop. The ride home is the same. Your commute takes up a good part of the day, yet you cannot remember what you did with it. According to a survey by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, people who commute to work or school on weekdays spend an average of 1 hour 19 minutes a day traveling there and back. Change how you use that time, and the whole day starts to feel different.

The short version

  • On weekdays, people who commute to work or school spend a national average of 1 hour 19 minutes a day traveling (2021)
  • In Kanagawa it is 1 hour 40 minutes, and in Chiba and Tokyo 1 hour 35 minutes. The Tokyo area runs about 20 minutes longer than the national average
  • Over a five-day week, that is about six and a half hours. Over a year, it is more than 300 hours
  • Deciding in advance what to do on days you sit, stand or walk makes the habit stick

The average commute: 1 hour 19 minutes a day

Every five years, the Statistics Bureau of Japan runs the Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities to find out how people spend their day. These figures come from the 2021 survey and show the average time spent by people who actually commuted to work or school on a weekday.

Take the national average of 1 hour 19 minutes over five days, and you get 6 hours 35 minutes a week. If you work about 50 weeks a year, that comes to more than 300 hours. That is enough time to read dozens of books.

Days you get a seat

  1. Read a book: the classic choice. Just keep one in your bag, and you can reach for it instead of your phone (for people who can no longer read books)
  2. Write three things in a notebook: in the morning, "three things to do today"; on the way home, "three good things about today". It takes five minutes
  3. Close your eyes and rest: doing nothing is a perfectly good use of the time. Simply not looking at a screen gives your eyes and your mind a break

Days you stand

  1. Look out the window: even a view you see every day changes with the season and the weather. You may spot a new shop or a park you never knew was there
  2. Listen to music or the radio without looking at the screen: once it starts playing, put the phone in your pocket. If you only use your ears, your eyes are free
  3. Learn one word: take a single flashcard with you and learn it before you reach your station
  4. Plan the order of your day in your head: if you decide what to start with, you get going faster once you arrive

Days you walk

  1. Walk one station: get off one stop early and walk, and your commute becomes exercise (the benefits of walking)
  2. Take a different route: just going one street over from your usual way changes how the town looks
  3. Put your phone away and look ahead as you walk: walking while looking at your phone can also cause accidents (data on accidents caused by walking while using a phone)

Summary

  • On weekdays, people who commute to work or school spend an average of 1 hour 19 minutes a day traveling
  • In the Tokyo area it is 1 hour 35 to 40 minutes. Over a year, that is more than 300 hours
  • Decide in advance what to do on days you sit, stand or walk
  • Something to do today: pick just one thing to do on tomorrow morning's train
