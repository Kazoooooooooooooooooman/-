In math class, other students using their phones are distracting. Only 4.0% of Japanese first-year high school students said so. The OECD average was 25.2%, or 1 in 4. Japan's figure was the lowest of all participating countries. In classrooms around the world, phones are getting in the way of lessons, and Japan has not reached that point yet. This summary is based on the OECD's international assessment of student learning (PISA 2022).

The short version

  • About 4 to 5% of Japanese students are distracted by phones and other devices in class. The OECD average is 25 to 30%
  • Japan has the lowest figure of all participating countries on both questions
  • Japan's math classes rank first in the OECD for "disciplinary climate"

Japan vs. the OECD average

The question was "How often do these things happen in your math lessons?" Digital devices include phones, websites and apps. The figures combine "every lesson" and "most lessons."

If you include "in some lessons," about 1 in 5 students in Japan, too, say they sometimes get distracted by phones in class.

Japan's classes rank first for "disciplinary climate"

PISA compares how orderly lessons are using an index called "disciplinary climate." Japan ranked first among OECD member countries (Japan 1.09, OECD average 0.02). A classroom culture in which students don't touch their phones during lessons likely helps explain this figure.

Reading these numbers

  • The survey was carried out from June to August 2022, among first-year high school students (age 15) selected from across Japan
  • In Japan, the survey took place while the rollout of one device per high school student was still under way (according to a note in the report)
  • The questions were about math lessons, and do not cover other subjects or studying at home

FAQ

What is PISA?

It is an international assessment that the OECD (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development) carries out every three years among 15-year-old students. Alongside reading, math and science skills, it includes a questionnaire about lessons and daily life.

How many students around the world are distracted by phones in class?

On average across the OECD, 25.2% of students are distracted by other students' phones and other devices, and 30.4% by their own devices. That is roughly 1 in 4 or more.

What about studying at home?
