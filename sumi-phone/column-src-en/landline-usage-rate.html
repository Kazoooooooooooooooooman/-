In fiscal 2025, 45.9% of people across all ages used a landline phone. That is less than half. The gap between age groups is large: only 17.0% of people in their 30s use one, while 71.4% of people in their 70s do. This summary is based on a survey by Japan's Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC).

The short version

  • 45.9% of people across all ages use a landline
  • About 20% of people in their 20s and 30s, and about 70% in their 60s and 70s
  • By number of contracts, NTT East and West subscriber lines have fallen by about 30% in five years

Usage by age group

Usage is lowest among people in their 30s, starts rising in their 40s, and passes half in their 50s. The figures show whether the survey respondents themselves use a landline.

The number of contracts keeps falling too

Separate MIC statistics put the number of landline contracts at 47.35 million at the end of December 2025. Subscriber lines with NTT East and West (Japan's former state telephone company, now split in two) numbered 10.78 million, down about 30% from 15.73 million at the end of March 2021 (the number of landline contracts).

Deciding whether to keep a home phone

  • Worth keeping: if someone in the family has no cell phone, or you have given your home number to hospitals or schools
  • Fine to give up: if everyone in the family has their own cell phone and the only calls you get are sales calls
  • If you keep it, protect against scams: you can block international calls in and out for free (phone scam losses and how to protect yourself)
