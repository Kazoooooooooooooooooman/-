You miss a step on the station stairs while looking at your phone. You don't notice the edge of the platform and fall onto the tracks. According to the Tokyo Fire Department, 171 people in Tokyo were taken to hospital by ambulance over the five years from 2021 to 2025 after accidents involving using a phone while walking and similar situations. What kinds of accidents happen, where, and to whom? Let's look at the data.

The short version

  • 171 people were taken to hospital by ambulance over the five years from 2021 to 2025, including 43 in 2025 (preliminary figure)
  • The most common accidents were falls (71 people), followed by falling from a height and bumping into something
  • About 70% happened on roads or at transit facilities, many of them on sidewalks and at stations
  • People in their 20s were the largest age group (31 people). Being young does not make you safer

171 people in 5 years: the most common accidents

The Tokyo Fire Department groups accidents that happen while someone is looking at or using a phone screen while walking or cycling as accidents involving "using smartphones while walking and similar" (covering Tokyo except Inagi City and the outlying islands).

Falling over accounts for more than 40% of the total. Nearly 80% of injuries were minor, but 36 people were diagnosed with moderate or worse injuries that needed a hospital stay.

Where accidents happen, and to which age groups

By location, 119 people, about 70% of the total, were hurt on roads or at transit facilities, with many accidents on sidewalks and at stations. By age, people in their 20s were the largest group at 31, but those taken to hospital ranged widely, from their 20s to their 70s.

Here are some examples the Tokyo Fire Department has shared.

  • Fell over: was going down station stairs while looking at a phone, missed a step, fell and hurt their head (40s, moderate)
  • Bumped into something: was walking while using a phone, didn't see a utility pole and hit their forehead on it (20s, minor)
  • Fell from a height: was walking along a station platform while looking at a phone, fell onto the tracks and hurt their lower back (teens, moderate)

It is not only the person on the phone who gets hurt. There have also been accidents in which people nearby were injured after being hit by someone walking or cycling while looking at a phone.

Three ways to prevent accidents, starting today

  1. Stop when you look: this is the Tokyo Fire Department's whole message. Stop and look at your phone off to the side, where you won't block the flow of people
  2. Put it in your pocket on stairs and platforms: falls from a height happen on stairs and station platforms. Make it a rule not to hold your phone in these places
  3. Check your route and transfers before you set off: the longer you look at a map while walking, the more dangerous it gets. Look things up before you leave, and if you get lost, stop to check

Using a phone while cycling also became subject to fines in April 2026 (see penalties for using a phone while cycling).

Summary

  • In Tokyo, 171 people were taken to hospital by ambulance over five years after accidents involving using a phone while walking and similar situations
  • Falls are the most common accident. Most happen on sidewalks and at stations, and people in their 20s are the largest age group
  • 36 people had injuries that needed a hospital stay
  • Something to do today: put your phone in your pocket on station stairs and platforms
