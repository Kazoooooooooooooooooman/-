72% of US high school teachers say students being distracted by their phones is a "major problem in the classroom". Among middle school teachers it is 33%, and in elementary schools 6%. The higher the grade, and the more students who have phones, the bigger the problem for teachers. This summary is based on a survey by the Pew Research Center in the US.

The short version

  • 7 in 10 high school teachers see phones in class as a major problem
  • Even where there are rules, 6 in 10 high school teachers said they are hard to enforce
  • Teen students, on the other hand, mostly don't see phones as a bad thing

Differences by school level

Across all levels from elementary to high school, 1 in 3 teachers called it a "major problem", and another 20% a "minor problem".

"Very difficult" is the share among teachers at schools that have a phone rule. In high schools, 6 in 10 teachers struggle to enforce the rules even where they exist.

What do students think?

In a separate Pew Research Center survey of teens, 45% said smartphones "help people their age do well in school". 23% said they "make it harder", and 30% said they "have no impact". Teachers and students see it very differently.

Compared with Japan

Among Japanese first-year high school students (age 15–16), 4–5% said they get distracted by phones and other devices in class, one of the lowest levels in the world. For details, see students distracted by phones in class (PISA 2022).

Things to keep in mind with these numbers

  • The survey was carried out in fall 2023 among US public school teachers
  • It measures how big a "problem" teachers feel it is, not students' grades

FAQ

Can students use phones in class at US schools?

It depends on the school and the state. The share of teachers who said their school has rules was 84% in elementary schools, 94% in middle schools and 71% in high schools.
