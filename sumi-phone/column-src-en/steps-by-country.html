How many steps a day do people around the world take? A Stanford University research team looked at step counts recorded on the smartphones of about 720,000 people in 111 countries (Nature, 2017). Comparing the 46 countries and regions with the most users, Japan ranked 4th with 6,010 steps a day. Here we look at that study together with Japan's own national survey by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare.

The short version

  • By smartphone step counts, Japan is one of the most active countries in the world
  • What was most closely linked to obesity was not average steps, but the gap between people who walk a lot and people who barely walk
  • Japan's own national survey shows steps have declined over the past ten years (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare)

Daily steps by country and region

The team used data from users of Argus, an activity-tracking app for the iPhone. The records cover a total of 68 million days, from July 2013 to December 2014. The 46 countries and regions are those with at least 1,000 users. Japan had 20,386 users.

The top places went to Hong Kong, China, Ukraine and Japan, with Asia and Eastern Europe standing out. The US ranked 30th with 4,774 steps.

What mattered was the gap

The main message of the study was not average steps but "activity inequality." Within a country, the bigger the gap between people who walk a lot and people who barely walk, the higher the share of people with obesity.

  • Inequality explained obesity rates better than average steps did (R² of 0.64 vs. 0.47)
  • People in the five countries with the highest inequality were 196% more likely to be obese than people in the five with the lowest
  • About 40% (43%) of the variation in inequality was explained by the gap between men and women

Japan had the 6th-lowest inequality. Its gender gap (how much fewer steps women took compared with men) was 14.9%, smaller than the 25.3% in the US. An analysis of 69 US cities also found that more walkable cities had lower activity inequality.

Japan's national survey

The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's National Health and Nutrition Survey measures steps by having adults aged 20 and over wear pedometers. In 2023, the average was 6,628 steps for men and 5,659 for women. Over the past ten years, steps have decreased significantly for both men and women. In 2024, the survey changed how it measured and tallied steps (pedometers with three-axis accelerometers, nationally adjusted figures), and the average was 7,071 steps (7,763 for men, 6,495 for women). When prefectures were split into four groups by steps and compared with age adjusted, men aged 20 to 64 in the top group walked 9,151 steps, against 7,713 in the bottom group, a gap of 1,438 steps. Even within Japan, where you live makes a difference.

What to keep in mind when reading the numbers

  • Smartphones only count steps while you carry them. They give lower numbers than pedometers worn all day, so they cannot be compared directly with the ministry's figures
  • The data comes from iPhone users of a health app and may not represent each country as a whole
  • The data is from 2013–2014 and may differ from step counts today

FAQ

Which country walks the most?

In the Nature study (2017), Hong Kong had the most steps of the 46 countries and regions, at 6,880 a day, followed by China (6,189), Ukraine (6,107) and Japan (6,010).

How many steps a day should you walk?

Japan's national target (Health Japan 21, third term) is an average of 7,100 steps a day (age-adjusted). An analysis combining 15 studies (Lancet Public Health, 2022) found that the risk of death fell as steps increased, leveling off at around 6,000 to 8,000 steps for adults aged 60 and over, and 8,000 to 10,000 steps for adults under 60.

Summary

  • In smartphone data, Japan ranked 4th of 46 countries and regions, at 6,010 steps a day
  • For health, whether many people barely walk matters more than the average
  • Steps in Japan are declining, and in 2023 neither men nor women reached the national target of 7,100 steps
