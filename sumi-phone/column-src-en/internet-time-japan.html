People in Japan use the internet for an average of 183.9 minutes (about 3 hours 4 minutes) a day on weekdays and 192.9 minutes (about 3 hours 13 minutes) on weekends and holidays. That is more than 30 minutes longer than five years ago, in fiscal 2020 (149.3 minutes on weekdays). The figures cover all devices combined, including phones, computers and tablets. This summary is based on a survey by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC).

The short version

  • The average across all ages (13 to 79) is 183.9 minutes on weekdays and 192.9 minutes on days off
  • People in their 20s spend the longest: 266.9 minutes on weekdays and 324.5 minutes on days off
  • 86.8% of people used the internet on a given weekday

Teens and people in their 20s spend about an hour longer online on days off than on weekdays. People in their 60s and 70s spend less time online on days off.

The five-year trend

Time online on days off also rose, from 154.2 minutes in fiscal 2020 to 192.9 minutes. Over the same period, weekday time spent watching live TV fell from 193.2 minutes to 156.1 minutes, so the gap with the internet is widening.

How many people go online

  • The share of people who used the internet on the survey day (the "participation rate") was 86.8% on weekdays and 83.5% on days off
  • In fiscal 2020 the weekday figure was 81.3%, and it has risen a little each year

A note on the numbers

  • Respondents recorded what they did on the survey days in 15-minute blocks. The averages include people who did not go online at all
  • The figures include not only phones but also computers, tablets and internet-connected TVs. For time on phones alone, see average phone use time
  • Use for work and study is included

Summary

  • People spend about 3 hours 4 minutes a day online on weekdays and about 3 hours 13 minutes on days off
  • People in their 20s spend the longest, over 5 hours on days off
  • Time online has grown by more than 30 minutes in five years, and the gap with TV is widening
