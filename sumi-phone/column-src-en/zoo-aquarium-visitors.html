Japan's aquariums recorded 32.62 million visits in fiscal 2023 (April 2023 to March 2024). Zoos recorded 29.50 million. In the Social Education Survey by the Ministry of Education (MEXT), whose figures go back to fiscal 1986, this is the first time aquariums have come out ahead of zoos. Zoo visits are about 36% below their peak of 46.09 million in fiscal 1989. This summary draws on national statistics and official figures for Ueno Zoo and Asahiyama Zoo.

The short version

  • In fiscal 2023, aquariums had 32.62 million visits and zoos 29.50 million, about 62 million combined
  • Aquariums have passed their pre-COVID level (31.97 million in fiscal 2017), but zoos have not returned to their fiscal 2017 level of 33.95 million
  • Per facility, aquariums (388,341 visits) also beat zoos (317,235)

Zoos drew around 45 million visits a year in the late 1980s, fell sharply in the 1990s, and then stayed at roughly 31 to 35 million. Aquariums climbed to 34.37 million in the early 1990s and have since moved between roughly 26 and 33 million. Both dropped steeply in fiscal 2020 because of COVID-19. By fiscal 2023, aquariums were above their fiscal 2017 level, while zoos were at only about 87% of theirs.

Ueno Zoo welcomed 3.36 million visitors in fiscal 2024, up 3.0% on the year before. The Tokyo government's report credits events linked to the return of its pandas to China, among other things. The four Tokyo metropolitan zoos and aquarium (Ueno, Tama, Inokashira and Kasai) had 6,279,018 visitors in total in fiscal 2024.

Asahiyama Zoo in Hokkaido peaked at 3,072,353 visitors in fiscal 2007. In fiscal 2025 it had 1,327,911, less than half of that peak. Winter-season visitors, however, rose from 353,012 in fiscal 2019 to 533,045 in fiscal 2025.

Who goes?

In MIC's Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities, the share of people aged 10 and over who went "sightseeing at amusement parks, zoos, botanical gardens, aquariums, etc." in the past year fell from 33.8% in 2016 to 19.0% in 2021, a year affected by COVID-19. By age in 2021, people aged 30 to 39 were the most likely to go, at 35.1%, which suggests these outings are especially common among people taking their children out. For people 70 and over it was 6.5%.

In the Japan Productivity Center's Leisure White Paper (people aged 15 to 79; the preliminary 2026 edition was an online survey of 3,279 people), the number of people who went to "zoos, botanical gardens, aquariums, museums" was 25.0 million in 2023 and 23.8 million in 2024. In 2025 the participation rate was 24.3%, while 35.8% said they wanted to go (the "desire rate"), the second highest of any activity after domestic sightseeing trips. In other words, many more people want to go than actually do. For trends in going out in general, see the share of people who go out.

Things to keep in mind

  • The Social Education Survey figures combine museums (registered museums and designated facilities) with museum-like facilities. The survey counts zoos and aquariums as types of museum
  • Visits are counted per entry, for the fiscal year (April to March). The survey is carried out roughly every three years
  • Combined zoo-botanical gardens (6.37 million visits in fiscal 2023) are not included in the zoo figure
  • The Japanese Association of Zoos and Aquariums had 140 full members at the end of March 2026 (91 zoos and 49 aquariums). We could not find a published total of visitors across its members

FAQ

How many people visit zoos in Japan each year?

MEXT's Social Education Survey counts 29.50 million visits to zoos in fiscal 2023. Aquariums had 32.62 million, more than zoos.

How many visitors does Ueno Zoo get a year?

According to the Tokyo government's business report, Ueno Zoo had 3,362,664 visitors in fiscal 2024, up 3.0% on the previous year.
