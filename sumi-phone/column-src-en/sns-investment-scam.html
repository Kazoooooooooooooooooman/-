In 2025, there were 9,523 cases in Japan of people being cheated out of money in the name of "investment" by someone they met on social media or after clicking a social media ad, with losses of 128.80 billion yen. Both the number of cases and the losses rose by about 50% from the year before. This summary is based on final figures from the National Police Agency.

The short version

  • There were 9,523 social media investment scam cases (up 48.5% from the year before), with losses of 128.80 billion yen (up 47.9%)
  • First contact came through banner and other ads in 3,785 cases and direct messages in 3,549 cases, together about 80%
  • In about 90% of cases, the ad or DM was followed by an invitation into an "investment group" on LINE (Japan's most-used messaging app)

Cases and losses

The 2024 figures were calculated from the 2025 figures and the size of the increase (6,413 cases, losses of 87.11 billion yen). Taking social media investment scams and romance scams together, the average amount stolen per case was 12.133 million yen, more than twice that of "special fraud" (Japan's term for phone and other remote scams such as the "it's me" scam), at 5.236 million yen.

  • Ads on YouTube and investment websites, and DMs on Instagram, LINE and Facebook, rose especially sharply
  • Instagram was the most common tool used for first contact, followed by YouTube. YouTube cases were about 21 times higher than the year before
  • Ads have been found that use photos and videos of celebrities without permission and promise "guaranteed profits" or "no risk to your principal"

How people get drawn in

  1. On social media, you see an investment ad claiming to be from a celebrity, or get a DM from a stranger
  2. You are invited into an "investment group" on LINE, with offers like "learn for free" or "we'll send you stock reports" (about 90% of cases starting from ads or DMs)
  3. In the group, you are encouraged to invest and transfer money to a designated account. Cases where money was handed over by bank transfer make up 74.9% of the total

Who is being targeted

  • Men: 5,463 cases and 74.77 billion yen. Women: 4,057 cases and 53.49 billion yen. Men account for about 60% of both cases and losses
  • Most victims are in their 40s to 60s, but the fastest growth is among people in their 30s

How to protect yourself

  • Be suspicious of any investment talk on social media: Even if a celebrity's name or photo is used, it may not be them
  • Don't join a LINE group because a stranger invited you: This is the turning point
  • Talk to someone before you transfer money: the police consultation line "#9110" or the consumer hotline "188" (both are Japanese phone numbers)
