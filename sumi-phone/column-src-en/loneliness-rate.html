4.5% of people said they feel lonely "often or always". Including "sometimes" and "occasionally", about 4 in 10 people feel some degree of loneliness. By age, the rate is highest among people in their 30s, and it rises the less often people eat together with someone else. This summary is based on a national survey by Japan's Cabinet Office (conducted in December 2025).

The short version

  • Feel lonely "often or always" 4.5%, "sometimes" 13.7%, "occasionally" 19.5% (37.7% in total)
  • By age: 6.1% in their 30s, 5.5% in their 20s, 5.7% in their 40s and 50s. Men 5.3%, women 3.7%
  • 17.3% among people who "hardly ever" eat with someone else, and 2.7% among those who do so "almost every day"

How many people feel lonely

The share answering "often or always" has stayed at roughly 4 to 5% since the survey began in 2021 (4.5% in 2021, 4.9% in 2022, 4.8% in 2023, 4.3% in 2024).

By age group

Loneliness is often thought of as a problem for older people, but in this survey the rates were higher among people in their 30s to 50s. By gender, the rate was highest among men in their 50s and women in their 30s.

The link with how often people eat together

People who hardly ever eat with someone else feel strong loneliness more than six times as often as people who share meals almost every day. Also, 9.7% of people said they "never" meet and talk in person with family or friends they do not live with.

How the survey was done

  • Its official name is the "Basic Survey on People's Connections". It was mailed to 20,000 people aged 16 and over, chosen at random from across Japan, and 11,873 responded (a response rate of 59.4%)
  • Loneliness was measured in two ways: a direct question asking whether people feel lonely, and three questions based on the UCLA Loneliness Scale. 6.5% of people had a high total score (10 to 12 points) on the three questions

Small steps toward reconnecting

  • Plan one meal a week with someone: family, friends or people from work all count
  • Make time to talk in person, not just through a screen: this survey also uses how often people meet and talk in person as a sign of isolation. Try setting up a face-to-face meeting, even once a month
  • Start by going out on your own: if meeting people feels like too much, just getting outside is enough to begin with (things to enjoy outside on your own)

If the pain does not go away, there are places to get help, such as "Mamorouyo Kokoro", the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's portal listing support lines in Japan.

Summary

  • 4.5% of people feel lonely "often or always", and about 4 in 10 feel some loneliness
  • By age, the rate is highest among people in their 30s
  • Among people who hardly ever eat with someone else, it is 17.3%
