Leaving out sleep, meals, work and housework, people in Japan have 6 hours 16 minutes a day to spend as they like. The largest share of that goes to TV, radio, newspapers and magazines, at 2 hours 8 minutes. Sports get 13 minutes, and "socializing", meaning meeting and talking with other people, gets only 10. This summary is based on the Statistics Bureau of Japan's Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities.

The short version

  • Of the 6 hours 16 minutes of free time, TV, radio, newspapers and magazines take 2 hours 8 minutes, and rest and relaxation 1 hour 57 minutes
  • Socializing fell from 17 minutes five years earlier to 10 minutes
  • The biggest increase was in rest and relaxation, up 20 minutes

How the 6 hours 16 minutes break down

TV, radio, newspapers and magazines plus rest and relaxation take up more than 60% of free time (4 hours 5 minutes) between them. Time spent moving your body and time spent with other people add up to just 23 minutes.

By gender

  • Free time: 6 hours 34 minutes for men, 6 hours 0 minutes for women
  • Sports: 16 minutes for men, 10 minutes for women
  • Socializing: 8 minutes for men, 12 minutes for women

Things to keep in mind about these numbers

  • The survey took place in October 2021, when the COVID-19 pandemic meant people went out and met others less. That is likely part of the reason socializing fell
  • The results come from people aged 10 and over recording what they were doing in 15-minute blocks. The averages include people who did not do the activity at all

FAQ

What are "tertiary activities"?

In the Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities, they are the free time left after "primary activities" such as sleep and meals, and "secondary activities" such as work, school and housework.

How much time do people in Japan spend exercising?

An average of 13 minutes a day (time spent on sports, ages 10 and over, across the whole week). The average includes people who did not exercise at all.

Are people spending less time with others?

Time spent socializing fell from 17 minutes in 2016 to 10 minutes in 2021. To see how far it has recovered from the effects of the pandemic, we will have to wait for the next survey, which is held every five years.
