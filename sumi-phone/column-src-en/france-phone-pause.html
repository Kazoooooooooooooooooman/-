In French middle schools, students hand in their phones when they arrive in the morning and do not touch them until they go home. The scheme is called the "digital pause" (portable en pause). It was trialled in some middle schools in the 2024–25 school year, and France plans to extend it to all middle schools from the start of term in September 2025. This summary is based on announcements from the French Ministry of National Education and press reports.

The short version

  • Since 2018, French law has banned phone use in primary and middle schools
  • The digital pause goes one step further: phones are handed in, so students cannot touch them at all
  • Schools that tried it reported a better atmosphere

From "don't use it" to "don't have it"

The 2018 lawThe digital pause
RulePhones may not be used at schoolPhones are handed in on arrival
Where the phone isIn the student's bagWith the school, for example in lockers at the entrance
During breaksNot allowed (it stays in the bag)Out of reach, so it cannot be touched

A rule against using the phone is not enough when the phone is still within reach and keeps pulling at your attention. The digital pause tries to prevent that by putting the phone somewhere students simply cannot get to it.

What the trial schools found

The scheme was trialled in some middle schools in the 2024–25 school year. Press reports put the number of trial schools anywhere from about 100 to about 180. The education minister at the time called the results "very good", pointing in particular to a better atmosphere in schools.

The challenges

  • The lockers for storing students' phones cost money, and some local authorities objected over who should pay
  • Some point out that the scheme is hard to sustain without the cooperation of teachers and staff
  • We have not yet found an official count of how many middle schools actually started the scheme from September 2025

Could the same thing work in Japan?

Japan has no national rule banning phones in schools. At home, you can create the same "handed in, out of reach" effect by leaving your phone in another room while you study or read. We collected practical ways to do this at home in house rules for putting phones away.

FAQ

Are phones banned in French schools?

Yes. Under a 2018 law, phone use is banned in nursery, primary and middle schools. From September 2025, France plans to extend the digital pause, in which middle school students hand in their phones on arrival, to all middle schools.

How many countries ban phones in schools?

According to UNESCO, about 58% of countries worldwide as of March 2026. See school phone bans now cover 58% of the world's countries.

Summary

  • France has banned phone use in primary and middle schools by law since 2018
  • From September 2025, France plans to extend the digital pause, in which phones are handed in, to all middle schools
  • A system that keeps the phone out of reach is easier to keep than a rule against using it
