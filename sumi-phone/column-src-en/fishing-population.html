The share of people in Japan who went fishing at least once in a year has more than halved over the past 25 years. In the government's Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities, the share of people aged 10 and over who went fishing fell from 16.9% in 1996 to 7.8% in 2021. That is still about 8.73 million people. Among sports, fishing ranks fifth, ahead of golf and baseball.

The short version

  • It recovered slightly in 2016, then fell again in 2021
  • By age, 10- to 14-year-olds have the highest rate, followed by people in their 30s and 40s

The share who went fishing, 1996–2021

The Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities is a national survey held every five years, asking about 190,000 people aged 10 and over what they did in the past year. Figures for ages 10 and over start in 1996. For ages 15 and over, the share was 14.0% in 1986, 15.9% in 1996 and 7.4% in 2021.

The 2021 survey asked about the year from October 2020 to October 2021, which overlaps with the COVID-19 pandemic. Even so, fishing fell by only 0.9 points from 2016, a smaller drop than swimming (−5.3 points) or bowling (−7.6 points).

Of those who went fishing, about 6.62 million were men and about 2.11 million were women. The rates are high among 10- to 14-year-olds and among people in their 30s and 40s. The survey does not ask whether parents and children went together, but the pattern is high for both the children's and the parents' generations. Even among 10- to 14-year-olds, though, the rate was 32.1% in 1996, so fishing among children has also more than halved.

How often do people go?

Of the roughly 8.73 million people who went fishing, more than half, about 4.82 million, went on just 1–4 days a year. About 570,000 people went at least once a week (40 or more days a year). For most people, fishing is an occasional outing.

Things to keep in mind

  • The survey's "participation rate" is the share of people who did the activity at least once in the past year. It does not distinguish between sea fishing and river fishing
  • The 2021 figures come from a survey during the COVID-19 pandemic
  • The survey is held every five years, and the latest results we could confirm are from 2021
  • The Japan Productivity Center's "White Paper on Leisure 2025" says that in the 2024 market, fishing tackle fell back after earlier growth

FAQ

How many people go fishing in Japan?

In MIC's 2021 Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities, about 8.73 million people aged 10 and over (7.8%) had gone fishing in the past year.

Is fishing declining?

As a share of the population, yes. Among people aged 10 and over, it fell from 16.9% in 1996 to 7.8% in 2021. It recovered slightly to 8.7% in 2016, then fell again in 2021.

Summary

  • The share who went fishing fell from 16.9% in 1996 to 7.8% in 2021, less than half in 25 years
  • That is still about 8.73 million people, the fifth most common sport
  • Most people go 1–4 days a year. It is popular among both the children's and the parents' generations
