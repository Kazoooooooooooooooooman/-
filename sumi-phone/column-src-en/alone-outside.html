It's your day off, and you suddenly realize you've spent the whole day on your phone. You want to go out, but you can't think of anything to do on your own. For days like that, we collected 20 ways to enjoy time outside by yourself. They're sorted into four kinds, so just pick one that suits your mood. All of them can be enjoyed without looking at your phone.

The short version

  • If you're not sure, choose from four kinds: move your body, go and see something, make something, or be among people
  • Decide on just one destination before you leave home. If you start looking things up once you're out, you'll never get off your phone
  • Going out alone feels easier when you set a time to finish

Move your body

  1. Walk a street you don't know: Just taking a different street from your usual one brings small discoveries. Research has also shown that walking makes ideas come more easily (the benefits of walking)
  2. Go to a public bath or sauna: Your phone goes in the locker. It's a place where you have no choice but to let it go, so stepping away comes naturally
  3. Go to a public pool or community gym: Many public facilities cost just a few hundred yen to use
  4. Cycle to the next station: You'll see parts of the neighborhood you can't see from the train
  5. Climb a nearby hill or lookout: Once you're at the top, just looking down over the town shifts your mood

Go and see something

  1. See only the permanent collection at an art museum: It's often less crowded than special exhibitions, and you can get around it in about an hour. Try standing in front of one piece you like for five minutes
  2. Browse library shelves you'd normally skip: Cooking, travel, photo books. Pick things up with no particular goal
  3. Watch a film at the cinema: It's one of the few "good screens," where for two hours you don't have to look at any other screen
  4. Walk through a shopping street or market: Chatting a little with shopkeepers keeps you from feeling lonely, even on your own
  5. Pick one book from a used bookstore's bargain shelf: Choosing by title alone leads you to books you would never search for yourself

Make something or try something

  1. Write in a notebook at a café: Things you want to do, things on your mind, things you saw today. Writing by hand helps sort out your thoughts
  2. Go out to take photos: Set a theme, like "only red things today," and the town starts to look different
  3. Try a one-day class: Pottery, cooking, woodworking and so on. Many are aimed at beginners
  4. Sketch: Just draw what's in front of you from a park bench. It doesn't have to be good
  5. Take one book out and finish it outside: A coffee shop, a park, the train. Decide that you'll go home once you've finished it

Be among people

  1. Join local volunteering: Things like neighborhood cleanups or keeping an eye on children; many are easy to join on your own
  2. Go and watch a sports match: It doesn't have to be pro. University and amateur adult games are easy to drop in on
  3. Visit a morning market or farmers' market: You buy directly from the people who made things, so conversation happens naturally
  4. Join a running or yoga group: Simply being among people doing the same thing feels comfortable, even when you're on your own
  5. Find one regular spot: Go to the same place a few times and they'll start to recognize you

Tips for enjoying it without your phone

  • Decide where you're going and when you'll be back before you leave home: If you start searching "where next?" while you're out, you'll end up back on your phone
  • Look at the map once before you go, and remember it: If you get lost, ask someone. That's part of the fun of going out too
  • Set a number of photos to take: If you take lots, you spend more time taking pictures than actually looking
  • When you get home, write one line about what you enjoyed: It becomes the best reason to go out next time

Summary

  • When going out alone, choose from four kinds (move your body, go and see something, make something, be among people) and you won't get stuck
  • Decide where you're going and when you'll be back before you leave home
  • For today: pick one of the 20 and go out for just two hours
