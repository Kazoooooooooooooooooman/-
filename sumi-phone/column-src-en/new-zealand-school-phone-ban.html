Since April 29, 2024, students in New Zealand's state schools have not been allowed to use phones at school. The rule covers lunch and breaks, not just lessons. The slogan is "Phones Away for the Day." When the country's Education Review Office (ERO) looked a year later, 79% of secondary teachers said students' focus had improved. At the same time, fewer than four in ten senior students follow the rules. This summary is based on government announcements and the ERO report.

The short version

  • New Zealand's ban is a legal duty set by national regulations (the Education (School Boards) Regulations 2020)
  • In secondary schools, 79% of teachers said it had a positive effect on focus, and 77% on classroom behavior
  • But the older the students, the less they follow the rules, and the top reason for breaking them is "to contact family"

What is the rule?

According to the Ministry of Education, state schools and state-integrated schools must make sure students do not use or access a phone at school. This includes lunch and breaks. It also applies when students are away from school as part of a school program, such as a course or a visit. Each school board must include this in its school policy.

However, schools must allow phone use when:

  • a phone is needed for health reasons (for example, to monitor insulin levels for a student with diabetes)
  • a phone helps a student with a disability or learning support need
  • a teacher tells students to use phones for a specific class task
  • the principal decides it is needed for special circumstances (for example, the student is a teenage parent)

Parents apply for an exemption, and the school decides whether to grant it. The Ministry asked schools to set up their rules during Term 1 of 2024 and to have them in place no later than April 29, 2024, the first day of Term 2. ERO began checking compliance from Term 2.

What the official review found

In Term 2 of 2025, ERO looked at English-medium state schools with students in Year 7 and above. It collected over 10,700 survey responses from board members, school leaders, teachers, students and parents, and interviewed more than 65 people. The report came out in November 2025.

Nearly all schools had a rule, and 94% banned phone use throughout the day. How they did it varied by type of school: 64% of primary schools (up to Year 8) store students' phones, while 80% of secondary schools let students keep their phones on the condition that they do not use them.

  • Among secondary school leaders, 83% said focus had improved, 78% classroom behavior and 58% achievement
  • 69% of secondary school leaders said bullying had decreased
  • Teachers, leaders and board members also said that, without phones at breaks, students interact with each other better

Are students following the rules?

The higher the year level, the less the rules are followed. In secondary school (Years 9–13), 53% of students said they never use their phone in class. Some students use phones at lunch or breaks (38%), or in bathrooms and other out-of-sight places (36%).

  • The top reason for breaking the rules is "to contact family," given by 54% of students who break them
  • 59% of parents have not changed how they contact their child while at school
  • 57% of teachers said they do not strictly enforce the rules during scheduled breaks

ERO recommended that the rule continue and that parents not contact their children during school hours. It also asked the government to consider adding smartwatches and limiting student access to social media during school hours. The education minister said the government would "seek further advice."

Reading the numbers with care

  • "Improved" means teachers and school leaders feel that way. It is not measured by test scores or other outcomes
  • The minister's announcement used figures that differ slightly from the report summary, such as "80% of teachers" for focus and "66%" for achievement. This article uses the figures from the report summary
  • The review covered English-medium state schools with students in Year 7 and above. Schools that teach in other languages are not included
  • As of November 14, 2026, we have not found any government announcement that the rule will be extended to smartwatches

More and more countries are setting national phone rules for schools (school phone bans now cover 58% of the world's countries). New Zealand's review shows that support from families matters as much as the rule itself.

FAQ

Are phones banned in New Zealand schools?

Yes. Since April 29, 2024, national regulations have barred students in state and state-integrated schools from using phones at school, including at breaks. There are exceptions, such as health reasons or when a teacher asks students to use them for a class task. Private schools are not listed as covered by these regulations.

Has the phone ban changed students?

In ERO's 2025 review, 79% of secondary teachers said focus had improved and 77% said classroom behavior had improved. But these are teachers' views. Only 37% of senior students (Years 12–13) follow the rules.
