Since December 10, 2025, an Australian law has stopped children under 16 from holding social media accounts. The responsibility for following it lies with the social media companies, not with children or parents. In the first few weeks, about 4.7 million accounts believed to belong to under-16s were deactivated, removed or restricted. This summary is based on announcements from the Australian government and the eSafety Commissioner, the agency in charge of online safety.

The short version

  • Social media companies must take "reasonable steps" to stop under-16s from having accounts
  • About 4.7 million accounts were shut down in the first few weeks
  • In March 2026, the government put five platforms with weak age checks on notice

What the law says

  • Social media companies must take "reasonable steps" to make sure under-16s do not have accounts
  • Penalties fall on the companies, not on children or parents
  • Companies that do not comply face fines of up to A$49.5 million (about US$33 million)

The problems that remain

On March 31, 2026, eSafety put five major platforms on notice. Children under 16 were still keeping their accounts, opening new ones, or getting past age checks. eSafety said the platforms relied too heavily on users simply typing in their own age when they sign up.

What about Japan?

Japan has no law that bans social media use by age. Most platforms set a minimum age of 13 in their terms of use. For figures on children who become crime victims through social media, see children who become crime victims through social media.

A note on the numbers

  • 4.7 million is the number of accounts the companies judged to belong to under-16s and shut down. One person can have several accounts, so it is not the same as the number of people
  • The maximum fine is A$49.5 million. The "$33 million" figure often seen in the press is the same amount converted into US dollars
  • The government is reported to plan doubling the maximum fine to A$99 million. We have not yet confirmed whether this has become law

FAQ

When did Australia's social media ban start?

On December 10, 2025. From that date, social media companies have been required to stop under-16s from having accounts.

Are LINE and YouTube banned too?

YouTube is covered. Messaging apps such as WhatsApp and Messenger, online games, and education and health services are not covered. However, a messaging app with social-media-style features may be covered.

Summary
