Holding a phone to talk while driving, or staring at its screen, is banned under Japan's Road Traffic Act. For an ordinary car, it brings a fine of 18,000 yen and 3 penalty points. If it causes a "traffic hazard" such as an accident, a fine is no longer enough: it becomes a criminal offense, with 6 points. Based on National Police Agency materials, here are the penalties and the accident data.

The short version

  • "Holding" (holding the phone to talk or staring at the screen) brings an 18,000 yen fine and 3 points for an ordinary car
  • If that causes a traffic hazard: up to one year in prison or a fine of up to 300,000 yen, and 6 points (the fine-payment system cannot be used)
  • In 2025, there were 148 fatal or serious-injury accidents caused by phone use while driving. The number has been rising since 2021

The penalties at a glance

The penalties were raised in December 2019. They currently stand as follows.

Holding (holding the phone to talk or staring at the screen)Causing a traffic hazard
Criminal penaltyUp to 6 months in prison or a fine of up to 100,000 yenUp to 1 year in prison or a fine of up to 300,000 yen
Fixed fineLarge vehicles 25,000 yen, ordinary cars 18,000 yen, motorcycles 15,000 yen, mopeds 12,000 yen, bicycles 12,000 yenNot applicable
Penalty points3 points6 points

"Holding" is handled through the procedure of paying a fixed fine, but "causing a traffic hazard" has no fixed fine and is dealt with as a criminal offense. The 2019 change tripled the penalty points (an article on Government of Japan Public Relations Online, linked from the National Police Agency page, describes it as "three times the penalty points, higher fines, and instant license suspension!").

Since November 1, 2024, cyclists have also faced penalties (a 12,000 yen fine; no points apply to bicycles). See penalties for using a phone while cycling for details.

What is banned

Article 71, item 5-5 of the Road Traffic Act bans the following, except when the vehicle is stopped:

  • Talking on a cell phone or similar device that can only be used by holding it in your hand
  • Staring at a screen mounted in or brought into the vehicle (such as a smartphone or car navigation system)

Calls that are unavoidable while moving, such as to help injured people or for public safety, are exempt. The National Police Agency urges drivers who really need to use a phone to stop somewhere safe first.

Accident data (2025)

  • There were 148 fatal or serious-injury accidents caused by phone use and similar while driving, and the number has been rising since 2021
  • When a driver was using a phone, the share of accidents that turned fatal was about 3.4 times higher than when they were not
  • About 90% of the driver behavior behind fatal or serious-injury accidents was failure to watch the road ahead

According to the National Police Agency, research reports agree that drivers sense danger when they look at a screen for 2 seconds or longer. At 60 km/h, a car travels about 33.3 meters in those 2 seconds.

Enforcement figures

Police carry out about 4.03 million traffic enforcement actions a year in total, of which about 180,000 a year are for phone use and similar while driving. In 2025, that was about 4.5% of the total.

If you can't resist checking your phone

  • Enter your destination in the navigation before you set off, and don't touch it once you're moving
  • Use features that silence notifications while driving, such as the Driving Focus on iPhone
  • Put your phone out of reach, for example in your bag

For ways to cut down on notifications themselves, see how to clean up your phone notifications.
