You open YouTube to look up one thing. When the video ends, the next one starts. Go back to the home screen and recommendations are waiting. Then at night a notification pulls you back in. Watching too much YouTube is not a sign of weak willpower. The app is full of triggers that keep you watching. This article walks through settings that switch off those triggers one by one, and habits that help the settings last. You can do all of it today.

The short version

  • The four triggers for watching too much are autoplay, home-feed recommendations, notifications and Shorts
  • Start with three settings: turn off autoplay, turn off watch history and cut back notifications. It takes five minutes
  • The Shorts limit and the break and bedtime reminders only notify you. If you ignore them, you can keep watching
  • To really limit your time, use Screen Time on iPhone or Digital Wellbeing on Android, and decide in advance when you will watch

How widely YouTube is used

In a fiscal 2025 survey by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) of 1,800 people aged 13 to 79 in Japan, 81.5% of people across all ages used YouTube. It is the most used of all video-sharing services. By age group, usage is over 90% among people in their teens through their 40s (see the chart below). For many people, YouTube is not a special app but one of the tools they open every day. That is exactly why viewing time grows without you noticing.

Four features that lead to watching too much

1. Autoplay: when one ends, the next begins

With autoplay on, another related video starts automatically when the current one ends. It keeps going without you deciding to watch the next one, so it never stops unless you decide to stop.

2. Home-feed recommendations: something you want to watch, every time you open it

The home feed is filled with recommendations based on your watch history. Even if you opened the app to find a specific video, the recommendations are what you see first.

3. Notifications: pulled back even when you're not watching

Besides new videos from channels you subscribe to, you also get notifications for "recommended videos" from channels you don't subscribe to. Notifications give you a reason to open the app at times you had no plan to.

4. Shorts: vertical videos with no natural stopping point

Inside YouTube is Shorts, a stream of vertical videos up to three minutes long that play one after another. You came to watch a long video, but once you enter Shorts, there are no breaks. Why vertical videos are so hard to stop is covered in detail in why you can't stop watching short videos, and how to step away. This article sticks to what you can do inside the YouTube app.

Settings to change today: five, in order of impact

Work from the top down and the triggers that keep you watching will drop away one by one. The menu names are from the YouTube mobile app (iPhone and Android) and follow YouTube's official help (as of October 2026). App updates can move things around, so if you can't find a setting, look inside the app's Settings.

1. Turn off autoplay

  1. Open the watch screen for a video
  2. Tap the "Autoplay" toggle at the top of the video player to turn it off

You can also switch it by tapping your profile picture, then the settings icon at the top right, and opening "Autoplay." On a computer, turn it off with the autoplay toggle under the video player. Autoplay is set separately on each device, so if you watch on both your phone and a computer, turn it off on both. Autoplay is off by default for accounts of users aged 13 to 17.

2. Turn off watch history

  1. Tap your profile picture, then go to "Settings" and open "Manage all history"
  2. Open the "Manage" tab
  3. Uncheck "Include your YouTube watch history"

Videos you watch while watch history is off are not saved to your history. YouTube's official help explains that if you have little recent watch history and have chosen not to receive recommendations based on it, features such as recommendations on the home page are turned off. When you open the app, you start by searching for the video you want. This is the single biggest change.

If you also want to clear your existing history, go to the same "Manage all history" screen and remove videos one at a time, or use "Delete" to clear them in bulk. If you choose a time range with "Delete," your search history for that range is cleared too. With history off, you won't be able to find videos you watched during that time in your history later. In return, YouTube becomes an app where you decide what to watch.

3. Cut back notifications

  1. Tap your profile picture, then open "Settings" → "Notifications"
  2. Turn off "Recommended videos." This stops recommendation notifications from channels you don't subscribe to
  3. For every channel except the ones whose new videos you really wait for, tap the bell next to "Subscribed" and choose "None"

To review all your subscriptions at once, open "Subscriptions" at the top of the home screen, then "All" at the top right, where you can change the bell for each channel.

If you want to stop everything, turn off YouTube notifications entirely in your phone's settings.

  • iPhone: "Settings" → "Notifications" → "YouTube" (under "Notification Style") → turn off "Allow Notifications"
  • Android: "Settings" → "Notifications" → "App notifications" → "YouTube," then turn notifications off (if it isn't listed, look under "All apps"; names vary slightly by phone model)

To catch new videos you want to see, it's enough to check your subscriptions when you open the app yourself.

4. Put a limit on Shorts

  1. Open the "You" tab and tap "Settings"
  2. Open "Time management"
  3. Tap "Shorts feed limit" and choose a daily limit. You can even set it to 0

When you reach the limit, a notice appears and you can choose to close the Shorts feed or ignore the limit and keep watching. In other words, the limit does not stop you; it makes you notice. If you tend to ignore it, it's more reliable to limit YouTube time across your whole phone, as described in the next section.

5. Turn on break and bedtime reminders

  1. Tap your profile picture, then open "Settings" → "General"
  2. Turn on "Remind me to take a break" and choose how often
  3. Turn on "Remind me when it's bedtime" and choose a start and end time

The official help says both are under "Settings" → "General." You can also set them by tapping your profile picture and opening "Time watched." If you can't find them, also check "Time management," where the Shorts limit is.

When a break reminder appears, the video pauses until you dismiss it or resume. The bedtime reminder tells you "it's time for bed" at the time you set. If you turn on "Wait until I finish my video to show reminder," it waits until the video you're watching ends. Both are features of the YouTube app on phones and tablets, and are on by default for accounts of users aged 13 to 17.

The key point is that both only notify you. You can dismiss a break reminder and carry on right away, and with the bedtime reminder you can keep watching by dismissing it or choosing "Snooze" (which reminds you again 15 minutes later). Reminders are settings for noticing, not for stopping.

Limit YouTube time across your whole phone

Every setting inside the YouTube app can be switched back by you in a moment. If you really want to limit your time, use your phone's own features.

  • iPhone (iOS 27): In "Settings" → "Screen Time" → "Allowances," set how much time you can spend each day on categories such as "Entertainment." You can see which apps are in which category under "Manage Categories." If you set a four-digit passcode under "Manage Screen Time" → "Lock Screen Time Settings," you will need the passcode to change the settings
  • iPhone (up to iOS 26): In "Settings" → "Screen Time" → "App Limits" → "Add Limit," choose YouTube and set a daily time. Apple explains that with the default settings, you can ignore the limit when you reach it
  • Android: In "Settings" → "Digital Wellbeing" → "App timers," tap the timer icon next to YouTube and choose a time. When you reach the limit, the app closes and its icon turns gray. Timers reset at midnight. If you set a four-digit PIN under "Digital Wellbeing" → "App limits" → "App limits PIN," no one else can change it (steps vary slightly by phone model)

In every case, if you know the passcode or PIN yourself, you can turn it off yourself. For ways to make limits harder to undo, see for people who keep switching off their own Screen Time limits. Also, if you deleted the app and started watching YouTube in a browser, limit your browser time too. On Android, if you select Chrome in the Digital Wellbeing chart, you can set a daily time for each website.

Three habits that make the settings stick

1. Decide what to watch before you open it

Decide before you open the app: "one video on how to cook this dish," or "just this week's episode from this channel." With history off and no home-feed recommendations, all you do is search for that one video and close the app when it ends.

2. Decide when you will watch

Decide in advance when you watch YouTube, such as "30 minutes after dinner" or "weekend mornings." A time slot is easier to keep to than a daily total. Don't make the time before bed one of your YouTube slots.

3. Before bed and while working, keep your phone in another room

Overwatching happens most in bed and in the gaps between studying or working. In those two situations, put the phone itself out of reach. If you want to step away for a whole day, see how to do a digital detox.
