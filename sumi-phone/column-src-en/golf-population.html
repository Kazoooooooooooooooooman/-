How many people in Japan play golf today? In the government's Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities, the share of people aged 10 and over who played golf (including driving ranges) in the past year fell from 11.1% in 2001 to 6.9% in 2021. That is a drop of about 40% in 20 years. This summary draws on government statistics, a Sasakawa Sports Foundation survey and figures from the "White Paper on Leisure."

The short version

  • The share who played golf (including driving ranges) keeps falling: 11.1% in 2001, 8.1% in 2011 and 6.9% in 2021
  • Among people aged 15 and over, the peak was 17.8% (17.84 million people) in 1991
  • In 2021 the rate was 11.7% for men and 2.3% for women. Both have been slowly falling

How the share of golfers has changed

The Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities is a large survey the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC) runs every five years. It asks which sports people did in the past year. "Golf" includes people who only hit balls at a driving range, not just those who played a course.

Applied to the population aged 10 and over (about 112.46 million), 6.9% in 2021 works out to about 7.76 million people (our own calculation). In the 2021 survey, golf was the seventh most common sport (ranking of the sports people in Japan play).

The peak was 1991

Older figures exist for people aged 15 and over. In 1991, 17.8%, an estimated 17.84 million people, played golf. The share then fell to 14.4% (15.04 million) in 1996 and 11.5% (12.25 million) in 2001.

The Japan Productivity Center's "White Paper on Leisure" shows a similar trend. According to figures cited in news reports and company materials, the number of people who played on a golf course peaked at 14.8 million in 1992, then stood at 6.7 million in fiscal 2018 and about 5.3 million in 2023.

By sex and age

In 2021, 11.7% of men and 2.3% of women played golf. For men that is down from 15.2% in 2006, and for women from 3.0% in 2006.

In the 2016 survey, which has a full age breakdown, the rate was highest among people aged 50 to 54 and 55 to 59, both at 10.9%. It was still about 8% to 10% among people in their 60s and early 70s. Golf is more common among middle-aged and older people than among the young.

Recent trends

In the Sasakawa Sports Foundation's 2024 survey (preliminary results), 6.9% of people aged 18 and over, an estimated 7.23 million, played "golf (course)" at least once in the year. "Golf (driving range)" was 6.8%, or an estimated 7.12 million. Course golf ranked sixth, after strolling, walking, light exercise, strength training and jogging/running.

The Japan Productivity Center's "White Paper on Leisure 2025" says of the 2024 market that "driving ranges were flat, but golf courses turned negative."

Reading the numbers

  • The 2021 Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities was carried out in October 2021, during the COVID-19 period
  • "Golf" in that survey includes driving ranges. From the 2021 survey, "ground golf" (a simplified form of golf) is a separate item (1.7%) and is not counted as golf
  • The table up to 2001 covers people aged 15 and over; the trend from 2001 covers people aged 10 and over. The age ranges differ, so don't compare them directly
  • The Sasakawa Sports Foundation's 2024 figures are preliminary and come from a survey of 3,000 people aged 18 and over across Japan
  • The White Paper on Leisure figures are taken from news reports and other companies' materials that cite it

FAQ

How many people play golf in Japan?

It depends on the survey. The Sasakawa Sports Foundation's 2024 survey estimates that 7.23 million people aged 18 and over played on a course. In MIC's 2021 survey, 6.9% of people aged 10 and over played golf, including driving ranges.

Is the number of golfers rising or falling?

Over the long run, it is falling. In the Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities, the peak among people aged 15 and over was 17.8% in 1991. Among people aged 10 and over, the rate fell from 11.1% in 2001 to 6.9% in 2021.

Summary

  • The share of people aged 10 and over who played golf (including driving ranges) fell from 11.1% in 2001 to 6.9% in 2021
  • Among people aged 15 and over, the peak was 17.84 million in 1991
  • Still, an estimated 7.23 million people played on a course in 2024, and golf has become a sport played mostly by middle-aged and older people
