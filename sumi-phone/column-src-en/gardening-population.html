Growing flowers, planting vegetables, pulling weeds in the yard. In 2021, 26.0% of people in Japan aged 10 and over did this kind of gardening at least once in the year. That is roughly one in four. The older the age group, the higher the rate: among 70 to 74-year-olds it reaches 43.1%. People in their 30s to 50s, on the other hand, garden far less than they did 35 years ago. This summary is based mainly on the Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC), together with the Japan Productivity Center's Leisure White Paper.

The short version

  • 26.0% of people did gardening in 2021, making it the 7th most common hobby or pastime in the survey
  • The rate rises with age and passes 40% in the late 60s and early 70s. More women (31.4%) garden than men (20.3%)
  • Among people 15 and over, the rate fell from 34.8% in 1991 to 26.9% in 2021, mainly because fewer people in their 30s to 50s garden

Gardening is flat to slightly down

The Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities is carried out every five years. Among people aged 15 and over, the rate was highest in 1991 at 34.8% and dropped between 2006 and 2016. In 2021 it was 26.9%, about the same as in 2016. For everyone aged 10 and over, it went from 25.7% in 2016 to 26.0% in 2021 (+0.3 points).

The "past year" asked about in the 2021 survey ran from October 20, 2020 to October 19, 2021, a period affected by COVID-19. In the same survey, travel and karaoke fell sharply, but gardening, which you can do around your own home, barely dropped. For how it compares with other hobbies, see Japan's most popular hobbies.

By age: highest in the late 60s and early 70s

The rate is under 5% for people in their late teens and early 20s, starts rising in the 30s, and reaches 41.3% at 65 to 69 and 43.1% at 70 to 74. It falls back to 36.0% for people aged 75 and over.

Women garden more than men in every age group, and among women in their late 60s it is almost one in two. People who gardened did so on 62.8 days a year on average (57.0 days for men, 66.4 for women). That is more than once a week.

In 1986, more than 40% of people in their 40s and 50s gardened. By 2021 the rate had fallen a lot among people in their 30s to 50s, while the 70-and-over group stayed about the same. One likely reason the overall figure has not changed much is that the older generations, who garden the most, make up a growing share of the population.

What the Leisure White Paper shows

The Japan Productivity Center's Leisure White Paper surveys people aged 15 to 79 (the preliminary 2026 edition was an online survey of 3,279 people). Its estimate of people who did "gardening, yard work" fell from 21.1 million in 2023 (14th place) to 19.8 million in 2024 (15th place). In 2025 the participation rate was 19.8%, and participants gardened 39.7 times a year on average and spent an average of 17,800 yen on it.

Because the age range and survey method differ from the Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities (26.0% of people aged 10 and over), the two numbers cannot be compared directly. Both surveys, though, show gardening as an everyday hobby on the scale of tens of millions of people.

FAQ

How many people in Japan garden?

In MIC's Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities (2021), 26.0% of people aged 10 and over did gardening at least once in the year. Multiplied by the estimated population aged 10 and over (112.46 million), that is about 29 million people. The Leisure White Paper puts the number of gardeners aged 15 to 79 at 19.8 million in 2024.

Which age group gardens the most?

In 2021 the highest rate was among 70 to 74-year-olds at 43.1%, followed by 65 to 69-year-olds at 41.3%. Among women aged 65 to 69 it was 49.4%.

Summary

  • 26.0% of people aged 10 and over did gardening in 2021
  • The rate rises with age, reaching 43.1% at 70 to 74, and is higher for women
  • It has fallen sharply among people in their 30s to 50s over 35 years, so gardening is now centered on older generations
