Denmark has been working to take phones out of primary and lower secondary schools and after-school clubs. A government commission made the recommendation in February 2025, and in September eight parties agreed to make it a legal requirement. But the bill was never passed before the general election in March 2026. Then, in September 2026, the government announced a "total ban" plan. What is law, and what is still only a plan? We sorted it out from Danish government announcements and other sources.

Trivsel is a Danish word for feeling well in body and mind and getting through each day in good shape. The commission had 11 experts and looked at children and young people aged 0 to 25.

The commission's recommendations (February 2025)

The commission published its report on February 25, 2025, with 35 recommendations. One of them is "smartphone-free primary and lower secondary schools and upper secondary education."

  • Make primary and lower secondary schools phone-free, including after-school clubs (called SFO or fritidshjem in Danish)
  • Have upper secondary schools and colleges set rules for screen use
  • At the same time, teach children to use digital technology well

Another recommendation calls for filters on school networks that block websites unrelated to learning. But recommendations have no legal force.

The eight-party deal (September 2025)

On September 30, 2025, the three governing parties (the Social Democrats, Venstre and the Moderates) reached an agreement with the Socialist People's Party, Liberal Alliance, the Conservative People's Party, the Danish People's Party and the Social Liberals. According to the Ministry of Children and Education, it said:

  • The law would require school boards, after-school parent boards and others to set a "local policy" making schools and after-school programs phone-free
  • It covers smartphones and other private devices that connect to the internet. Computers and tablets used in lessons are not included
  • Devices can still be used for health reasons (for example, measuring blood sugar for a student with diabetes) and to support students with disabilities (for example, dyslexia)
  • Schools can also set their own exceptions for teaching or practical reasons. The ministry would draft a model policy
  • Schools would also be required to install filters on their networks that block sites such as gaming, streaming, shopping and social media

Eurydice, the European Commission's education information network, reported that this was meant to start with the 2026–27 school year. But this was an agreement to pass a law; at that point, it was not yet law. According to press reports, the agreement also said that "a total ban would not be appropriate."

The election and the "total ban" plan (2026)

Denmark held a general election on March 24, 2026. According to the news agency Ritzau, the bill based on the agreement was never passed by parliament, because the prime minister called the election.

On September 10, 2026, at a press conference held after historically poor PISA results (an international student assessment), Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen announced a plan for a "total ban" on phones and other private screens in public primary and lower secondary schools (folkeskoler) and independent schools. It would cover the schoolyard as well as the classroom. Education Minister Magnus Heunicke said exceptions such as blood sugar monitoring would remain.

How this "total ban" will differ from the 2025 agreement has not yet been spelled out. When we checked the news from the Ministry of Children and Education (now the Ministry of Education) on November 14, 2026, we found no new bill.

Reading the numbers and reports with care

  • When the deal was struck in September 2025, some reports said parliament had "passed" a ban. In fact it was an agreement between parties, and no law was passed
  • When the agreement was made, the head of the school leaders' association said that "the vast majority of schools already have a ban or a phone-free policy" (according to press reports). Even without a national law, many schools have their own rules
  • The finding that "over half of 6th and 8th graders are distracted by screens in class" is survey research cited by Eurydice, which does not give details of the survey method

Finland, another Nordic country, changed its law to ban phones in class as a general rule (Finland's school phone law). The Netherlands started with a national agreement rather than a law (the Dutch classroom phone ban). Each country is taking its own path.

FAQ

Are phones banned by law in Danish schools?

As of November 14, 2026, we could not confirm that a national law banning them has been passed. In September 2025, eight parties agreed to make it a legal requirement, but the bill was never passed before the general election in March 2026. In September 2026, the government announced a new "total ban" plan. Many schools already have their own rules.

Are after-school clubs included?

The commission's recommendations in February 2025 and the agreement in September 2025 both included after-school clubs (SFO and fritidshjem). The "total ban" plan of September 2026 mentions classrooms and schoolyards in primary and lower secondary schools; how after-school clubs will be handled has not been made clear yet.
