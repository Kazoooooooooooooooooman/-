A sento (Japanese public bath) or sauna is one of the few places where you cannot bring your phone. The moment it goes into the locker, your time without notifications or screens begins. But if it is your first time, you may feel a little unsure: "What should I bring?" "What order do I do things in?" This article explains what to bring, how it all works, and what to keep in mind, so first-timers will not get lost.

The short version

  • Two towels and some coins or a transit IC card (Japan's tap-to-pay train cards) are all you need. Many places provide soap and shampoo
  • First-timers tend to get stuck on the shoe locker key, the rinse before bathing, and how to wash off sweat after the sauna. Get these right and you will be fine
  • The most important rule of etiquette is to wash your body before getting into the bath
  • Do not push yourself in the sauna. Get out before it becomes uncomfortable
  • Leave your phone in the locker. That alone gives you an hour away from the screen

Prices, extra charges for the sauna, and towel rental vary from place to place. Check the website or the sign at the entrance before you go.

Where first-timers get stuck

  • The shoe locker key: take off your shoes at the entrance and put them in a shoe locker. At many places you pull out a wooden tag or key and take it with you; sometimes you hand it in at the front desk
  • Tell the front desk if you want the sauna: some places charge extra for the sauna. You may be given a sauna towel or a wristband
  • Wear the locker key on your wrist or ankle: put your phone and wallet in the locker and take only the key with you
  • Rinse off and wash before the bath: wash your hair and body at the washing area before getting into the bath. This is the most important rule of etiquette
  • After the sauna, wash off the sweat before the cold plunge: the rule is not to get into the cold bath while still sweaty
  • Dry off before going back to the changing room: give yourself a quick wipe in the washing area first so the floor does not get wet

Things to keep in mind

  • Do not put your towel in the bath: this is etiquette for sharing the water with everyone
  • If you have long hair, tie it up: keep your hair out of the water
  • Do not splash water on others at the washing area: sit down and keep the shower low
  • Put stools and buckets back where they were: give them a quick rinse first
  • Do not take photos: that is exactly why you leave your phone in the changing room
  • Do not bathe after drinking alcohol: it puts a strain on your body

A gentle way to enjoy the sauna

The routine often recommended is to repeat "sauna, cold plunge, rest." There are no rules about how long or how many times, though. If it is your first time, start with something like this.

  1. Sauna: dry off before you go in. The lower benches are less hot. Get out before it becomes uncomfortable
  2. Wash off the sweat: rinse off the sweat with a scoop of water or a shower, then go to the cold bath
  3. Cold plunge: if you do not like the cold, just your feet or a short time is fine. A lukewarm shower is enough too
  4. Rest: sit on a chair and rest slowly. Many people feel their head clear during this time

If you have concerns about your heart or blood pressure, or on days when you are not feeling well, do not push yourself. If you start to feel unwell, get out right away and let someone nearby or a staff member know. Drink water often, before you go in and after you come out.

An hour without your phone

At a sento or sauna, you cannot look at your phone. You may feel a bit at a loss at first, but as you soak in the bath and gaze idly at the ceiling, the urge to find "what to look at next" fades away.

  • Do not check it as soon as you get out: drink some water in the rest area and leave your phone in the locker for just 10 more minutes
  • Choose a sento within walking distance: the walk there and back also helps you switch off (the benefits of walking)
  • Put it in your plans for your day off: deciding "sento in the morning" makes it harder for your day off to disappear into your phone (how not to lose your day off to your phone)
