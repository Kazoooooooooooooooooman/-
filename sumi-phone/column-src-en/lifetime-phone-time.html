165 minutes a day. That is the average time people aged 15 to 69 in Tokyo spend on their smartphones or mobile phones. It looks like only 2 hours 45 minutes, but keep it up every day from age 13 to 80 and the total comes to about 7.7 years. That is roughly one-sixth of your waking hours. We used figures from published surveys to convert it into the length of a lifetime.

The short version

  • At 165 minutes a day, the 67 years from age 13 to 80 add up to about 7.7 years
  • Counting all internet use (183.9 minutes on weekdays), it is about 8.6 years
  • Cut just one hour a day, and you get back about 2.8 years over a lifetime

How we calculated it

It only takes multiplication.

  • Daily use: 165 minutes (daily time spent on mobile phones and smartphones in Hakuhodo DY Media Partners' "Media Teiten Survey 2025")
  • Period of use: the 67 years from age 13 to 80 (assuming you get a smartphone in middle school)
  • 165 minutes ÷ 1,440 minutes (one day) × 67 years = about 7.7 years

In hours, 7.7 years is about 67,000 hours. That works out to spending in front of a screen about as long as it takes a newborn to reach second grade.

Measured against waking hours: about one-sixth

People in Japan aged 10 and over sleep an average of 7 hours 54 minutes (Statistics Bureau of Japan, 2021 Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities). Subtract sleep from the day, and you are awake for about 16 hours 6 minutes. Of that, 165 minutes go to the phone: about 17% of waking hours, or about one-sixth.

  • Waking hours: 24 hours − 7 hours 54 minutes = about 16 hours 6 minutes (966 minutes)
  • 165 minutes ÷ 966 minutes = about 17%

One hour less a day means about 2.8 years over a lifetime

Looked at the other way, cutting your phone use by just one hour a day gives you back about 2.8 years over 67 years. Even 30 minutes gives you about 1.4 years. That is more than the four years of a university degree, available for reading, seeing people and walking outside.

Things to keep in mind about these numbers

  • The 165 minutes is the average from a survey of people aged 15 to 69 in Tokyo. It is not an average for all ages across Japan
  • In reality, the time people spend varies a lot by age. It tends to be longer when you are young and shorter as you get older, so a calculation that assumes the same time for all 67 years is only a rough guide
  • The figure includes time spent on work and study. It does not mean "wasted time"

FAQ

How do you calculate how much of your life you spend on your phone?

Divide your daily use (in minutes) by 1,440 minutes, then multiply by the number of years you will use it. At 165 minutes a day for 67 years, 165 ÷ 1,440 × 67 = about 7.7 years.

How can I work out my own number of years?

On iPhone, go to Settings → Screen Time; on Android, go to Settings → Digital Wellbeing. You can see your average daily use there. Then look up that time in the table above.

How much do I need to cut each day to make a difference?

Thirty minutes a day is about 1.4 years over a lifetime, and one hour is about 2.8 years. An easy way to start is to pick one situation, such as before bed or while traveling, and cut back there.
