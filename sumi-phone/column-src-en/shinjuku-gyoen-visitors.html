Gardens in central Tokyo are drawing more people than ever. Shinjuku Gyoen (a former imperial garden in Shinjuku, Tokyo) had 2,806,273 visitors (about 2.81 million) in fiscal 2024, a new record. Visitor numbers fell to about 810,000 in fiscal 2020 because of COVID, and have since more than tripled. In fiscal 2025, the garden passed 3 million for the first time. This summary is based on the Ministry of the Environment's Survey of Natural Park Visitors and its press releases. (Japan's fiscal year runs April–March.)

The short version

  • Shinjuku Gyoen set a new visitor record every year from fiscal 2023 to fiscal 2025
  • The Ministry of the Environment estimates that about half of visitors are inbound tourists from overseas
  • Compared with fiscal 2014, ten years earlier (about 1.5 million), the number has nearly doubled

Until the early 2010s, the garden had about 1 to 1.5 million visitors a year. Numbers began rising sharply in fiscal 2015, and in fiscal 2017 reached 2,502,561, the highest on record at the time. The Ministry of the Environment gave two reasons for that jump: more visitors from overseas, and cherry blossoms that bloomed more than a week earlier than usual.

Down to 810,000 during COVID, then more than tripled

In fiscal 2020, as COVID spread, visitors fell to 814,514, just 37.2% of the previous year. In fiscal 2021 the garden closed temporarily from April 25 to June 3, 2021, and after reopening it limited entry to 2,000 people per hour.

After that, numbers recovered quickly. In fiscal 2023 the garden had 2,506,000 visitors, just above the fiscal 2017 record. Fiscal 2024 brought 2,806,273, a record for the second year in a row. According to a Ministry of the Environment announcement in August 2026, the garden set a new record three years running from fiscal 2023 to fiscal 2025, and passed 3 million visitors for the first time in fiscal 2025. About half of visitors are estimated to be tourists from overseas.

What about the Imperial Palace Outer Garden and Kyoto Gyoen?

Shinjuku Gyoen, the Kokyo Gaien (Imperial Palace Outer Garden) and Kyoto Gyoen were all once imperial gardens. After the Second World War they were opened to everyone as "national gardens" (kokumin koen), and today they are managed by the Ministry of the Environment.

Before COVID, in fiscal 2019, the Kokyo Gaien area had about 7.85 million visitors and Kitanomaru Park about 2.56 million. Visitors to the Kokyo Gaien had reportedly risen every year from 3.68 million in fiscal 2012. In fiscal 2023, the Kokyo Gaien area was still below half its pre-COVID level.

A 2017 Ministry of the Environment document said the national gardens and related sites received more than 12 million visitors a year, and that about half were expected to be from overseas.

Reading the numbers

  • The Shinjuku Gyoen figures are visitor numbers reported by its management office. The Kokyo Gaien and Kyoto Gyoen figures are described as "estimates" even in Ministry of the Environment documents. The years and counting methods differ, so the three cannot be compared on the same scale
  • Shinjuku Gyoen figures are by fiscal year (April to March). The fiscal 2023 figure of 2,506,000 is given to the nearest thousand
  • For fiscal 2025, the ministry has only said that visitors "passed 3 million"; we have not yet found an exact figure
  • Changes in admission fees (such as in fiscal 2019), the timing of the cherry blossoms and the weather all affect the yearly numbers

FAQ

How many people visit Shinjuku Gyoen each year?

In fiscal 2024, 2,806,273 people (about 2.81 million) visited, a record high. The Ministry of the Environment has announced that visitors passed 3 million for the first time in fiscal 2025.

Are most Shinjuku Gyoen visitors from overseas?

The Ministry of the Environment estimates that about half of visitors are tourists from overseas. When the garden set a record in fiscal 2017, the ministry also named the rise in overseas visitors as one of the reasons.

Summary

  • Shinjuku Gyoen had a record of about 2.81 million visitors in fiscal 2024, and passed 3 million in fiscal 2025
  • Visitors fell to about 810,000 in fiscal 2020 because of COVID, then more than tripled in four years
  • More and more people, from Japan and abroad, want time walking among the greenery of central Tokyo
