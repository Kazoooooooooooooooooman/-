Among fifth graders, 71.7% of boys and 73.6% of girls slept 8 hours or more a day. But by the second year of junior high (eighth grade), that falls to 37.5% of boys and 27.7% of girls. Japan's national sleep guide recommends 8 to 10 hours of sleep for junior high and high school students, so most junior high students are not getting enough. This summary is based on a national survey by the Japan Sports Agency.

The short version

  • Children sleeping 8 hours or more: 71.7% of fifth-grade boys, 73.6% of fifth-grade girls, 37.5% of eighth-grade boys, 27.7% of eighth-grade girls
  • Children sleeping less than 6 hours: 8.9% of eighth-grade girls, 6.3% of eighth-grade boys
  • The national guide recommends 9 to 12 hours for elementary students and 8 to 10 hours for junior high and high school students

Share of children sleeping 8 hours or more

In the three years from fifth grade to eighth grade, the share of children sleeping 8 hours or more falls by more than half. Among eighth-grade girls, nearly 3 in 4 fall short of 8 hours.

Changes since fiscal 2019

Compared with fiscal 2019, the share sleeping 8 hours or more rose from 28.4% to 37.5% among eighth-grade boys and from 22.6% to 27.7% among eighth-grade girls. The Japan Sports Agency also describes a rising trend for both boys and girls in elementary and junior high school. Even so, most junior high students still fall short of 8 hours.

How much sleep is enough?

The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's "Sleep Guide for Health Promotion 2023" gives these guidelines by age:

  • Elementary students: 9 to 12 hours
  • Junior high and high school students: 8 to 10 hours
  • Adults: get the sleep you need, with 6 hours or more as a guideline

The survey's top category is "8 hours or more," so it does not tell us how many elementary students sleep 9 hours or more. Since the guideline for elementary students starts at 9 hours, the "8 hours or more" group includes children who are still not getting enough.

How to win back sleep

  • Work back from bedtime to decide when screens go away: in the same survey, about half of junior high students spend 3 hours or more on screens on weekdays outside of study (screen time data)
  • Charge the phone outside the bedroom: if it is within reach, it is hard not to check notifications
  • Get morning light: the sleep guide also recommends getting sunlight after you wake up
