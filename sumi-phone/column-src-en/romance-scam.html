In a "social media romance scam", someone you meet on social media or a dating app, and have never met in person, gets you to feel love or closeness toward them and then cheats you out of money. In 2025 there were 5,645 such cases in Japan, with losses of 54.64 billion yen. Compared with the year before, the number of cases rose 47.6% and losses rose 36.3%. This summary is based on final figures from the National Police Agency.

The short version

  • 5,645 social media romance scam cases (3,824 the year before), with losses of 54.64 billion yen (40.09 billion yen the year before)
  • In 92.8% of cases the first contact was a direct message. Platforms: dating apps 32.7%, Instagram 22.8%, Facebook 18.6%
  • Scams in which victims were told to send crypto assets reached 2,177 cases, about 2.8 times the year before

Cases and losses

The 2024 figure was calculated from the 2025 figure and the increase. About 15 cases a day are being reported to the police.

Where victims first made contact

In almost every case, the other person sends the first message (direct messages account for 92.8%). Profile photos and job titles are not necessarily real.

Who is being targeted

  • Men: 3,633 cases and 32.87 billion yen. Women: 2,012 cases and 21.77 billion yen. Men account for about 60% of both cases and losses
  • People in their 40s to 60s make up 75.6% of all victims

How the money is handed over is changing

  • Scams in which victims were told to send crypto assets: 2,177 cases and 24.77 billion yen. The number of cases was about 2.8 times the year before (+177.0%)
  • Including bank transfers to crypto asset accounts, money was effectively handed over in crypto assets in 40.4% of cases, accounting for 48.6% of losses

Warning signs

  • There is always a reason you cannot meet: they work overseas, they are busy with work, and plans to meet keep getting pushed back
  • Money or investing comes up: they urge you to send money or buy crypto assets "for our future together" or so "we can grow it together"
  • Tell someone: if you talk to family or friends, someone looking from the outside can spot what is wrong. In Japan, the police consultation line is #9110
