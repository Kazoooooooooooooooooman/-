On January 1, 2024, the Netherlands started a national agreement that phones, tablets and smartwatches are not used in the classrooms of secondary schools (VO, the equivalent of junior high and high school). It was extended to primary and special schools from the 2024–2025 school year. In a 2025 study commissioned by the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science, 75% of secondary schools said the agreement had a positive effect on students' concentration. This summary is based on government announcements and the study report.

The short version

  • The Netherlands took phones out of classrooms not by law but through an "agreement" between the government and representatives of students, parents, teachers and schools
  • 75% of secondary schools reported a positive effect on concentration, and 59% on the social climate
  • But some also report a heavier workload for teachers enforcing the rules and more bullying and other trouble

What is the rule?

The agreement is built on the idea of "no, unless": no devices in class, with exceptions. Phones are allowed when they are truly needed for the lesson (for example, a media literacy class), and students who need their phone for medical reasons or a disability may use it. Each school sets its detailed rules in consultation with teachers, parents and students.

The agreement is not a legal ban. In July 2024, the Dutch government said it would review with all parties in summer 2025 whether further measures, such as a legal ban, were needed.

What the 2025 study found

The study was carried out by the Kohnstamm Instituut and Oberon for the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science. It combined questionnaires for the staff responsible for each school's policy with group interviews with teachers, support staff, students and parents. In secondary education, 317 schools answered the questionnaire. The government describes the study as covering more than 800 primary, secondary and special schools.

  • The share of secondary schools that see non-educational phone use as a problem fell from 62% before the agreement to 29%
  • Not a single secondary school reported a negative effect on concentration
  • Teachers say lessons are calmer and students talk with each other more during breaks. Some also say the school feels safer because unwanted photos are no longer being taken

In primary schools, few children brought phones to school in the first place, and most schools saw no effect on concentration or results. Even so, 23% reported a positive effect on children's well-being and on the school and classroom climate.

The challenges

  • Enforcing the rules has added to teachers' workload
  • Some teachers have noticed more physical bullying and disruptive behavior
  • Only 49% of secondary schools say enforcing the policy takes little or no effort
  • Smartwatches are harder to spot, and more children are bringing them (primary schools)

Schools say one key to success is offering alternatives to phones during breaks, such as sports, musical instruments or board games.

What sets the Dutch approach apart

Other countries are working on school phone rules too (France's "digital pause"). What stands out in the Netherlands is that it started with an agreement among the people involved rather than a law, and that the government then studied the results and published them. Families can do the same at home: set a rule, then look back at how it went.

Reading the numbers

  • A "positive effect" means the person responsible at the school felt there was one. It was not measured through test scores or similar data
  • The secondary school questionnaire ran from October 8 to November 14, 2024, and the primary and special school questionnaire from January 14 to February 14, 2025. The report describes the situation after a little more than half a school year under the agreement
  • Reuters reported that "nearly two-thirds" saw a better social climate, but the figure in the report is 59%

FAQ

Are phones banned in Dutch schools?

Since January 2024, a national agreement has kept phones, tablets and smartwatches out of use in secondary school classrooms. Primary and special schools have been covered since the 2024–2025 school year. It is not a legal ban, and phones may be used when needed for a lesson or for medical reasons.

Did the phone ban improve concentration?

In the 2025 government-commissioned study, 75% of secondary schools said it had a positive effect on students' concentration. But these are the schools' own impressions, not a direct measurement of learning.

Summary
