In 2025, there were about 6.82 million pet dogs in Japan. That is down from about 8.71 million in 2013, a drop of about 1.9 million in 12 years. Meanwhile, studies in the UK and Japan find that people who own dogs walk more and are more likely to meet exercise guidelines. This summary is based on the Japan Pet Food Association's survey and peer-reviewed research.

The short version

  • The number of pet dogs has fallen since 2013, to about 6.82 million in 2025 (Japan Pet Food Association)
  • But households now keep more dogs each, and the number has leveled off in recent years
  • In research, dog owners spend more time walking because of dog walks, and do no less of other exercise

The share of households with a dog fell from 12.85% to 8.50% over 12 years. But because the average number of dogs per household rose from 1.24 to 1.36, the 2025 total was slightly higher than the year before. The Japan Pet Food Association describes the number of dogs as "leveling off."

For comparison, there were about 8.85 million pet cats (8,847,000) in 2025. Cats have continued to outnumber dogs. The average lifespan of dogs was 14.82 years (2025).

How much do dog owners walk?

A research team from the University of Liverpool and other institutions surveyed adults and children in 385 households in the same area of the UK (survey in 2015, published in Scientific Reports in 2019). They compared 191 adults who owned dogs with 455 who did not.

  • Dog owners walked their dogs 7 times a week, for a total of 220 minutes (median)
  • 64% of dog owners met the exercise guideline (150 minutes a week) through dog walking alone
  • After accounting for factors such as age, dog owners had about four times (4.10 times) the odds of meeting the guideline
  • The amount of exercise other than dog walking did not differ between owners and non-owners

That last point matters. Dog walking was not replacing other exercise; it was added on top. Children with dogs also reported more minutes of walking and free-time play.

Studies in Japan

A team from Waseda University and other institutions surveyed 1,926 people aged 65 to 74 (survey in 2010, published in the American Journal of Preventive Medicine in 2012).

  • 14.0% owned a dog, and 71% of them walked their dog
  • They walked their dogs for an average of 308.5 minutes a week
  • Dog walkers walked 508.0 minutes a week in total, more than people without dogs (384.5 minutes a week)

A team including the Tokyo Metropolitan Institute of Gerontology followed about 11,000 people aged 65 and over for about 3.5 to 4 years. Current dog owners had an odds ratio of 0.54 for becoming disabled (needing long-term care) compared with people who had never owned a dog (PLoS ONE, 2022; a 2024 correction states that death was also counted in this outcome). In a separate analysis, the odds ratio for disabling dementia was 0.60 (Preventive Medicine Reports, 2023). In both studies, the results were even lower for dog owners who also exercised regularly.

What to keep in mind when reading the numbers

  • The number of dogs is a national estimate based on an online survey of about 50,000 people aged 20 to 79. The estimation formula changed in 2021
  • All the studies here are observational, comparing people who own dogs with people who do not. They cannot fully separate whether dogs make people healthier or whether healthier people are more likely to get a dog
  • The UK and Japanese studies measured exercise from what people reported in questionnaires

FAQ

How many pet dogs are there in Japan?

The Japan Pet Food Association's National Dog and Cat Ownership Survey (2025) estimates about 6.82 million (6,820,000). 8.50% of households had a dog.

Does owning a dog increase how much you exercise?

In a UK study (2019), dog owners had about four times the odds of meeting the exercise guideline (150 minutes a week) compared with non-owners. Dog walks were added on top of other exercise. Because it is an observational study, however, it does not prove that getting a dog will always make you more active.

Summary

  • Pet dogs in Japan fell from about 8.71 million in 2013 to about 6.82 million in 2025
  • Dog owners tend to spend more time walking, thanks to dog walks
  • Having a reason to go outside every day likely helps support a walking habit
