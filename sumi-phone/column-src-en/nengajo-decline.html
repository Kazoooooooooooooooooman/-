In Japan, many people traditionally mail New Year greeting postcards (nengajo) to friends, relatives and colleagues. For 2027, Japan Post's initial print run is 555.93 million postcards. For 2025 it was about 1.07 billion, so it has almost halved in two years. More and more people now send their New Year greetings by LINE (Japan's most-used messaging app) or social media instead. This summary is based on Japan Post's announcements.

The short version

  • Initial print runs: about 1.07 billion for 2025 → 748.41 million for 2026 → 555.93 million for 2027
  • The 2026 run was about 69.9% of the year before. The 2027 run fell by roughly another quarter
  • For 2027, the Disney New Year postcards and the photo-printing type (inkjet photo paper) were dropped

Print runs over time

The initial print run is the number prepared before sales begin. More may be printed later depending on demand. The postcards cost 85 yen each for both 2026 and 2027.

Keeping up New Year cards, or stopping

  • If you keep sending them, narrow the list: sending cards only to people you rarely see, or who don't use email or LINE, cuts both the number of cards and the effort
  • If you stop, say so: some people add a line to their last New Year card saying they will no longer send greetings from next year
  • A handwritten line: with fewer cards, you can add a short handwritten sentence to each one
