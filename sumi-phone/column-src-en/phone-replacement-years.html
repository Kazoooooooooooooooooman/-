Households that replaced a mobile phone (including smartphones) had used the old one for 4.6 years on average. That is nearly double the 2.4 years of 2005. Even so, compared with TVs (10.8 years) and computers (7.7 years), it is the shortest period of any household durable good in the survey. This summary is based on the Cabinet Office's Consumer Confidence Survey (March 2026).

The short version

  • Mobile phones are used for 4.6 years on average (households of two or more people that replaced one between April 2025 and March 2026)
  • Reasons for replacing: breakdown 32.4%, upgrading to a better model 25.6%, other 41.8%
  • 95.8% of households have a mobile phone, with 247.6 phones per 100 households

Average years of use over time

Since around 2017, the figure has stayed in the 4-year range. The longest was 4.9 years in 2020. Each year refers to the survey conducted in March of that year.

Twenty years ago, half of households replaced their phone because they wanted a better model. Today that share has fallen to a quarter, and "other" is above 40%. The survey does not say what "other" includes, but possible reasons are a battery that no longer lasts, or a switch to a different carrier or price plan.

What you can do to keep a phone longer

  • Replace the battery: many people replace their phone because the battery has worn out, but replacing only the battery costs less than a new phone
  • Keep your use light: the more apps you have, the slower the phone runs and the faster the battery drains. Deleting apps you do not use can make even an older phone comfortable to use
  • Check how long OS updates last before you buy: choosing a model that gets updates for a long time lets you use it longer with peace of mind

Notes on the survey

  • The figures are averages calculated only from households that replaced a phone during the year. Households that did not replace one are not included
  • The survey covers households of two or more people
  • The survey method changed in April 2013 and October 2018, so treat comparisons across those dates as rough guides

Summary

  • Mobile phones are used for 4.6 years on average, nearly double what it was 20 years ago
  • Replacing to get "a better model" fell from half of households to a quarter
  • Even so, phones are replaced sooner than any other household good in the survey
