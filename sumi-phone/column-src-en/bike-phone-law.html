Since April 1, 2026, looking at your phone while riding a bicycle in Japan can get you a "blue ticket" (an on-the-spot fine notice). If you are 16 or older, you can be asked to pay a fine on the spot. The amount is 12,000 yen, one of the heaviest among bicycle offenses. Based on National Police Agency materials, here is what is not allowed and what is.

The short version

  • From April 1, 2026, cyclists aged 16 and over can be given a blue ticket (a fine)
  • Holding your phone to talk, or staring at the screen, while riding: a fine of 12,000 yen
  • It does not apply when you are stopped. Even with the phone in a holder, you may not keep looking at the screen while riding
  • The surest approach: turn off notifications before you ride and put the phone in your bag

What changed in April 2026

According to the National Police Agency, a law took effect on April 1, 2026 that brings cyclists aged 16 and over under the "traffic violation notification system", commonly known as the blue ticket.

The blue ticket is a system for relatively minor offenses: if you pay the fine, the case does not go to court. It has long applied to cars and motorcycles, and now it covers bicycles too. Riders under 16 are still not subject to blue tickets, as before.

Phone use is one of the heavier bicycle offenses

Here are some common offenses from the list of fines published by the National Police Agency.

Running a red light costs 6,000 yen. Using a phone while riding costs twice that, 12,000 yen. In serious cases a blue ticket is not the end of it: according to National Police Agency materials, the penalty is "imprisonment of up to one year or a fine of up to 300,000 yen, etc."

What is and isn't allowed

The rule comes from Article 71 of the Road Traffic Act. Reading the text shows where the line is drawn.

SituationWhat happens
Riding while holding the phone to talkNot allowed (subject to a fine)
Staring at or operating the screen while ridingNot allowed. Even with the phone in a holder, keeping your eyes on the screen counts
Looking at or operating the screen while stoppedNot covered (the law says "except when stopped")
Unavoidable calls, such as helping an injured personNot covered

Three ways to prepare if you tend to check your phone

  1. Turn off notifications before you ride: you look because it buzzes. Make a habit of switching on Do Not Disturb or a Focus mode just for cycling (how to clean up your notifications)
  2. Check the route in advance: riding while following a map is when you look at the screen the longest. Look at the route before you set off, and if you get lost, stop and check
  3. Put the phone in your bag: if it is within reach, you will look at it. Deep in a bag takes one more step to get out than a pocket does
