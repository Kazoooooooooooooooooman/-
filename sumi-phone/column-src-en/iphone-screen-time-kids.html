If you want to keep your child from overusing their iPhone, use Screen Time, which comes built into every iPhone. In iOS 27, the features for parents (parental controls) have been redesigned. Based on Apple's official support information, here is how to prepare, how to set it up, and what it can do.

The short version

  • Preparation: set up Family Sharing, create an Apple Account for your child, and update the family's devices to the latest OS
  • Setup: on the parent's iPhone, go to Settings → Family → your child's name → Screen Time
  • What you can control: how long they can use apps (allowances), schedules by time of day, who they can contact, and which apps and websites they can see

What to prepare before you start

  1. Set up Family Sharing on the parent's device
  2. If your child does not have an account yet, create an Apple Account for them (their own account, not one shared with anyone else in the family)
  3. Invite other family members to the Family Sharing group
  4. Update every device in the group to the latest software
  5. Choose "Upgrade Parental Controls" so you can use the new Screen Time

If the group includes an older device that cannot be updated, you can keep using the previous version of Screen Time.

How to set it up

  1. Open the Settings app on the parent's iPhone
  2. Tap Family, then tap your child's name
  3. Tap Screen Time and follow the on-screen instructions

Apple recommends managing your child's Screen Time from the parent's device rather than from the child's iPhone. When you manage it through Family Sharing, you also set the Screen Time passcode on the parent's device.

What you can manage

FeatureWhat it does
AllowancesSet how much time can be spent on each app category, such as entertainment, games and social media. You can also set a cap on the daily total
Screen Time schedulesChoose which apps can be used at different times of day, such as before school, during school and after school. You can also set weekdays and weekends separately
Allowed contactsThe parent chooses who the child can talk to through Messages, FaceTime and phone calls. Messages from new contacts stay hidden from the child until the parent approves them
Apps and websitesAllow or block specific apps and websites. Require the parent's approval for purchases, downloads and opening new websites
Content and privacy restrictionsSet age ratings for music, movies, books and more. Decide whether the child can change settings such as location sharing
Communication SafetyHelps protect children from sending or receiving photos and videos in which nudity is detected. It also works during FaceTime calls

From within the Screen Time settings, you can also pause the device on the spot, remove time limits for just that day, or change that day's schedule.

What is turned on by default, by age

  • Under 13: requiring the parent's approval to open new websites (browsing requests)
  • Under 18: "Limit Adult Websites"

The age thresholds and available settings may vary by country or region.

Tips for making it stick

  • Don't set detailed rules from the start. Begin by deciding just the daily total and the time before bed
  • Talk with your child about why you chose these limits, and review them together as they move up a grade
  • Showing your child your own Screen Time makes the conversation easier

If your child turns the limits off themselves, see for people who keep turning off their own Screen Time. For Android, see how to use Digital Wellbeing.

Summary

  • Set up Family Sharing first, then set up your child's Screen Time on the parent's iPhone
  • Settings → Family → your child's name → Screen Time
  • You can manage time, time-of-day schedules, contacts, and apps and websites from one screen
