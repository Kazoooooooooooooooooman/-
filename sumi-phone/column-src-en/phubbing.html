"Phubbing" combines "phone" and "snubbing". It means ignoring the person you are with to focus on your phone. In a 2018 experiment, the more a conversation partner phubbed, the lower the perceived conversation quality and relationship satisfaction. This summary draws on papers from the University of Kent in the UK, Baylor University in the US, and others.

The short version

  • In an experiment, the more the partner looked at their phone, the lower the conversation quality and relationship satisfaction
  • One reason is a weaker sense of belonging, the feeling of being left out
  • But most studies are surveys, and many cannot tell which way cause and effect run

What is phubbing?

Varoth Chotpitayasunondh and Karen Douglas of the University of Kent describe phubbing as snubbing someone in a social setting by concentrating on your phone instead of talking to the person directly. Think of checking your phone during a meal, or glancing at a notification in the middle of a conversation.

Some research suggests a phone can pull at your attention just by sitting on the table, even when you are not looking at it. We cover this in does a phone on your desk dull your mind?

The experiment: what happens to a conversation when the other person looks at their phone?

In an experiment published in 2018, participants watched a three-minute animation and imagined themselves in a two-person conversation. Their conversation partner did one of three things:

  • Did not look at their phone
  • Looked at their phone some of the time
  • Looked at their phone a lot

The more the partner phubbed, the lower participants rated the quality of the conversation and their satisfaction with the relationship, and the difference was significant. The researchers point to two reasons: a weaker sense of belonging, and changes in mood (less positive feeling and more negative feeling).

A separate experiment in 2025 looked at conversations from the outside, as an observer. Conversations in which one person was phubbing were seen as less appropriate. The topic, casual or serious, made no difference. When watching phubbing, female observers rated the conversation quality lower than male observers did.

Effects on couples

James Roberts and Meredith David of Baylor University created a nine-item scale to measure phubbing between romantic partners and surveyed adults in the US (Computers in Human Behavior, 2016). Items include, for example:

  • My partner places his or her cellphone where they can see it when we are together
  • My partner glances at his/her cellphone when talking to me
  • If there is a lull in our conversation, my partner will check his or her cellphone

According to the university's announcement, people who felt phubbed by their partner had more conflict over phones, and that conflict was linked to lower relationship satisfaction. Lower relationship satisfaction was in turn linked to lower life satisfaction and to feeling depressed.

A 2022 study of 504 adults in China, on the other hand, points the other way. People less satisfied with their relationship felt lonelier, and loneliness was linked to more phubbing. Phubbing may harm relationships, or people in unhappy relationships may turn to their phones. Both directions seem possible.

Parents' phubbing and children

Parents absorbed in their phones in front of their children is also a form of phubbing. A 2023 analysis combining 42 studies conducted in China (covering 56,275 children) found that in families where parents phubbed more, children tended to have more anxiety, low mood and behavior problems, and lower self-image and lower social and emotional skills. When both parents phubbed, the link with behavior problems was stronger than when only one did.

FAQ

What does "phubbing" mean?

It is an English word made from "phone" and "snubbing". It means focusing on your phone and ignoring the person you are with instead of talking to them.

Is phubbing bad for relationships?

In a 2018 experiment, the more the other person looked at their phone, the lower the conversation quality and relationship satisfaction. Surveys of couples and of parents and children have also linked phubbing to poorer relationships. But most of these are surveys, so we do not know for sure which is the cause.

Summary

  • Phubbing means putting your phone ahead of the person you are with
  • In an experiment, more phubbing meant lower conversation quality and relationship satisfaction
  • The more people are phubbed, the more normal it feels, so it helps to notice it and stop yourself
