The most widely used way to read the news was "portal sites" such as Yahoo! News and Google News (69.1%). Next came news delivered through social media, such as LINE NEWS (43.8%), and print newspapers (37.6%). Depending on age, people come to the news through completely different doors. This summary is based on a survey by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications.

The short version

  • How people read the news: portal sites 69.1%, via social media 43.8%, print newspapers 37.6%, curation services 18.2%
  • Among teens, social media (53.6%) edges out portal sites (52.9%)
  • 8.4% of people do not read the news in any of these ways. Among teens it is 25.0%

How people read the news (all ages)

Curation services are news apps such as SmartNews and Gunosy. Some people use more than one service, so the total is over 100%.

Reading the news on social media: things to watch

  • Check who wrote it: look for the name of the newspaper or news agency that supplied the story
  • Do not judge by the headline alone: reading the article itself can change your impression
  • Recommendations are skewed: the topics you read most are the ones you are shown most. We summarize how much people trust each type of media in trust in the media

Summary

  • Portal sites are the most common way into the news, at 69.1%
  • From teens to people in their 30s, more than half get news via social media
  • Print newspapers are read by 72.7% of people in their 70s and 5.7% of teens
