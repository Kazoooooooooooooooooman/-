In England, schools are now meant to be phone-free places by default. In February 2024 the Department for Education issued guidance asking schools to stop phone use throughout the whole day, not just in lessons but also at breaktime and lunchtime. The guidance was revised in January 2026, and since June 29 it has been guidance with a basis in law. Schools are expected to follow it from September 2026. This summary is based on UK government announcements and the text of the law.

The February 2024 guidance

In its announcement on February 19, 2024, the Department for Education said it would back head teachers in banning phone use throughout the school day, including breaktimes. It gave examples of how this could be done:

  • Not allowing phones on school premises
  • Having pupils hand in their phones on arrival
  • Keeping phones securely locked away all day

The announcement also cited Ofcom (the UK communications regulator) figures showing that 97% of children have their own phone by age 12, and Department for Education data from 2022–23 in which 29% of secondary school pupils said phones were being used when they were not supposed to be in most or all lessons.

What the current guidance says

The revised guidance says that "all schools should be mobile phone-free environments by default." The main points are these:

  • Phones are prohibited throughout the school day, including lessons, the time between lessons, breaktime and lunchtime
  • Smartwatches and other devices that can send messages, show notifications or record audio or video are treated the same way
  • Head teachers decide how to do it, for example with lockers or by having pupils hand phones to staff in the morning
  • Exceptions are allowed for pupils managing a medical condition (such as diabetes monitoring), disabled pupils, pupils with caring responsibilities and others
  • Where the policy is clearly broken, phones may be confiscated

The guidance covers maintained schools, academies (state-funded independent schools), independent schools and others. Standalone schools and colleges for 16- to 19-year-olds are not covered.

What changed with the law

Section 36 of the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026, which became law on April 29, 2026, added a new provision to the Education Act 1996. Head teachers and others responsible for a school "must have regard to" the government's guidance on mobile phones when running the school. The provision came into force on June 29, 2026.

A UK government document from July 2026 says that "Ofsted will check school mobile phone policy on every inspection." According to the same document, 75% of those who responded to the public consultation supported making the guidance statutory.

Is it working?

Citing figures from TeacherTapp, a survey app for teachers, the UK government says the share of teachers reporting unauthorized use in lessons fell from 38% in 2024 to 24% in May 2026, calling this "early signs of improvement." On the other hand, in the national behaviour survey report of June 2025, 58% of pupils said that in at least some lessons, pupils used mobile devices when they were not supposed to.

Things to keep in mind

  • The guidance covers only schools in England, not the rest of the UK. It does not apply to Scotland, Wales and the other nations
  • The legal duty is to "have regard to" the guidance; schools decide how to make themselves phone-free
  • The TeacherTapp figures are the share of teachers who answered in the app, not official statistics

FAQ

Are phones banned in UK schools?

In England, Department for Education guidance makes it the default that phones are not used at any point in the school day. Since June 29, 2026 it has been statutory guidance, and schools are expected to follow it from September. Scotland and the other nations outside England, however, are not covered by this guidance.

What are other countries doing?

France banned phone use in primary and middle schools by law in 2018 and is extending the "digital pause," in which middle school students hand in their phones on arrival. See France's digital pause and school phone bans now cover 58% of the world's countries.
