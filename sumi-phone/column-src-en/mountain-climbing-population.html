From easy hikes up a nearby hill to serious climbs, the mountains are a place to spend time away from screens. But are more people heading for the hills? In Japan's Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC), the share of people who went mountain climbing or hiking in the past year fell from 10.0% in 2016 to 7.7% in 2021. That is a drop of 2.3 percentage points in five years. Here is what the survey shows.

The short version

  • The number of people aged 15+ who went hiking or climbing rose from 9,727,000 in 2011 to 10,732,000 in 2016
  • The participation rate in 2021 was 7.7%, down from 10.0% in 2016 (age 10+)
  • The 2021 survey covers a pandemic year (October 2020 to October 2021), which may have affected the result

The "participation rate" is the share of people who went mountain climbing or hiking at least once in the past year. The 2011 and 2016 figures cover people aged 15 and over, while the 2016–2021 comparison covers people aged 10 and over, so the age ranges differ. Comparing like with like, participation rose from 2011 to 2016 and fell from 2016 to 2021.

Compared with other sports (2021)

In 2021, hiking and mountain climbing ranked sixth among sports. Walking and light exercise rose 3.0 points from 2016, but hiking and climbing fell 2.3 points, swimming 5.3 points and bowling 7.6 points. For the rankings of other sports, see the sports Japanese people play, ranked.

Who walks in the mountains? (2016)

  • By age, the rate was highest among people aged 65–69, at 12.5%. But every age group from 20 to 74 was at 10.0% or more, so people of many ages walk in the mountains
  • Compared with 2011, the rate rose most among people in their 20s and those aged 70–74
  • By prefecture, Tokyo was highest at 15.0%, followed by Kanagawa at 14.1% and Nara at 12.9%. Rates tend to be high in the Kanto and Kinki regions
  • People aged 75 and over spent the most days a year in the mountains (average days of activity)

In the 2011 survey, the rate was highest among men aged 65–69 (13.2%) and women aged 60–64 (12.0%).

Notes on the numbers

  • The Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities is held every five years. The 2021 survey covered about 190,000 people aged 10 and over in about 91,000 households
  • The 2021 figures cover activities between October 20, 2020 and October 19, 2021, and may have been affected by pandemic-era requests to stay home
  • Anyone who went even once in a year counts as a participant, so the figures include easy hikes on local hills as well as serious mountain climbing

FAQ

How many people go mountain climbing in Japan?

In MIC's Survey on Time Use and Leisure Activities, 10,732,000 people aged 15 and over went mountain climbing or hiking in the past year in 2016 (a participation rate of 10.0%). In 2021, the participation rate was 7.7% among people aged 10 and over.

Is mountain climbing growing or shrinking?

It grew from 2011 to 2016, but fell from 10.0% to 7.7% between 2016 and 2021. The 2021 figure covers a pandemic year.
