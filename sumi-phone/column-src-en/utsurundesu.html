With a smartphone, you can take hundreds of photos, look at them right away, and delete the ones you do not like. The Utsurundesu is the opposite. It takes only 27 shots, and you cannot see them until they are developed. It should be inconvenient, yet some people take one with them when they go out. Here is how to use it, and the kind of fun a phone camera does not give you.

The short version

  • The Utsurundesu, launched in July 1986, was the world's first single-use camera (a roll of film with a lens built in). It turns 40 in 2026
  • It weighs 90 g (less than half a smartphone), takes 27 shots, and has a built-in battery, so it needs no charging
  • Using it is just "wind and press". When you have finished the roll, you have it developed at a shop
  • Because shots are limited and you cannot see them right away, you can focus on the moment you take the picture, and on being where you are

What is the Utsurundesu?

According to Fujifilm, the Utsurundesu (the name means roughly "it takes pictures!") went on sale in July 1986 as the world's first single-use camera. It was designed so that "anyone could take photos easily, anytime, anywhere". Outside Japan it is sold as the QuickSnap, and more than 1.7 billion have been shipped worldwide.

How to use it: just wind and press

  1. Wind the film: turn the winding dial until it stops
  2. Turn on the flash in dark places: push up the switch on the front of the camera. The light reaches up to 3 m
  3. Look through and press: look through the viewfinder and press the shutter button
  4. Take it to be developed when you are done: when the counter reaches "0" and the dial no longer stops, the roll is finished. Take it to a photo shop or similar and they will develop it for you. You can search for shops that develop film on Fujifilm's website

New models for the 40th anniversary in 2026

Two new models were announced to mark the 40th anniversary.

  • Utsurundesu Active: a waterproof model with the camera encased in plastic, able to shoot down to 10 m underwater. Good for the beach, the pool and camping (released August 7, 2026)
  • Utsurundesu Black and White: a model with black-and-white film (announced for release from September 2026 onward)

Three pleasures a phone camera does not give you

  1. Choosing each shot: with only 27 shots, you think about what to take. Because you think about it, you look more closely at the scene in front of you
  2. Looking up as soon as you take it: you cannot look back at the photo on the spot, so after you take it, you go back to the people and the view in front of you, not to a screen
  3. Looking forward to seeing them later: during the few days you wait for the prints, the memories ripen a little. On the day the photos arrive, you get to remember that day all over again

Taking lots of photos on your phone is fun too, of course. But if you have ever felt after a trip or a day out that you were "so busy taking pictures that you hardly remember it", it is worth spending just one day with an Utsurundesu.
