The TV is on, and there is a phone in your hand. How many people watch TV this way, with a second screen going at the same time (called "nagara" viewing in Japanese, meaning "while doing something else")? Of the people watching TV between 8 and 9 p.m. on weekdays, 27.4% were also using the internet. Among teens between 9 and 10 p.m. it was 48.7%, so about half of teens watching TV were also looking at a phone or another device. This summary is based on a survey by the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications (MIC).

The short version

  • In the evening (7–11 p.m.), roughly 20–30% of people watching TV are also using the internet
  • The highest shares are teens at 9 p.m. on weekdays and people in their 20s at 10 p.m. on days off
  • Among people in their 70s, the share is in the 10% range

By age group: weekdays, 9–10 p.m.

After teens, the next highest group is people in their 40s (45.8%). That is more than three times the lowest group, people in their 70s (13.2%).

On days off, two out of three people in their 20s who were watching TV between 10 and 11 p.m. were also online.

Compared with five years ago

The share of people "using TV and the internet at the same time" between 8 and 9 p.m. on weekdays fell from 11.4% in fiscal 2020 to 9.9% in fiscal 2025. This is because fewer people are watching TV at all. Among those who do watch TV, the share also using the internet has stayed in the 20–30% range.

A note on the numbers

  • The survey counts people who were watching live TV and using the internet at the same time as "parallel use," for each hour of the day
  • "The internet" includes all devices, such as phones, computers and tablets
  • "Teens" means ages 13 to 19. In time slots where an age group has few respondents, the figures can swing more

FAQ

What is "nagara" viewing?

It means using the internet on a phone or other device while watching TV, often called second-screening in English. The MIC survey calls it "parallel use."

Which age group does it most?

On weekdays, the highest share is teens between 9 and 10 p.m. (48.7%). On days off, it is people in their 20s between 10 and 11 p.m. (66.6%).

How can I stop using my phone while watching TV?

The simplest way is to charge your phone in another room while you watch. Another option is for the family to agree that TV time is "phones-down time."
