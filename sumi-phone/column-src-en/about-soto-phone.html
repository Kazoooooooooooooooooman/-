Soto Phone is a phone that comes with no social media, no games and no video apps. "Soto" is the Japanese word for "outside." We leave out anything that gives you a reason to stay glued to the screen, and include only what you need to go out: calls, maps, transit, a camera and so on. In this article, I, the person making it, explain what it can and cannot do, how much it will cost, and when you can get one.

The official website is sotophone.jp. We have no connection with the similarly named "Sotophone - Tech News," an app that publishes smartphone specs and news.

In a nutshell

  • A phone with no social media, games, video, app store or web browser

What is included, and what is not

IncludedNot included
Calls, SMS, maps, transit, weather, camera, music, emergency alerts (such as earthquake warnings), clock, notes, stampsSocial media, games, video apps, app store, web browser

There is no app store, so you cannot add apps later. The phone is built so that "I deleted it, then installed it again" simply cannot happen. The biggest feature of Soto Phone is that these things are not there from the start, rather than being limits like Screen Time that you can switch off yourself.

For payments, the plan is that you use your own card. We are considering adding a transit IC card (Japan's tap-to-pay train cards).

The screen is normally black and white

The screen normally displays in black and white. Color comes back only when you open a photo. Bright, colorful screens grab your eye and make you open them more often. In black and white, once you have done what you needed to do, your eyes naturally drift away from the screen.

Focus mode

Set a time, such as "90 minutes, English vocabulary," and press start: notifications stop until it ends. You can let calls from family still come through. Your focus time is recorded, so you can see how much you got done in a week.

We plan to let parents set rewards based on that time. All parents can see is how many hours you focused, not what you were doing. It is a system for cheering you on, not for watching you.

A phone bill that gets cheaper the more time you spend out (in preparation)

What we have in mind: when you visit a partner shop (a café where you can study, a bookstore, a sento public bath and so on), Soto Phone collects one stamp, and next month's phone bill goes down by that amount. We are preparing this now, including the partner shops, and nothing is decided yet.

For example, on a 1,980 yen monthly plan, visiting partner shops five times in a month would bring it to 1,480 yen (these amounts are an illustration only; the plans and discounts are still in preparation).

We are thinking of having the partner shops cover the discounts. For a shop, this would replace advertising spent on attracting new customers, and we want it to work so that they pay only when a customer actually comes in.

Price and the path to launch

  • Handset price: planned in the 50,000s to 60,000s yen range. People who register early will be the first to hear the official price, together with the phone plans
  • Path to launch: we will build prototypes, then have a small group of people use one for about a week (early trial; you can extend it if you like), then take preorders through crowdfunding. We will decide the launch date after the early trial
  • Carrier and SIM: you will be able to put in the SIM from your current carrier and use it as is. We are also preparing Soto's own plans, which get cheaper the more time you spend out

Can you use LINE?

We will decide how to handle LINE (Japan's most-used messaging app) and announce it before launch. In Japan, LINE is essential for class, club and family communication, but it is also a big reason notifications keep pulling you back to the screen. During development, we are testing ways to include it with notifications kept to a minimum.

Who is it for?

  • Students who keep reaching for their phone while studying or working: it removes any reason to touch it, without relying on willpower
  • Adults who want to leave their smartphone at home when they go out: with calls, maps, transit and a camera, you will rarely be stuck when you are out. You can carry Soto day to day and leave your smartphone at home
  • Parents giving a child their first phone: you can start with no social media and no games

What it cannot do

I also want to be honest about what you should know before you choose it.

  • It cannot do social media, games, video apps or web browsing. When you need those for work or school, please use a computer or the smartphone at home
  • You cannot add apps
  • We do not plan to include mobile payments (QR code payments)
  • It is still in development, and you can only get one after launch. Specifications may change

Who is making it
