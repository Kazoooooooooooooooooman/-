You check your phone as soon as you wake up, and suddenly it's noon. You keep looking at the screen through the afternoon without really meaning to, and by evening you think, "I didn't do anything today." Your day off disappearing into your phone is not a sign of weak willpower. The more time there is with nothing you have to do, the more easily the phone, which is always closest to hand, slips in. This article shows how to get your day off back by deciding just three things the night before.

The short version

  • Your day off disappears into your phone because there's nothing planned for "the morning" and the "empty time"
  • Decide three things the night before: where to go in the morning, a plan with someone, and when to put the phone away
  • What works best is putting one plan in the morning that gets you out of the house
  • Rather than "quitting" your phone, "decide where it goes and when"

Why days off disappear into your phone

On weekdays, work or school divides up your time for you. On days off, there are no such breaks. Time without breaks gets filled by the phone, which is always within reach. And once you start looking in the morning, it carries on to the next video, the next post.

So the fix is not "resisting your phone" but creating breaks in your day off yourself. Surveys also show that people spend more time online on days off than on weekdays (average smartphone time in Japan).

Decide just three things the night before

  1. Pick one place to go in the morning: a bakery, a park, the library, a public bath (sento), anywhere at all. It gives you a reason to "leave the house by 10". For how to use your mornings, see also how not to look at your phone first thing in the morning
  2. Make one plan with someone: lunch, exercise, even a phone call. A promise to someone is easier to keep than a rule you set for yourself alone
  3. Decide when the phone goes away: for example, "from 1 p.m. to 5 p.m., the phone stays by the front door". The trick is to decide both the time and the place

To start with, just "leaving the house in the morning" is enough. If you're not sure what to do, pick something from 20 things to enjoy outside on your own.

For days when it doesn't go to plan

  • If you spent the morning on your phone: going out in the afternoon is plenty. It's never too late to get the day back
  • Rainy days: the library, a museum, a cinema, a public bath. Have one indoor destination ready
  • Days when you're tired and don't want to do anything: putting the phone in another room and taking a nap is a perfectly good way to rest. Resting and staring at your phone are not the same thing

Summary

  • Days off disappear into your phone because it slips into time without breaks
  • The night before, decide "where to go in the morning", "a plan with someone" and "when to put the phone away"
  • Something to do today: pick just one place to go on the morning of your next day off
